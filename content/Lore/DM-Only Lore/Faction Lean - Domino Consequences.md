# Faction Lean — Domino Consequences

A reactive-world toolkit. When the party leans toward **Stasis**, **Convergence**, or **Renewal** at a major faultline moment, the world responds — quietly at first, then cumulatively.

These are designed to be **small, real, and cumulative** — not "the world changes overnight," but "the world starts remembering your choice."

Each path has:

- **Immediate** (same session)
- **Near-term** (next 1–2 sessions)
- **Mid-term** (3–6 sessions)

See [[Campaign Framework - The Faultlines of Mercy]] and the [[Lore/DM-Only Lore/Antagonists/index|Antagonist Triad]] (Stasis / Renewal / Convergence) for the philosophical grounding.

---

## If the party leans **Stasis**

### Immediate

- **Stability device gets installed** (even "temporary"). The yard feels subtly *flattened* — magic is easier to predict, but less expressive. Students notice.
- The party is treated as "responsible witnesses" and **their presence gets logged** (even if names don't).
- Greenward staff becomes polite-but-wary: *"You brought control to our doorstep."*

### Near-term

- A Crownspire courier arrives with an **updated compliance checklist** for [[Greenward Annex]] ("new standard practices").
- Felix / Zaz / Arster notice small restrictions: workshops/tools/archives now require **sign-out + supervision**.
- Renewal sympathizers (students, fringe contacts) avoid or cold-shoulder the party: *"You chose the cage."*

### Mid-term

- Stasis uses Greenward as a precedent: **"See? Even the Annex agreed."** That expands checkpointing and "stability audits" along the border.
- A Stasis-friendly NPC offers the party a deal: "cooperate and we protect you." (tempting, compromising)
- The Node / thin-place activity **shifts elsewhere** (like pressure seeking a new seam). The world adapts around containment.

---

## If the party leans **Convergence**

### Immediate

- A neutral process is established: **no stabilizer installed today**, but the yard is temporarily restricted under [[The Treaty Wardens|Treaty Warden]] supervision.
- The party's identities are **not logged**, but they become "known to the Wardens" (protective and watchful).
- Stasis backs off with a tight smile: *"Noted."*

### Near-term

- Treaty Wardens offer the party a **quiet protection framework**: safe lodging, escorted travel, discreet messages — but also expectations ("check in," "don't inflame things").
- Greenward becomes a **neutral hub** for information: you can get answers here faster than elsewhere, because Convergence wants stability through understanding.
- Renewal voices grow frustrated: *"You chose process over change."*

### Mid-term

- Convergence influence spreads: nearby towns adopt "neutral arbitration" norms, tempers cool… and people start saying things like "it's easier when the Wardens decide."
- A "[[The Still Point|Still Point]]" rumor starts earlier than it otherwise would: places feeling **too calm** become more common (subtle Convergence foreshadow).
- Stasis tries to bypass neutrality with quiet maneuvering (paperwork around paperwork).

---

## If the party leans **Renewal**

### Immediate

- Stasis cannot install the device. The yard stays "alive," slightly prickly — students feel vindicated and brave.
- The party gains **informal student protection**: doors held, whispers offered, someone provides a hiding route.
- Treaty Wardens look concerned: not angry, but wary of escalation.

### Near-term

- The Annex becomes quietly famous as a "defiant place." More curious outsiders show up. More risk.
- Stasis response becomes *indirect*: audits, funding pressure, surveillance, "missing permits," sudden inspections at inconvenient times.
- Renewal contacts begin reaching toward the party (softly): "We heard what you did."

### Mid-term

- Micro-flares and anomalies become **more frequent near the border** (not because Renewal caused them, but because nothing is damping them).
- A major "miracle with a cost" incident happens in a nearby community — the party gets pulled in whether they want to or not.
- Convergence tries to reassert balance later — potentially with heavier hands than before.

---

## How to Use

- Pick the lean **based on what the party actually did**, not what they said.
- Apply the **Immediate** column the same session.
- Drip the **Near-term** column across the next 1–2 sessions as ambient flavor and NPC reactions.
- Use the **Mid-term** column to seed your *next arc's* faultline — the world's response *becomes* the next problem.

This is the world *remembering* the players. It's the Slow Unravel's social cousin.
