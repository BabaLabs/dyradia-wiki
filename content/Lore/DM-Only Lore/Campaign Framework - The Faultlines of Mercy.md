# Campaign Framework — The Faultlines of Mercy

*A story about people the world failed — and what happens when the world starts failing back.*

---

## Elevator Pitch

Three hundred years after the Fracture, something is stirring again. Not loudly. Not grandly. Quietly — through the forgotten, the discarded, the ones nobody was watching.

Portals misfire. Leylines hum off-tempo. Magic answers to unfamiliar names.

And the first people to feel it are **those who were never meant to matter**.

This campaign isn't about saving the world. It's about deciding whether the world is *worth* saving.

---

## The Present-Day Setting — The Waning Era

Magic still exists… but it feels wrong. See [[State of Magic in Dyradia]] and [[The Slow Unravel]].

The world hasn't lost magic. It has lost **consistency**. And people are scared — not panicking, not rioting, but something worse:

> *"We can live like this, right? We can just… adapt?"*

---

## The Central Problem — The Chains Are Reconnecting

The connections to the [[The Dyra|Dyra]] plane are reforming on their own — instinctively, violently, like nerves firing without a brain to tell them how to behave.

> Magic is healing itself the way the body heals a wound: clumsily, aggressively, blindly.

The danger isn't "magic returning." It's magic returning **wrong**.

Three hundred years ago, [[Maeleus]] and [[Ometrieysa]] prevented the world from tearing itself open. Now something is undoing their sacrifice. Not maliciously — instinctively. Their deaths weren't an ending. They were a **delay**. And delays always expire.

---

## What the World Believes

- **Common people:** *"It's just bad luck. Strange times. Old superstitions waking."*
- **Scholars:** *"It feels like the Awakening trying to happen again."*
- **Old soldiers:** *"I have heard this rhythm before. It sounds like war."*
- **The very few who know:** *"This must not happen a second time… not without a guide."*

---

## The Hook — Session One: The Reverberation Event

A quiet town. A normal day. The party intersects by chance — caravan, festival, job posting, escort, whatever feels natural.

Then it happens:

> A sound like a heartbeat in the sky. Like someone knocking on the inside of the world.

- Ravens fall silent mid-flight
- A portal opens like a cut through canvas
- Everyone feels something warm crawl down their spine — *except the PCs*

**Because whatever is coming recognized them.**

It leaves something behind:

- A sigil burned into the ground
- A dying messenger
- A map where the ink won't stay still
- A body with two shadows

Suddenly, multiple nations want to know *why this group didn't react like everyone else.*

## Why This Fits the Party

### Centaur Gunslinger (Felix)

Born on a border that never wanted to admit he existed. Branded, commodified, numbered, not named. Escaping slavery just as new chains (arcane, planar, cosmic) begin to form.

His story intersects with the world's question: *"If freedom returns, does it return for everyone or only the chosen?"*

### Human Mercy Monk (Zaz)

A soul broker whose body was never his own. Part mortal, part raven, part psychopomp. Fey influence = first warning sign of planar gates shifting.

The campaign asks: *"If the boundaries between worlds open again, do you heal them… or guide them to a gentler ending?"*

### Lizardfolk Rogue (Vesh)

Exile by choice, but survival imposed. A creature whose worth is measured by *proof*. The perfect PC for discovering secrets societies didn't want found.

The world whispers to him first, because it thinks he's beneath notice. That's his edge.

### Dhampir Vengeance Paladin (Potential)

Product of a union the world punished. Bloodline that shouldn't exist. Their oath isn't divine — it's personal.

In a world where old magic stirs, their lineage becomes… *relevant.* Not holy. Not cursed. Important.

### Dwarven Artificer (Potential)

Healer, supporter, builder. Someone who makes things *work* — in a world where magic is beginning not to.

Their struggle becomes cosmological: *"Can you mend what was broken when the Fracture happened — without repeating the mistakes that caused it?"*

## Political Intrigue Seeds — What Each Nation Wants With the Party

### [[Mehrendt]]

> *"If the world is breaking again, we need to control the narrative before someone else does."*

They want the party **contained**, not killed. They learned from what happened with [[Ezericht La'Monte]].

### [[Fey'thra]]

> *"This feels like the Awakening. We remember what that cost."*

They don't trust the party — but they don't want them dead either. They want **witnesses.**

### [[Avalon]]

> *"If the chains are reforming, maybe we break them properly this time."*

Avalon might *support* the PCs — but for goals the party might not like.

### [[Gohlstout]]

> *"We sealed vaults after the Fracture. Some of them were never meant to open."*

They know something has already changed, because they felt it in the stone.

## Core Story Question

Not:

- "Can you stop the apocalypse?"
- "Can you restore the old world?"

But:

> **When the world heals itself, who gets to decide what "healed" means?**

Because if the Dyra chains reconnect without guidance, the world won't be Awakened — it will be *claimed.*

The party is the first ones being asked to answer — not *"can you save us,"* but: **"Do you think we should be saved?"**

## Campaign Arcs

### Act I — We Are Not Chosen

Surviving the consequences of the first anomaly.

### Act II — Inheritance Without Consent

Each PC discovers how the Fracture shaped their lives generations later.

### Act III — The Echo of Maeleus

The truth of the sacrifice resurfaces — and someone tries to undo it.

### Act IV — The Chain Reforged

Not stopping the return of power, but *guiding what it becomes.*

---

## Core Themes

### 1. Legacy Without Permission

The players inherit a world shaped by people they never met — and never got to mourn properly.

### 2. Truth as Contamination

History isn't inaccurate because of lies — it's inaccurate because the truth would unravel the world.

### 3. Restoration vs Resurrection

Bringing something back isn't the same as healing it. Something can function and still be wrong.

### 4. Becoming What Was Needed

[[Maeleus]] and [[Ometrieysa]] saved the world by dooming their names. The players might one day face that same choice.

---

## The Throughline

This campaign shouldn't feel like resurrecting history, redoing the past, or avenging ghosts. It should feel like this:

> **The past built the cage.**
> **The present rattles the bars.**
> **The future asks who holds the key.**
