# EXECUTOR ARCHETYPES

## (DM-Only, Internal Use)

Each Executor is defined by:

- **Method**
    
- **Operational preference**
    
- **Failure signature**
    
- **What it means if _this_ type is deployed**
    

Executors are not chosen at random.  
Their presence is diagnostic.

---

## 🜂 ARCHETYPE I — THE STILL HAND

_(Containment via Absence)_

### Core Function

Remove a destabilizing element **without leaving a narrative void**.

The world continues as if nothing happened.

---

### Preferred Methods

- Induced disappearance (no body, no witnesses)
    
- Legal erasure (records voided retroactively)
    
- Administrative death (assets seized, titles dissolved, identity invalidated)
    
- Social isolation that culminates in exile or obscurity
    

Violence is **rare**.  
Finality is **quiet**.

---

### When This Archetype Is Used

- Threshold III situations
    
- Early Threshold IV, where escalation is still avoidable
    
- Individuals whose _existence_, not actions, is the problem
    

This is the Mandate saying:

> “We can still fix this gently.”

---

### Failure Signature

If the Still Hand fails:

- Rumors spread
    
- Questions multiply
    
- The absence becomes _noticed_
    

This failure often escalates the situation rather than resolving it.

---

### DM Meaning

If you deploy this archetype, you are:

- Giving the world a chance to self-correct
    
- Choosing delay over conclusion
    
- Preserving player agency
    

This is the **least invasive** Executor.

---

## 🜃 ARCHETYPE II — THE CLOSER

_(Definitive Resolution)_

### Core Function

End a destabilizing trajectory **permanently**.

Not dramatically.  
Not symbolically.  
Efficiently.

---

### Preferred Methods

- Targeted assassination
    
- Accidental death with corroborating evidence
    
- Fatal “self-defense” scenarios
    
- Environmental lethality (falls, fires, structural failure)
    

Always plausible.  
Always deniable.

---

### When This Archetype Is Used

- Confirmed Threshold IV
    
- Situations where delay increases damage
    
- Individuals whose continued existence guarantees escalation
    

This is the Mandate saying:

> “This ends now.”

---

### Failure Signature

If the Closer fails:

- The target survives publicly
    
- Or dies messily
    
- Or the act is witnessed
    

This almost always results in **institutional panic**.

The Mandate _does not_ double down lightly.

---

### DM Meaning

Deploying this archetype means:

- The situation has become irreversible
    
- You are closing a branch of the narrative tree
    
- The world is choosing stability over justice
    

Use sparingly.

---

## 🜁 ARCHETYPE III — THE UNRAVELER

_(Collapse via Exposure)_

### Core Function

Destroy the **context** that makes a threat viable.

The person may live.  
Their _position_ does not.

---

### Preferred Methods

- Controlled leaks
    
- Public disgrace
    
- Revelation of contradictions
    
- Turning allies against the target
    
- Weaponized truth (partial, timed)
    

The target is not killed.  
They are **rendered inert**.

---

### When This Archetype Is Used

- Politically embedded threats
    
- Figures whose power relies on legitimacy
    
- Situations where death would create martyrs
    

This is the Mandate saying:

> “Let the world do the work.”

---

### Failure Signature

If the Unraveler fails:

- Sympathy builds
    
- The target adapts
    
- The narrative becomes polarized
    

This can accelerate destabilization.

---

### DM Meaning

This archetype:

- Creates excellent secondary fallout
    
- Shifts blame away from unseen hands
    
- Preserves moral ambiguity
    

This is your **political knife**, not a combat one.

---

## 🜄 ARCHETYPE IV — THE SEVERER

_(Structural Neutralization)_

### Core Function

Remove access to **dangerous systems**, not people.

The threat survives.  
Their tools do not.

---

### Preferred Methods

- Artifact destruction
    
- Interstice link severance
    
- Magical dampening zones
    
- Legal confiscation
    
- Economic strangulation
    

No blood required.

---

### When This Archetype Is Used

- Artifact-driven destabilization
    
- Magical research approaching Awoken thresholds
    
- Situations where the _means_ are the danger
    

This is the Mandate saying:

> “You can exist — just not like this.”

---

### Failure Signature

If the Severer fails:

- The system adapts
    
- Redundancy appears
    
- Or the act reveals the system’s importance
    

This often escalates to **Closer authorization**.

---

### DM Meaning

This archetype:

- Preserves characters while reshaping the world
    
- Is excellent for near-misses
    
- Allows recovery arcs
    

This is a **pressure valve**, not an ending.

---

## 🜅 ARCHETYPE V — THE MIRROR

_(Redirective Resolution)_

### Core Function

Cause the destabilizing force to **resolve itself elsewhere**.

The Mandate doesn’t stop the disaster.  
It **moves it**.

---

### Preferred Methods

- Misdirection
    
- False alternatives
    
- Redirected blame
    
- Engineered rivalries
    
- Creating a “bigger” problem that absorbs attention
    

No one realizes the original threat mattered.

---

### When This Archetype Is Used

- Large-scale instability
    
- Multi-faction conflicts
    
- Situations where direct action would expose the Mandate
    

This is the Mandate saying:

> “Not here. Not now.”

---

### Failure Signature

If the Mirror fails:

- Both crises converge
    
- Or the redirection is traced
    

This is catastrophic for secrecy.

---

### DM Meaning

This archetype is:

- Extremely subtle
    
- High-risk
    
- High-payoff
    

Use it when you want **history to look accidental**.

---

## INTERNAL EXECUTOR TRUTH (Lock This In)

Executors:

- Are not interchangeable
    
- Do not compete
    
- Do not escalate on their own
    
- Do not “hunt” heroes
    

They resolve **authorization**, not curiosity.
