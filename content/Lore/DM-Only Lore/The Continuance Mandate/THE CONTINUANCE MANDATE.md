# THE CONTINUANCE MANDATE

## DM-Only Internal Structure & Function

_(Working name; never spoken aloud in-world unless deliberately breached)_

---

## 0 Core Design Law (Do Not Break This)

> **The Mandate never acts because something is dangerous.  
> It acts because something cannot be allowed to resolve naturally.**

If you ever feel tempted to deploy them because:

- A fight is dramatic
    
- Stakes feel high
    
- The party is “too strong”
    
- You want tension
    

Stop.

That is not their function.

They are a **last-resort stabilizer**, not an antagonist tool.

---

## 1 INTERNAL STRUCTURE (REAL, BUT UNSEEN)

This faction is deliberately **non-hierarchical in appearance**, but rigid internally.

No one “commands” them in a traditional sense.  
They execute _process_.

---

### 🜂 The Archivist of Continuance

_(Exists. Singular. May not be mortal.)_

**Function:**  
Maintains the sealed archive where:

- Threshold III & IV cases are recorded
    
- Names are written
    
- Outcomes are tracked across decades
    

**Constraints:**

- Cannot authorize action
    
- Cannot intervene
    
- Cannot erase records once finalized
    

They are **custodians**, not decision-makers.

Narratively:

- If this figure ever acts directly, the world is already failing
    

---

### 🜃 The Arbiters

_(Small number, rotating, anonymous)_

**Function:**  
Determine whether a situation:

- Can resolve naturally
    
- Must be slowed
    
- Must be concluded
    

They never choose _how_ something ends — only _whether intervention is justified_.

**Constraints:**

- Require consensus or weighted majority
    
- Bound by precedent
    
- Forbidden from personal involvement
    

They are closer to judges than generals.

---

### 🜁 The Executors

_(Field procedures, not personalities)_

These are what people would _mistake_ for assassins — but that’s reductive.

**Function:**  
Carry out **authorized conclusions**.

Key truth:

> Executors are not given objectives.  
> They are given **outcomes**.

How they achieve them is their discretion.

**Constraints:**

- Cannot act without authorization
    
- Cannot improvise beyond mandate scope
    
- Must minimize narrative disturbance
    

If an Executor fails:

- The Mandate does not “try again”
    
- The situation is reassessed
    

Failure is data, not shame.

---

### 🜄 The Scribes

_(Information synthesis layer)_

These are not spies.

They are:

- Diviners
    
- Analysts
    
- Archivists
    
- Pattern-readers
    

Their magic returns answers like:

- “Filed”
    
- “Pending”
    
- “Inadmissible”
    
- “Contradiction detected”
    

Never clarity.  
Never prophecy.

**Constraint:**  
They cannot see _people_ — only **processes**.

This prevents personal bias.

---

### 🜅 The Lanterns

_(Distributed, deniable infrastructure)_

Lanterns are:

- Dockmasters
    
- Census clerks
    
- Temple registrars
    
- Quartermasters
    
- Trade auditors
    
- Low-level officials
    

Most **do not know** they are Lanterns.

They just follow procedures that:

- Delay
    
- Redirect
    
- Quietly isolate
    

If a Lantern is exposed:

- The Mandate disowns them immediately
    
- Records show them acting alone
    

This is intentional.

---

## 2 AUTHORIZATION FLOW (THIS IS IMPORTANT)

No one can “send” the Mandate.

Instead:

1. Threshold pressure accumulates
    
2. Scribes flag anomaly density
    
3. Arbiters review _pattern_, not individuals
    
4. If justified, **authorization is written**
    
5. Archivist records outcome
    
6. Executors act
    

This process is **slow by design**.

Speed creates mistakes.  
Mistakes create stories.  
Stories create instability.

---

## 3 INTERNAL PHILOSOPHY (THEY ARE NOT MONOLITHIC)

Internally, the Mandate is split along **methodological fault lines**, not moral ones.

These factions never fight openly — but their preferences matter.

---

### 🔹 The Gradualists

> “Time resolves most dangers.”

Prefer:

- Delay
    
- Discredit
    
- Obfuscation
    
- Procedural exhaustion
    

They hate killing.  
They tolerate disappearance.

---

### 🔹 The Finalists

> “Some outcomes rot while waiting.”

Prefer:

- Decisive conclusions
    
- Clean removals
    
- Structural resets
    

They are not cruel.  
They are _exhausted_.

---

### 🔹 The Preservationists

> “The world has already broken once.”

Prefer:

- Overcorrection
    
- Preventative action
    
- Aggressive containment
    

They are the most dangerous internally.

If they ever dominate, the Mandate becomes authoritarian.

---

## 4 FAILURE STATES (FOR YOU AS DM)

These are **world-level consequences**, not punishments.

### 🟥 Internal Schism

If thresholds are triggered repeatedly:

- Arbiter consensus fractures
    
- Authorizations stall
    
- Executors receive conflicting outcomes
    

This creates _gaps_ players might unknowingly exploit.

---

### 🟧 Overreach

If the Mandate acts too often:

- Institutional paranoia increases
    
- False positives rise
    
- Innocents are “concluded”
    

This accelerates collapse — the Mandate’s worst fear.

---

### 🟩 Obsolescence

If the world stabilizes organically:

- Threshold pressure drops
    
- Authorizations cease
    
- The Mandate quietly withers
    

They do not seek relevance.  
They accept irrelevance.

---

## 5 MOST IMPORTANT DM SAFETY RULE

> **If the Mandate ever becomes a recurring on-screen antagonist, something has gone wrong.**

They are:

- Rare
    
- Conditional
    
- Regretful
    

They should feel like:

> “This wasn’t supposed to be necessary.”
