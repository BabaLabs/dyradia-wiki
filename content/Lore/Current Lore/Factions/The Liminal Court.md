# The Liminal Court

## Overview

The Liminal Court is one of the oldest known fey powers in Dyradia, though "court" is an imperfect mortal term. It is not a kingdom in the ordinary sense, nor a pantheon of gods, nor a political body with borders and armies. The Liminal Court is best understood as an ancient fey order of Regents who govern thresholds: moments, places, names, bargains, passages, and transformations where one state becomes another.

The Court is concerned with **crossings**.

- A door opened.
- A road chosen.
- A name given.
- A vow spoken.
- A guest welcomed.
- A soul departing.
- A stranger becoming known.
- A life ending.
- A journey beginning.

To the Liminal Court, these are not symbolic moments. They are laws of reality.

The world is not only made of places. It is made of passages between places. The Regents of the Liminal Court exist in, around, and through those passages.

Their oldest remembered doctrine is often phrased as:

> *All things cross. All crossings have laws. All laws have witnesses.*

---

## Nature of the Court

The Liminal Court predates most modern political institutions, and possibly predates formal mortal understanding of the Interstice. Its Regents are fey powers, but they are not merely woodland spirits or capricious nobles. They are closer to **living principles of transition**.

They are not gods in the same sense as the Dyra, and they do not command universal worship. Instead, they are invoked in old customs, superstitions, and rites surrounding dangerous transitions.

Common examples include:

- ringing a bell before entering fog,
- leaving a candle in a window for the lost,
- covering mirrors near the dead,
- asking permission before crossing a threshold,
- speaking a traveler's name aloud before a long journey,
- refusing to make promises in doorways,
- never accepting food from a smiling host without offering something in return.

Many people follow these customs without knowing they are tied to the Liminal Court at all.

## Relationship to the Interstice

The Liminal Court and [[The Interstice]] are conceptually related, but not identical.

The Interstice is an engineered, stabilized, repeatable liminal lattice created or formalized during the Bohrovan Period. It allowed structured travel through anchored thresholds and Nodes.

The Liminal Court is older, stranger, and less mechanical.

A useful distinction:

> *The Interstice is infrastructure.*
> *The Liminal Court is law.*

Bohrovan scholars may have mapped, measured, and partially harnessed liminal phenomena, but they did not create the underlying concept of crossing. The Court's Regents were associated with thresholds long before mortal scholars learned to make those thresholds repeatable.

This distinction matters greatly. A Node may be built, suppressed, repaired, or severed. But a crossing can still have witnesses, consequences, and old laws even when the infrastructure around it is broken.

This is why the Court is still relevant after the Fracture.

> *The Interstice may be shattered.*
> *The crossings remain.*

## Historical Presence in Dyradia

The Liminal Court appears in folklore across many cultures, though almost always under different names.

In ancient villages, they were invoked during funerals, marriages, births, migrations, border crossings, and oath-swearing. In [[Fey'thra|Fey'thran]] traditions, they were treated with reverence and caution. In older [[Avalon|Avalonian]] border stories, they appear as road-spirits, oath-keepers, and door-watchers. [[Gohlstout]] records reference liminal laws more indirectly, often through tunnel rites, sealed passages, and "permission marks" carved before entering unstable deep roads.

During the [[The Academic Awakening|Bohrovan Period]], some scholars attempted to classify the Court's influence as a natural liminal phenomenon rather than a fey authority. This created disagreement between arcane theorists, fey traditionalists, and early Interstice engineers.

After [[The Fracture]], the Court became more obscure. Much formal knowledge was lost, restricted, or dismissed as superstition. However, stories of the Court persisted in rural customs, traveling performers' traditions, funeral songs, borderland warnings, and the habits of people who live near thin places.

## The Regents of the Liminal Court

The Court is composed of several known Regents. These beings are not ranked in a simple hierarchy. Each governs a different class of threshold, and their domains often overlap.

They are not always worshipped. They are more often acknowledged, petitioned, or carefully avoided.

- Corvathiel — **The Quiet Feather** (final passages, death-thresholds, the souls' last roads)
- [[Veylora]] — **The Mirror Warden** (mirrors, reflections, selfhood, recognition, identity)
- [[Thamior Keth]] — **The Key-Bearer** (doors, locks, keys, access, permission)
- [[Lysanthir]] — **The Candle Between** (guidance, safe passage, wayfinding, hope in darkness)
- [[Maerovyn]] — **The Bell That Binds** (oaths, vows, bargains, contracts, binding words)
- [[Oryndel]] — **The River of Steps** (roads, routes, journeys, timing, inevitable arrival)
- [[Seravelle]] — **The Thorn-Smile Host** (hospitality, sanctuary, guest-law, dangerous welcome)

## The Court's Laws

The Liminal Court is governed by laws that are not always written, but are deeply felt in fey custom.

### The Law of Recognition

> *A thing recognized is changed by being recognized.*

Names matter. Symbols matter. Witness matters.

### The Law of Invitation

> *There is a difference between entering and being invited.*

Trespass has consequences.

### The Law of Passage

> *No crossing is meaningless.*

A passage may be delayed, guided, witnessed, taxed, or forbidden, but it is never nothing.

### The Law of Binding

> *Words spoken at thresholds carry unusual weight.*

Promises, names, bargains, and confessions are dangerous near doors, roads, mirrors, graves, or open water.

### The Law of Return

> *What crosses may return changed.*
> *What is barred may return angry.*
> *What is forgotten may return unnamed.*

## Modern Understanding

In modern Dyradia, most people consider the Liminal Court superstition, folklore, or religious residue from older fey traditions. Scholars debate whether the Regents are discrete beings, symbolic categories, or persistent liminal intelligences associated with the old world.

[[Greenward Annex|Greenward]] scholars are cautious. They tend not to dismiss the Court outright, especially when studying Interstice-adjacent phenomena. The phrase "liminal law" still appears in restricted or old-fashioned research, though many academics prefer safer terms like:

- threshold behavior,
- boundary response,
- symbolic resonance,
- transitional pressure,
- or fey-adjacent ritual patterning.

The Court is not openly worshipped in most major institutions, but its customs survive everywhere.

A person may deny believing in Corvathiel and still open a window after a death.

A scholar may dismiss Maerovyn and still hesitate before breaking a promise in a doorway.

A merchant may laugh at Seravelle and still never refuse salt under a host's roof.

> *The Court survives because crossings survive.*
