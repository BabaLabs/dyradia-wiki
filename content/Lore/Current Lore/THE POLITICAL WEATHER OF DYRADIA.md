### What the World Is Already Doing Without the Party

Dyradia is healing — but unevenly.

- Some places heal by **tightening control**
    
- Some heal by **inviting change**
    
- Some heal by **refusing to choose**
    

Those responses are not philosophical.  
They are _practical reactions to lived history_.

---

## I. MEHRENDT

### Drift: **Stasis → Convergence (Conditional)**

**Why Mehrendt Leans This Way**

Mehrendt:

- Was nearly consumed by the Church
    
- Survived by bureaucracy, armies, and compromise
    
- Learned that _chaos gets people killed fast_
    

The modern Mehrendtian mindset is:

> “Progress is acceptable as long as it’s supervised.”

That is fertile ground for **Seraphel Vaune**.

---

### Political Reality (Day-to-Day)

- Magic licenses are increasingly required
    
- Interstice research exists but is compartmentalized
    
- “Temporary” emergency powers keep getting extended
    
- Veinwatch is officially restrained — unofficially everywhere
    

People are safer.  
They are also watched.

---

### How Stasis Grows Here (Naturally)

- Seraphel’s reforms _work_ in Mehrendt
    
- City leaders cooperate willingly
    
- Laws expand quietly
    
- Detentions are rare but final
    

Most citizens don’t feel oppressed — they feel relieved.

---

### How Convergence Can Appear Here (If Stasis Is Resisted)

If Stasis overreaches:

- Neutral mediators are invited in
    
- “Independent stabilizers” become popular
    
- One person starts resolving disputes too cleanly
    

Mehrendt produces **administrative Convergence**:

> Balance enforced through efficiency.

---

### What Players Notice Early

- Healers afraid to practice openly
    
- Courts that rule “correctly” but coldly
    
- A sense that dissent is legal — but pointless
    

Mehrendt doesn’t feel evil.  
It feels _managed_.

---

## II. FEY’THRA

### Drift: **Renewal → Convergence (Rarely Stasis)**

**Why Fey’thra Leans This Way**

Fey’thra remembers:

- Bohrova’s wonder
    
- The Dyra’s closeness
    
- The loss that followed restraint
    

Elven culture accepts:

- Long consequences
    
- Slow catastrophe
    
- Sacrifice for future harmony
    

They fear stagnation more than chaos.

---

### Political Reality

- Leadership is diffuse and consensus-based
    
- Interstice research is philosophical, not weaponized
    
- Fey crossings are tolerated, even welcomed
    
- Suppression is deeply unpopular
    

They are _listening_ to the world — not commanding it.

---

### How Renewal Grows Here

- Elyndra’s ideas resonate deeply
    
- Rituals are framed as “reconnection”
    
- Awoken myths are treated as unfinished stories
    
- Dangerous magic is mourned, not banned
    

When something goes wrong, Fey’thra asks:

> “What was the world trying to say?”

---

### Why Convergence Is a Risk Here

Fey’thra’s biggest flaw:

- Endless deliberation
    
- Hesitation to impose limits
    

If crises stack:

- Someone who “just knows what to do” becomes invaluable
    
- Balance becomes centralized out of exhaustion
    

Convergence here feels **gentle and spiritual**.

---

### What Players Notice Early

- Beauty alongside danger
    
- Fey creatures acting with purpose
    
- Elders disagreeing without resolution
    
- Magic that feels _alive_ again — and unpredictable
    

---

## III. GOHLSTOUT

### Drift: **Stasis (Strong) → Catastrophic Resistance**

**Why Gohlstout Leans This Way**

Gohlstout survived the Fracture by:

- Building redundancies
    
- Sealing dangerous knowledge
    
- Trusting systems over inspiration
    

They believe:

> “Anything that cannot be stabilized will eventually kill us.”

Seraphel’s philosophy aligns _perfectly_ with this instinct.

---

### Political Reality

- Runeholds remain sealed
    
- Interstice experimentation is taboo
    
- Infrastructure is prioritized over innovation
    
- Stability is treated as a moral good
    

Change is not forbidden — it is **delayed until safe**.

---

### How Stasis Grows Here

- Gohlstout provides tools and engines
    
- Suppression tech originates here
    
- Stasis infrastructure is legitimized as “engineering”
    

They don’t see it as oppression.  
They see it as _maintenance_.

---

### What Happens If Stasis Fails Here

If the world destabilizes anyway:

- Gohlstout does not pivot to Renewal
    
- It fractures internally
    
- Clans and orders clash violently
    

This is where **extremism** is born — not ideology, but desperation.

---

### What Players Notice Early

- Cities that never change
    
- Forbidden halls humming faintly
    
- Young dwarves questioning sealed history
    
- Elders afraid of being right _too late_
    

---

## IV. AVALON

### Drift: **Renewal → Convergence Failure → Renewal Again**

**Why Avalon Leans This Way**

Avalon has no patience for:

- Bureaucracy
    
- Dogma
    
- Waiting
    

They already lost everything once.

Their instinct is:

> “Act now. Fix it later.”

---

### Political Reality

- Power is decentralized
    
- Magic is a survival tool
    
- Suppression is seen as tyranny
    
- Heroes rise quickly — and burn out
    

They are fertile ground for **Elyndra’s vanguard**.

---

### How Renewal Grows Here

- Radical change is celebrated
    
- Dangerous success stories spread fast
    
- Casualties are accepted as the cost of freedom
    

Avalon asks:

> “Who are you to tell us not to try?”

---

### Why Convergence Fails Here

Avalon cannot sustain central balance.  
Anyone who tries becomes:

- A tyrant
    
- A martyr
    
- Or a warning
    

Convergence attempts here collapse violently.

---

### What Players Notice Early

- Passionate leaders
    
- Short-lived peace
    
- Repeated rebuilding
    
- Ideals louder than results
    

---

## V. DAWNMERE & SHARED REGIONS

### Drift: **Convergence Incubator**

Dawnmere, Mirrorwake, borderlands, and trade hubs all share one trait:

> **They need things to keep working.**

Not ideology.  
Not faith.  
Not revolution.

Function.

---

### Why Convergence Grows Here

- Neutral ground attracts mediators
    
- Conflict fatigue is high
    
- Stability becomes the highest value
    

These regions don’t _choose_ Convergence.  
They **lean on it**.

---

### What Players Notice Early

- Conflicts ending quietly
    
- Compromises no one remembers agreeing to
    
- One person always present when things calm down
    

---

## VI. THE CHURCH ISLAND BASTION

### Drift: **Stasis → Renewal (Schism Event)**

This is the most volatile region.

- Conservatives ally with Stasis
    
- Reformists flirt with Renewal
    
- Faith itself is unstable
    

A single event here can:

- Legitimize Seraphel globally
    
- Or ignite a spiritual Renewal wave

# THE BIG PICTURE (Why This Works)

If the players do **nothing**:

- Mehrendt locks down
    
- Gohlstout fortifies
    
- Fey’thra experiments
    
- Avalon burns forward
    
- Neutral zones beg for balance
    

The antagonists don’t conquer the world.

The world **asks for them**.
