# State of Magic in Dyradia

## Current State

Magic is stable, and slowly diminishing. To a "hard to quantify, but easy to notice" kind of level. And as the story begins and progresses, it shows signs of becoming unstable and more "chaotic." Almost as if something, or someone, has managed to reconnect the severed chains and cannot be contained.

---

## The Severed Chain Model

Modern scholars believe:

- Magic is no longer drawn directly from the source
- It now passes through layers of mediation
- Each layer bleeds a little power

A common analogy:

> Ancient Awoken = direct current.
> Modern spellcasters = long-distance transmission.

Loss is inevitable.

This explains:

- Why no Awoken exist
- Why spellcasting is safer
- Why magic feels weaker
- Why overuse causes anomalies

Symptoms commonly noticed by practitioners and laypeople alike:

- Spells feel less generous
- Rituals take longer than old texts imply
- Magical craftsmanship is more brittle
- Enchantments fade faster than expected
- Old magic items "don't make them like they used to"

Nobody can point to a number. Everyone can point to a feeling. This is the "hard to quantify, easy to notice" state.

---

## The Reconnection

Crucially, this is not a clean restoration. It is not a benevolent act.

Someone — or something — is:

- Bridging the fracture
- Forcing a connection
- Reopening conduits that were never meant to be reopened

The system wasn't designed to handle it anymore.

This allows for chaos without full apocalypse — moral ambiguity, multiple factions with valid fears, and signs that escalate slowly but visibly.

---

## How This Feels to the World

### Common Folk

- "Magic's getting temperamental."
- "It doesn't listen like it used to."
- "My gran says this is how it felt before the war."

### Scholars

- Frantic
- Publishing contradictory theories
- Rediscovering forbidden texts

### Churches (All of Them)

- Interpreting this as: judgment, fulfillment, or proof they were right all along

### Elves & Long-Lived Races

- Deeply uneasy
- They recognize the shape of what's happening
- But not the scale
