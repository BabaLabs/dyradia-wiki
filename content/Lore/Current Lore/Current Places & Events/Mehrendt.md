# MEHRENDT (Modern Era)

### “The Crown Jewel” that learned to survive without certainty

## 1) Government

**Form:** Constitutional Monarchy with entrenched aristocracy  
**Head of State:** The Crown of Mehrendt (symbolic + emergency powers)  
**Actual Power:** The **Civic Synod** (nobles + guild seats + magistrates)

### The Civic Synod (the real engine)

- **High Houses (7 seats):** old bloodlines, land rights, military contracts
    
- **Guild Compact (5 seats):** trade consortiums, banks, river bargemen, smiths
    
- **Magistrate College (3 seats):** law courts, civil registry, taxation
    
- **Crown’s Voice (1 seat):** the monarch’s proxy; often a political knife
    

**Why this exists:** After the Fracture era, the people _refused_ another single-point-of-failure regime. Mehrendt rebuilt power like a ship with multiple masts—harder to steer, harder to capsize.

## 2) Power Centers

### The Crown Court (Valemarch)

- ceremonial, political marriages, oathkeeping, diplomatic theater
    
- the Crown still controls: **knighthoods, pardons, wartime mobilization**
    

### The River Guilds

Mehrendt’s rivers are highways. Whoever owns barges owns food, timber, and taxes.

- They “don’t do politics,” but they absolutely do.
    

### The Archive Basilica (not Church of Prim)

A secular scholarly institution that _absorbed_ many post-war records.

- They “catalog truth” — meaning they decide what survives.
    

### The Veinwatch Office

Mehrendt’s quietest and most feared civil agency.

- They track **Interstice anomalies**, magical incidents, and “Awoken-like events.”
    
- Officially: public safety. Unofficially: containment + leverage.
    

## 3) Magic Climate

**Public stance:** “Magic is permitted, regulated, and taxable.”  
**Private stance:** “Magic is returning wrong.”

- Everyday magic exists (healers, hedge mages, warders), but _structured_ magic has drifted.
    
- Older spellcraft works inconsistently near old Interstice scars.
    
- The Synod funds “responsible arcana,” while the Veinwatch suppresses anything that looks like Awakening potential.
    

## 4) Economy & Trade

Mehrendt is the breadbasket and the broker.

- **Exports:** grain, livestock, textiles, inks/paper, finished goods
    
- **Imports:** rare metals (Gohlstout), exotic reagents (Fey’thra), salt/fish (coast)
    
- **Currency power:** Mehrendt mints the coin most other nations grudgingly accept.
    

**Trade doctrine:** “Neutral routes, aggressive tariffs.”  
They don’t conquer land as much as they **buy influence**.

## 5) Military & Security

### The Crownward Legions

Professional army—disciplined, expensive, political.

- strong in defensive warfare, fortifications, river crossings
    

### The Border March Companies

Militias + mercenary auxiliaries on the Avalon edges.

- hardened, less lawful, more pragmatic
    
- also where “banditry” conveniently becomes deniable operations
    

### The Grey Lanterns (internal)

Investigators, magistrate-enforcers, and state-sanctioned problem solvers.

- think: detectives + counterinsurgency + monster-response
    

## 6) Foreign Relations

### Fey’thra

- **Officially:** respectful neighbors, trade agreements
    
- **Reality:** Mehrendt wants Fey’thran reagents and hates Fey’thran unpredictability
    
- there’s constant friction about border settlements and “lost persons”
    

### Gohlstout

- mutual dependency: Mehrendt needs metal; dwarves need food/luxury goods
    
- relationship is stable, a little cold, and very contractual
    

### Avalon

- not a nation, a problem
    
- Mehrendt’s policy: divide, bribe, and “contain the spillover”
    
- the Synod fears an Avalonian unification more than war itself
    

## 7) Internal Fault Lines (the stuff that makes sessions)

1. **Synod corruption vs stability** — people hate the politics but fear alternatives
    
2. **Veinwatch secrecy** — citizens suspect “disappearances” during anomaly events
    
3. **Border identity crisis** — March towns don’t feel like “Mehrendt” anymore
    
4. **Truth control** — the Archive Basilica edits history quietly
    
5. **Magic class divide** — licensed casters prosper; “wild talent” gets hunted
    

## 8) Key Locations in Mehrendt (anchors you can hang stories on)

### Valemarch (Capital)

- ancient stone, modern bureaucracy, masked balls, and river docks
    
- districts include:
    
    - **Crownspire** (court)
        
    - **The Ledgerways** (guild banks)
        
    - **Old Prim Row** (ruins + suppressed history)
        
    - **Southgate Shanties** (where “illegal magic” is common)
        

### Dawnmere (Border Triune)

Neutral trade settlement built over an Interstice Anchor Vault (as we established).

- not claimed by anyone
    
- too useful to crush, too dangerous to own
    

### Harrowfen

Marsh-city where smugglers and “monster hunters” coexist.

- people vanish here when the fog comes in wrong
    

### Stonebridge March

A fortress-town controlling the best land-route into Avalon territory.

- staffed by veterans who don’t trust the Synod anymore
    

### Whitebarrow Fields

Largest grain producers; quietly protected by “unofficial warders”

- crop failures here would break the nation
    

### The Crownroot Vein (DM-only site)

Buried below Valemarch foundations; a stabilizing structure from pre-Fracture meddling.

- if it fails, Valemarch becomes the loudest anomaly on the continent
    

## 9) Adventure Pressure in Mehrendt

Mehrendt is where players get:

- politics that matters
    
- moral compromise
    
- secrets that are _archived_, not spoken
    
- “civilized” answers to supernatural problems (containment, not understanding)
    

**If Dyradia is healing, Mehrendt is the nation most invested in deciding what “healed” is allowed to mean.**

# The Borderlands Region (Dawnmere Sphere)

This is the campaign’s front door, so I’m giving it structure too.

## The Triune Border Agreement (unwritten, enforced anyway)

- Fey’thra allows merchants to cross only via certain paths
    
- Mehrendt tolerates Dawnmere as a taxless necessity
    
- Avalon uses it as a recruitment and intelligence marketplace
    
- Everyone pretends it’s neutral
    
- Everyone breaks the rules
    

## Local Power Players (not “main villains,” just _alive_)

- **The Cartwheel Compact:** caravan families that own the roads
    
- **The Wellkeepers:** civic group that “maintains” the Anchor Vault (they don’t understand it)
    
- **The Bannerless:** mercenaries who enforce neutrality—by violence, if needed
    
- **A Fey’thran Listener:** a quiet figure who can “hear” Interstice hums
    
- **A Veinwatch Liaison:** pretending to be a tax clerk
