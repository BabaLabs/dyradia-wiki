# Chapter 10 — Of Gems and Fire

> *In which a contract becomes ash in a pocket, a fire-brother burns the southern town to ash in five minutes, a water-brother drowns a monk in a sphere of his own conjuring, and a woman in a hood opens a collapsed tunnel by means of a gem the colour of disturbed earth.*

---

## I. The Burning Contract

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] turned away from [[Natas]] in the smoking street of the southern town and smiled the small careful smile she used for strangers she did not wish to inform.

*"No,"* she said. *"I don't know who you are talking about. I just wanted to ask out of curiosity. If you don't mind — I'll be leaving now."*

She did not, the chronicle notes, wait for his response. She turned and walked.

She had taken three steps when she felt the shift in her pocket.

The shift was small — a flicker of weight in the parchment that lay folded against the inside of her robe — and she would not, in the ordinary press of her work, have noticed it. She had been listening for it. She had been listening for it since the moment the bracelet on her own wrist had pulsed, very lightly, against her skin perhaps twenty seconds earlier.

She drew the mission contract from the pocket.

She watched the letters of its clauses begin to disappear from the parchment, one after another, in the slow patient erasure of a thing that had decided to undo itself. She watched until the last word had unmade itself, and then she watched the parchment catch fire in her hand.

She dropped it.

The ash that the parchment left on the cobblestones at her feet had the consistency of fine sand. She looked at it.

She understood.

She turned, slowly, back to [[Natas]] — who was standing in the smoke of his hotel ten paces behind her, looking at her with the patient interest of a brother who had not yet been told what he had been sent to find.

*"I am sorry I lied,"* she said. *"I am not allowed to let much of the mission out. However — it seems the mission has failed. I do know [[Six]], and at this moment he is probably heading to a place near here. I think you would be of great help if you could find him and tell him that he is no longer tied to the contract. He is free to do as he pleases."*

She drew from her robe a small bag of gold coins and pressed it into his hand.

*"If you could give him this — it is the payment for the mission. I am afraid that I can't give it to him myself, since I need to follow the procedure in case of failure. Sorry for your trouble. But I have to go now."*

She did not wait for his response either.

She ran.

---

## II. Shift

She did not stop running until she had cleared the town's southern boundary and was perhaps a quarter-mile into the open country beyond it. She stopped in the cover of a low ridge. She closed her eyes.

*"Shift."*

The familiar presence — the small shift in the air that was, by now, as familiar to her perception as her own heartbeat — gathered into her open palm.

This time the form Shift took was not the mini-dragon-and-sea-serpent of [[Valham]] or the small ball of fog the chronicle had earlier recorded. This time Shift resolved into the form of an eagle — and once resolved, *duplicated itself* into several copies, each as large and as solid as the first, each watching Trinity from its own pair of yellow eyes.

She took from her pockets a sheaf of small notes she had folded ahead of time, and a number of small bags of the same kind of gold she had given Natas.

*"Take the money and the notes to the rest of the people in the mission,"* she said. *"The note informs them that they are free."*

She placed the notes and the gold across the assembled eagles' talons.

She drew, from another pocket, a small necklace of fine silver, and looped it once across the head of the eagle nearest to her.

*"When you are done, please go to the monastery, and inform them that I shall proceed with Plan B."*

She breathed out.

*"Go."*

The eagles lifted in a single quiet motion, scattered to the four directions of the country, and were gone.

Trinity opened her eyes.

She reached, with the part of her perception that had become a habit, for the largest concentration of unusual *vibe* in the country around her. She found one. It came from the north-west.

She smiled.

She set off toward it.

She had not gone very far when she felt, also, the familiar presences of [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] and [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] — heading west and east respectively, in directions that did not match the directions she was running.

*Okay,* she thought. *I'll have to detour, then.*

She turned south. She ran several minutes. She turned west.

She arrived, sooner than she had expected, in sight of a large building in the middle of an otherwise unfeatured stretch of country.

*Can you say "base."*

She reached with her perception again.

She found, inside the building, thousands of presences moving at speed.

*Damn it. There are thousands of people in there. Killing them would —*

She did not finish the thought. She drew her hood further forward. She ran toward the building.

---

## III. The Town That Was

[[Natas]] watched [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] run. He watched until she was out of sight in the dust at the southern edge of the burning town. He weighed the bag of gold she had pressed into his hand.

*"Where the hell did that woman go,"* he said to no one. *"And what the hell is this money for?"*

He turned the bag over.

*"Hmmm. If [[Six]] doesn't know I have this —"*

[[Six]]'s voice cut, abruptly, through his thoughts.

*"You can have the money. Just get to [[Zin]]. Now."*

Natas grinned at the air.

*"Alright. Cool. Thanks. I'll get there as soon as possible. But first — I have to do something here."*

A pause across the link.

*"Alright. Fine. Just hurry."*

*"Yeah, yeah. I know. I won't be long."*

Six did not respond.

Natas pocketed the gold. He turned, with the unhurried patience of a man who had decided that the rest of his day's errand could wait the small further while it took him to finish a thing he was already in the middle of, and walked back toward the centre of the town.

He raised both hands.

The chronicle records, of what followed, only the rough outline. The buildings that had not yet caught fire from the hotel caught fire in the first minute. The buildings that had already partially caught fire were brought, by the time the second minute had passed, to a degree of involvement they would not survive. The buildings in the outer ring — the ones the wind had not been carrying the heat toward — Natas approached individually and laid his hand upon, and from his palm the fire that he made, in the manner that he made it, took up residence in their walls. The fire-golem he had left at the hotel had not yet been recalled. It walked through the streets at his shoulder.

By the fifth minute, the chronicle records, the town was nothing but ash.

It had been, the chronicle observes, by no measure a large town.

Natas left it. He took the eastern road. He ran.

## IV. The Monk at the Gate

[[Marxus]] had not slept well in the room the [[Hypostasis Monastery]] had assigned him.

He had not, in truth, intended to sleep at all. He had told the monk who had shown him to the room — a middle-aged gate-keeper named **Medinas** — that he would let himself out at first light to speak with the order's senior, and Medinas, in the patient manner of a monastery employee whose job was to handle exactly such houseguests, had agreed.

In the small hour before dawn, however, the monastery had received word.

The chronicle has not preserved the precise channel by which the word reached the monastery's officer-of-night. The chronicle has preserved the consequence: that by the time Marxus emerged from the room at the appointed hour, the senior of the order who would have received him was no longer in residence — having been called east on what the staff would later describe as *urgent monastic business* — and the message Marxus was given in the senior's stead was that the mission he had come about had ended.

He did not, at first, ask what *ended* meant.

*"What do you mean it's over?"* he said to Medinas — who had, by misfortune of duty rotation, been the monk delegated to deliver the news. *"What happened to cause it to end? I was just informed of the mission yesterday."*

He turned slowly in the corridor.

*"You're kidding me, right?"*

He raised his hand. The water in the basin of the corridor's stone fountain lifted out of the basin and formed, in his palm, a small sphere that turned slowly and contained, in its core, a small still image of the inside of the fountain's stone.

*"This is — extremely irritating. I should kill all of you for wasting my time like this."*

He let the sphere drift to and from his fingertips.

*"Although — I don't think father or Six would appreciate it if I were to kill all of you. Hmm."*

He stared at the wall.

He thought.

He looked back at Medinas.

*"Well. I've decided not to kill all of you. That'd be extremely rude, I suppose."*

He set the sphere down on the floor.

*"But I would like you to let me stay here for another night to rest."*

Medinas inclined his head and showed him back to the room.

The chronicle records, of the rest of Marxus's stay at the monastery that morning, the precise act that did, in the end, take place: that Medinas — having been the monk who delivered the news the brother had not wished to hear, and having been the monk who walked the brother back to his room afterward — did not, by the time Marxus had reached the room's door, recall to Marxus's satisfaction why he had been spared. Marxus paused at the threshold. He turned. He raised the same hand he had drawn the water from.

The sphere of water that formed around Medinas's face was the same sphere of water that had been on the corridor floor a moment earlier.

The monk struggled.

Marxus added more.

He weighed the additional water across Medinas's chest until Medinas's struggle slowed to the small persistent twitching of a man whose body had not yet accepted that it had been drowned.

*"Oh, my,"* Marxus said. *"See — if you would have just shown me to my room like a nice quiet monk, none of this would have happened. But — no. You had to go and be a fool."*

The body slumped.

*"Oh — I'm sorry. What was that? You don't want to die, you say? Hmm. Well. Too bad. Now I'm going to sleep."*

He stepped over Medinas.

He paused at the threshold and turned back.

*"You'll probably be dead by the time I wake up. And if you are — it will show me how weak you monks are."*

He stepped into the room.

He paused. He came back out.

*"Oh — and if you wake me up, the same results of your dying will take place. So — once again. Good night."*

He went back into the room and slept.

The chronicle records that Medinas was, by the time Marxus woke that afternoon, dead.

## V. The Race to the Base

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] pushed the horse he had stolen from the rear-guard cart faster than any horse of its condition had any business being pushed.

He twisted. He wove. He had the horse jump over the random low waves of earth [[Zin]] periodically sent up across the road from somewhere on his back-trail. He arrived in sight of the Western Barracks as the sun was finishing its descent into the western mountains, and he screamed at the top of his lungs for the gate to open and for the men inside to be battle-ready before he had reached it.

The gate opened.

The gate closed behind him.

He swung off the horse at the courtyard's edge, threw the reins at a stableman, and crossed to the line of soldiers who had assembled in the yard.

*"Don't expect to kill it,"* he said. *"Just stall him until we find a plan."*

He did not, in the moment, name what *it* was. He did not, in the moment, need to. He turned and ran into the depths of the base, into the officer's library where the older books of the kingdom's old wars were kept, and began the kind of search a man performs when he has been given a problem his ordinary training has not equipped him for.

*Books on killing various inhumans,* he muttered to himself. *Books on killing various inhumans.*

Several leagues to the east, the chronicle records, [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] was riding hard in the opposite direction toward the capital city — without yet knowing the road to the city or what he would do upon reaching it, but knowing only that the man who had taught him to hold a sword over the past month was, on the road behind him, alone with a creature he was not equipped to fight.

He rode anyway.

The eastern road, the chronicle notes, was not the road that would, in the end, lead him to the help he was looking for.

---

## VI. The Wave at the Wall

[[Zin]] arrived at the Western Barracks not long after [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] had.

He came up over the slow rise that hid the building's east approach and surveyed the wall and the armaments above it. The chronicle understands that he was not impressed.

He raised his hands.

The wall of earth he sent at the barracks was the same kind of wall he had used at the cart, scaled up by perhaps a factor of three. It struck the wall of the building at the height of the parapet. The bowmen on the parapet were thrown from their positions — some onto the parapet's inner walkway, some over the parapet's outer edge into the dust of the yard. The wall of the barracks itself did not, by the chronicle's record, hold.

Zin walked through the breach.

He looked, as he came in, for the stranger he had been searching for. He did not find him in the entrance corridor. He continued, with the patient steady gait of a man who had been told by his brother to detain a particular person and who had not, by his temperament, been built to vary the order of his search.

He did not, in his patient pacing, notice the hooded figure who had passed through the same breach a heartbeat behind him.

---

## VII. The Confusion of the Hall

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] had observed, on her approach to the breach, that the thousands of presences in the building were moving as a collective mass — all in the same direction, all in something between panic and military discipline — toward the east gate, where the wave of earth had struck.

She had taken her advantage.

She entered the building behind Zin and stepped to the side at the first cross-corridor. She raised her hand. She whispered the word *turbato*.

The soldiers around her at the cross-corridor entered the brief, staggering state of confusion that the spell produced — not loss of consciousness, the chronicle is careful to note, but the loss of the small connective threads by which a soldier on a hurried errand registered the figure in front of him as a hostile to engage. They blinked at her. They blinked at each other. They resumed their rush eastward without including her in their consideration.

She moved.

She had been told — by the order's intelligence on the King's hiding place — the route the deep tunnels took from the surface to the chamber where the King was kept. She had been told that the route ran along the western wall of the lower halls. She found the western wall. She found the descending stairwell. She crossed half a dozen levels of confused soldiers — twice more whispering *turbato* against squads that came at her with their weapons rising — and came, in the end, to a door.

The door was a heavy wooden one with iron crossbeams. Behind it lay the corridor that had led to the runic chamber.

She opened the door.

She closed it behind her.

She ran.

The chronicle records that the corridor she ran down was not the corridor [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] had run down earlier in the evening. It was a parallel corridor — built, the chronicle suspects, as a maintenance route for the deeper chambers, and not provided to the company by the General that morning. Trinity had been provided it by her order. The chronicle records, neutrally, that this is a thing two competing institutions can occasionally each independently know about the same secret architecture.

She came to a junction.

She felt, ahead and below her, the presence of the King's body.

She felt, also, the presence that had been resident in his body until very recently — and which was, by her perception, no longer in the chamber where the body lay.

She felt it in the corridor between the two.

She stopped.

She breathed.

---

## VIII. The Tunnel Collapsed

The corridor between the maintenance route and the King's chamber had been, by [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s evacuation order, collapsed.

The earth that had been pulled down from the ceiling and the side walls of the corridor by the order's miners had been pulled down well. It had been pulled down with the practiced thoroughness of men who had been told by an officer they trusted that the cost of an incomplete job exceeded the cost of doing it twice. The corridor was sealed by a wall of compacted soil and broken stone of significant depth.

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] looked at the wall.

*They never make anything easy.*

She reached into her robe and drew, from one of the many pockets sewn along the inside of its lining, a small gem the colour of disturbed earth. The gem was a thumb's-length across and had been cut, by some craftsman the chronicle does not record, into a faceted oval of a kind the order used for purposes the order did not advertise.

She knelt at the wall of soil. She placed the gem against the wall. She closed her eyes.

She spoke, softly:

*"Ego lethargus vos moveo."*

The chronicle records — with the careful neutrality it reserves for the practices of orders whose deeper magic it does not yet understand — that the wall of soil began to part. The earth did not flow; it slid, in a slow controlled separation, into two equal masses against the corridor's left and right walls, leaving a clear passage at the corridor's centre.

She picked up the gem. She walked through.

---

## IX. The Chains of Fire

She had taken perhaps a dozen paces into the cleared corridor when the spectre struck.

The chronicle records the strike with as much precision as it is able. The thing that had emerged from [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]]'s body in the fourth chamber — the thing that had risen out of the throne when the bloodline-protections had lost their tether — had been pacing the collapsed corridor in the small interval since Edward's miners had sealed the chamber. It had been, by Trinity's perception, *waiting*. It had felt the wall part. It had felt her come through the wall.

It leapt at her.

Trinity threw up an energy shield in the same motion her body had been already half-completing when the leap began. The shield held — for a heartbeat. The force of the spectre's strike against it was not a force the order's standard shielding had been calibrated against, and the shield broke. Trinity was thrown the length of the cleared corridor backward and hit the wall of compacted soil at the corridor's far end.

She stayed there for a long moment.

The spectre paused.

It turned. It moved toward the breach Trinity had opened in the collapse — the only exit it had been given. It was, the chronicle notes, less interested in killing her than in escaping the chamber.

Trinity rolled to her feet. She raised her right hand toward it across the corridor.

She spoke, in the carrying voice the order taught its agents to use against entities whose ear was not, in the ordinary register, present:

*"Ego advoco super vestri pactum!"*

The chronicle records — and Trinity in her later report would corroborate — that what followed was the response of a thing that had heard, in the language of its binder, the syllables of a contract it had not yet understood it was bound by.

The corridor's floor opened.

From the opening rose chains.

The chains were on fire.

They rose in a slow controlled spiral around the spectre, looped its incorporeal shape twice at a height somewhere between its throat and its waist, and bound it in place. It writhed against them. It shrieked. The shriek was not a sound in the ordinary sense; it was the sound of a thing whose nature did not include throats but which had, in the moment of its binding, been required to express the kind of objection only a throat had been designed to produce.

*"VOS NULLUS OPULENTIA SUPER MIHI!"* it shrieked. *You have no power over me.*

Trinity smiled, briefly.

*"Yes, I do. And of course, so does the monastery. Besides — don't you know? Spectres are the weakest demonic creatures."*

She drew from another pocket of her robe a second gem. This one was white, perfectly faceted, and the size of a chestnut.

She raised it.

She spoke:

*"Absum."*

The spectre — the chains of fire still around it — was drawn into the white gem the way a thread is drawn into a needle. It did not, by the chronicle's record, go willingly. It went anyway. The white gem brightened, dimmed, and resolved into a deep stable red.

Trinity caught it.

She put it in her pocket.

She walked back along the corridor to the place where the brown gem had parted the soil, picked the brown gem up, and put it in the same pocket.

She walked, then, the rest of the way to the chamber.

---

## X. The Throne

The body of King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]] sat in the chair Trinity had been told to expect: a worked stone throne against the far wall of the chamber, with the dagger-wound under the breastbone now closed where Ezericht had pulled the blade free. The glass of wine the King had been holding had fallen from the arm of the throne to the floor. The wine had pooled and dried.

Trinity walked across the chamber slowly.

She stopped a single pace from the throne.

She propped the King's head up gently between her thumb and the heel of her hand, so that — for the moment of her speaking — they were facing each other eye to eye.

She smiled, faintly.

*"I can't help but wonder which of our blood was stronger,"* she said. *"Not that it matters now of course. But I am — curious. If as I am now I would be able to —"*

She stopped.

She shook her head.

*"Never mind."*

She lowered the King's head, gently, back to where she had taken it from. She looked at the body for a long moment.

*"Anyway. The Gheronda sends his most sincere apology for your state. After all — good trusting friends are hard to find now a days."*

She smiled, in the small bitter manner of a woman who had heard, in her own voice, the hypocrisy of the words she had been instructed to deliver.

*"And hopes you don't take the betrayal to the heart. Though the spectre that was out there suggests you already have."*

The smile faded.

She breathed.

*"About the — promise. It will be carried out. So — not to worry. Your death is not in vain."*

She spoke the next words at a register so low the chronicle is unable to confirm whether the King's body, by any mechanism the chronicle understands, could have heard them. The chronicle records that Trinity spoke them anyway.

The chronicle does not preserve the words.

She straightened.

She dropped the King's head, this time without ceremony.

She drew from another inner pocket of her robe nine more white gems. Each was the same size and faceting as the gem in which the spectre was now contained. She placed them in a slow careful arc across the floor of the chamber at the King's feet — three at the centre, three at each shoulder of the body, three at a distance the chronicle suspects was calculated against measures Trinity had not chosen to explain to it.

She stepped back two paces.

*"Good thing you're dead,"* she said to the body. *"Or this would be incredibly painful."*

She raised her hands.

The gems brightened in unison.

The chronicle records — with the same neutrality it reserved for the wall of soil and the binding of the spectre — that what followed was a procedure the order had developed, by means and over a period the chronicle does not yet specify, for the safe conveyance of bodies of significant magical valuation from places the order did not wish to be seen retrieving them from.

The body of the King, by the time the procedure was complete, was no longer in danger of decomposition.

It was, however, still in the chamber.

Trinity sat down cross-legged on the floor in front of the throne. She let her shoulders fall. She looked at the body of the man who had been, until a few hours earlier, the legitimate sovereign of [[Mehrendt]].

She thought, briefly, about the Gheronda's apology.

She let the thought go.

She closed her eyes and waited for [[Shift]] — who had, by now, distributed the gold and the nullification notes she had given it and would, by the rule of her summoning, be on its way back to her — to arrive and help her carry the body home.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the resolution of the mission contract by means of the destruction of its subject — the chronicle's first canonical instance of the **Veneficus instrument's self-burning at completion or breaking** — and the cascade of related events triggered by the same instant: [[Trinity Stolen]]'s dispatch of [[Shift]] with payment-bags and nullification notes to the surviving signatories; [[Natas]]'s incidental incineration of the southern town between his receipt of the news and his obedience to it; [[Marxus]]'s drowning of the gate-monk **Medinas** at the [[Hypostasis Monastery]]; [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s race back to the Western Barracks and his search for material on the killing of inhuman beings; [[Zin]]'s arrival and his wave-of-earth strike against the barracks wall; [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s entry to the base behind him under the cover of his attack; and Trinity's binding and capture of the spectre that had risen from the throne, by means of the order's Latin-tradition chain-of-fire instrument and a faceted white gem.

The chapter establishes, on the chronicle's first explicit record, several capabilities of Trinity Stolen's order beyond the Veneficus contract tradition: the *brown gem* parting of compacted earth; the *white gem* binding and containment of incorporeal entities; the *amplifico*, *turbato*, and the new *advoco super vestri pactum* incantations; and a body-preservation procedure of unspecified mechanism by which the order conveyed magically significant remains.

The chapter further records the *promise* between [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s order and [[Alaric La'Monte]] — the substance of which the chronicle does not yet specify — and Trinity's private acknowledgment, in soft register at the foot of the throne, that her order's apologies to the dead King were not, by her own assessment, sincere.

*Period source: the founding fragments preserved in The RP that Started it All, posts 193–199.*
