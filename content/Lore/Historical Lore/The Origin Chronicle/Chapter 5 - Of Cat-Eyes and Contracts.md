# Chapter 5 — Of Cat-Eyes and Contracts

> *In which a monk's face changes its shape in the morning light, an arrow turns to ash in her hand, three contracts are read aloud by a creature shaped like a serpent, and a man with a bow draws another man aside for a word that will alter the shape of the mission.*

---

## I. The Inn at First Light

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] woke from a restless sleep and lay for a long moment looking at the rafters.

*I wonder if they got into any trouble yet. Left them alone for about ten hours now.*

He sat up. He was not a man who, in the morning, took long to decide that the day's worries were as well met early as late. He pulled the rest of his gear out of the corner where he had stacked it the night before and laid it on the bed — the broadsword, the bow, the quiver. He added to the quiver several arrows he had not, the previous evening, found cause to draw: razor-tipped shafts for cutting at close range, eyelet shafts for tying lines through, and several shafts with small wax-sealed packages affixed at the tip.

He came, in the bottom of the gear-bundle, across a turquoise amulet whose pale stone had not warmed in his hand for some months. He laughed once, quietly, at himself for forgetting it, and slipped the cord over his head.

He set a small trap inside the room door — a simple line-and-weight he could disarm with a touch of the right hand — and went downstairs.

*"Innkeep. I'll be extending my stay."*

He slid payment across the counter.

*"Don't try to enter the room."*

The innkeeper nodded. Edward drew his hood forward against the brightness of the morning, stepped out into the sunlit street, and walked along the side-alleys toward [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s shack.

He stopped at the last corner.

The shack was visible from where he stood. So were the bodies in the yard. So was the figure in the dark robe coming up the path with another robed figure at her shoulder.

*Accursed priest. I knew she wasn't to be trusted. Reinforcements, eh?*

He stepped backward into the corner's shadow, drew an arrow with a small package at its tip from the quiver, nocked it, drew, and swung around the corner.

The arrow's tip was aligned with [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s chest.

*"Explain yourself,"* he called, *"or meet your maker!"*

---

## II. The Three Arrivals

[[Six]], walking from the Inn along a different alley, came around the same corner from the other side perhaps fifteen seconds later. He saw the bodies. He saw the bow. He saw the priest at the bow's far end.

*"Ummm. Good morning?"* he said.

Ezericht, inside the shack, heard Edward's voice carry through the wall and recognized the particular hard pitch of it from the South Gate.

*He's yelling. Great. More fighting?*

He set his glass down and looked across the table at [[Cain]].

*"If you will excuse me — there is some kind of commotion going on outside."*

He stepped out the door, took in the scene at the path — Edward with a drawn bow, Trinity and a robed monk at the bow's other end, Six standing carefully halfway between them — and did not, for one beat, breathe.

*Damn it. He's going to kill him.*

He ran down the path and put a hand on Edward's shoulder.

*"Stop! Hold your fire, Z!"*

He turned to Trinity, breathing hard.

*"Don't be so hasty, friend. I know not of this stranger either. But I'm sure she should be able to clear things up for us."*

He looked at the monk at Trinity's shoulder, found nothing he recognized, and addressed Trinity.

*"Who is this man? And what purpose does he have with you?"*

He turned, leaned against the wall of the shack, and added, without looking back at Edward:

*"G'morning. I hope you slept well."*

---

## III. The Insult

Trinity looked at Edward over the head of the arrow.

She had been about to reply with what she would have called, ordinarily, her diplomatic register — the register that converted the rude things she sometimes wished to say into less rude versions that still accomplished the same purpose — but the diplomatic register required, as a precondition, that she still feel diplomatic. The morning had not so far given her cause.

*"Mr. Ezericht is right,"* she said. *"I do have a rather good explanation for the presence of the priest. However — I refuse to give it to anyone as long as your arrow is pointing at me, Mr. Edward. And even if it were not so, I don't feel like making a… show for people. Therefore I won't speak unless it is inside the house. So now if you will excuse me, I shall go in."*

She turned to [[Zant]].

*"Mr. Zant, would you please follow me."*

She began to walk past the company toward the shack's door. Edward did not lower the bow. She passed within an arm's length of him. When she had nearly reached the door she stopped, turned, and addressed Edward over her shoulder with the diplomatic register entirely abandoned.

*"Oh — and Mr. Edward. You should not go around aiming your arrows at everyone. Not only does it make you look as someone obtuse, brute, and boorish — but also, rather than a professional, you look like a child who just received quivers for toys."*

She smiled. She turned. She continued walking toward the door.

*Serves him right. To have ruined my good mood this early in the morning — such a pity.*

Six, from the side of the path, allowed himself the faintest smile.

*"That's right, 'Mr. Edward.' You shouldn't go pointing your arrows at people like that. It's just plain rude."*

He laughed under his breath.

*"Oh, joy. This day just keeps getting better."*

He moved to follow the monk and the priest into the house.

---

## IV. The Shot

Edward did not answer aloud.

*The nerve of that damned religious puppeteer,* he thought. *I'll have too much explaining to do if I kill her now. But she's not getting away with that.*

He breathed once.

Trinity reached the door with her back fully toward him. He released the arrow.

The shaft sank into her lower leg at the calf.

Trinity went down hard and forward, catching herself against the doorframe with one hand. The arrow had passed through the muscle and out the other side. A spray of dark red crossed the doorframe behind her in a fine arc. The smell of fresh blood, coppery and warm, opened out into the morning air of the yard.

Six's eyes — what little of them the goggles let show — registered the spray with a small clinical interest.

Edward leaned the bow against his shoulder and laid his other hand on the sword hilt.

*"You would be wise not to insult a career killer, madam. The next time will not be a warning shot, I assure you."*

He set the bow back on his shoulder fully.

*"Oh — and I'm tripling my fee due to this incident."*

---

## V. The Hunter Returns

[[Snap]] came back into Sahlen through the main gate at the worst possible moment for someone who had hoped to deliver a deer to his butcher in peace.

He carried a longbow in one hand and was dragging the day's deer along the cobblestones by its hind legs with the other. He had been out before dawn; he had taken the deer cleanly with two shafts; and he had returned to Sahlen with the comfortable expectation of breakfast and a quiet morning of trinket-selling.

He saw the woman at the doorway, the arrow in her leg, the blood at the doorframe, and stopped.

He dropped the deer.

He went to the woman at a half-run, putting his hand on the still-blood-stained hatchet at his belt without drawing it. He spoke to her with the gentleness of a man who had treated wounded creatures all morning and knew that a person was, structurally, not too dissimilar a problem.

*"Would you like the arrow removed by me — or should I go get someone with medical experience?"*

He did not, when he asked it, look up at the man with the bow at the corner.

---

## VI. The Face

Trinity had not, at first, looked up at the man asking.

She had been concentrating on the pain in her leg, suppressing it with the small breath-discipline of her order, and on the slow movement of her hand toward the arrow's shaft. She had been further occupied by a thing she had been trying — and failing — to hold off since the moment the arrow had hit her.

She was angry.

The Hypostasis training had given her many disciplines for many circumstances, but the discipline for the particular kind of anger she was now feeling — the cold, exact, ancient kind that did not belong to the mortal version of her face — was a discipline she had never fully mastered.

Her face changed.

The change was not gradual. It was not theatrical. It happened in the small breath she took to steady her hand on the arrow.

Her brown eyes were no longer brown. They were a glowing pale blue, almost white, and they cast their own faint light onto the cheekbones below them. The shape of her pupils was no longer round. The pupils had narrowed to vertical slits in the manner of a cat or a serpent. From the corners of her eyes, fine markings the same pale blue-white as her irises traced downward along her cheekbones to her jaw and upward along her brow.

On her forehead, two small horns the same blue-white as the markings had not been there a moment before.

Snap, who had been about to lay his hand on the arrow shaft, withdrew it gently. He did not move away. He did not, particularly, show fear. The chronicle records: of the men present in the yard that morning, Snap was the only one whose first response to Trinity's altered face was to remain exactly where he was and wait for her to speak.

Trinity looked up at him.

She smiled — small, weary, deliberate.

*"No,"* she said. *"It will be fine. This is nothing but a small scratch."*

She closed her hand around the arrow's shaft.

She pulled it free in a single motion. The shaft turned to a thin grey ash in her fingertips. The skin where the arrow had passed knit closed across perhaps the span of three breaths, leaving only the trails of drying blood down her leg and a small patch of torn fabric on the ground where her flesh had briefly been.

She stood.

*"See. It isn't even there now."*

She patted dust from her robe. She had already, by her own standards, forgotten the pain. The anger had not gone. The pale eyes remained pale. The markings remained.

She turned the smile, mockingly, on Edward.

*"I am sorry, Mr. Edward. But I am afraid that I won't change the terms of the contract on your whim. If I did, then I would never get it signed."*

She let the smile sharpen.

*"Oh — and Mr. Edward. Even though your next shot won't be a warning — it won't come near killing me. Not only that, but I can promise you that the next time you shoot me, you will lose some limbs as an exchange. So please control your temper. I would rather not start fighting this early in the morning. Either way — that would be such a drag."*

She breathed out.

Her face returned, slowly, to the face the company had seen the night before — the brown eyes resurfacing, the markings fading, the horns receding into the smooth skin of her forehead.

The vertical pupils remained.

Six watched it all with the patience of a man cataloguing a thing he intended to study later. When the arc of blood on the doorframe came within reach he laid his hand against it and drew, in a slow careful gesture, the blood-spray off the wood and into the small glass he had taken from his coat. It went into the glass clean. He sealed the glass and put it away.

*"Hmm,"* he said. *"Bloodshed this early in the morning? This is my kind of team."*

---

## VII. The Deer Declined

Snap watched the arrow turn to ash without comment. He looked at Six, then at the deer he had dropped behind him in the road.

*"I am about to skin that deer over there,"* he said. *"Would you be needing the blood from it?"*

Six considered the offer with what passed for politeness in him.

*"Hmm. The blood of an animal. Nah. I think I'll have to pass."*

He turned toward the shack.

Snap did not wait for a follow-up. He went back to the deer, picked it up by the hind legs, and dragged it off into a side alley to handle the morning's business in a place better suited to it.

Six stepped through the door.

---

## VIII. The Undead Yardsmen

The interior of the shack was, by daylight, less hospitable than it had been by candlelight.

The corpses of the soldiers killed in the dawn ambush had not been removed. Ezericht's blade was leaning against a chair. Cain was on the corner stool by the back wall with a cup he had not, in the end, drunk from.

Six surveyed the situation with the satisfaction of a craftsman walking into a workshop full of unused materials.

*"Well. I see you don't believe in cleaning up very often,"* he said to Ezericht.

He raised his hands.

The bodies in the room stood up.

They did not speak; they did not register; they simply rose into the same stationary posture and waited. Six gestured. They walked, one by one, out the front door, past the deer-stain in the road, and arranged themselves at the corners of the shack's yard as a perimeter.

*"There. Much better, if I do say so myself."*

Ezericht looked at the empty places on his floor where men had recently been, and then at Six.

*"Looks like there is more to this woman than meets the eye,"* he said, half to himself. *"Lovely. I have to watch out for her as well."*

He sat down again in his chair.

*"No, I didn't have time after the raid of soldiers and Z nearly murdering our monkish friend to tidy up a bit."*

He turned to Cain.

*"Sorry about leaving you hanging there. But I had to step in before someone ended up as a corpse."*

He turned, finally, to Trinity.

*"Now, Miss Monk. Would you be so kind as to get this whole contract thing over with? I'm a patient man — but this is starting to annoy even me."*

Six took the other chair.

*"Yes,"* he said. *"Let's get on with this contract signing, shall we? I rather dislike having to wait this long for anything."*

He drew the new glass from his coat, tipped a measure of the freshly harvested liquid into his cross's reservoir, and recorked it.

*"You know — if you weren't the one responsible for this mission, I just might have had to kill you to get more of your blood. It is extremely…"* He searched briefly for the word. *"…unique. Yes. Unique."*

---

## IX. The Three Scrolls

Trinity crossed to the window. She did not, immediately, answer Six.

She placed her palm against the frame and reached, with the part of her perception that had become a habit, for [[Shift]]'s presence. The morning's anger had cost her some discipline; she had to work for it. She whistled once, very softly, into the morning air outside the window. The whistle was not for anyone in the yard. It traveled.

She held the concentration for perhaps fifteen seconds. The presence, when she had it, was thin and willing.

She let go of the breath and turned back to the room.

*"Mr. Six,"* she said, *"I don't know whether to take that as a compliment or not. However — talking about the other issue, the contract is on its way. Meanwhile I will explain the purpose of bringing the priest."*

She lifted her hand toward Zant, who had taken a chair beside Ezericht.

*"Since this is obviously a dangerous mission, I thought we should have someone who can heal others quickly — like if we were in the middle of a battle. I know that Mr. Ezericht has potions and medication that can do that as well, but it would be difficult to take them in the middle of a battle. Please don't take it as an offense."*

She felt Shift's presence return, larger this time.

She extended a hand.

*"I believe the contracts just arrived. Shift."*

Three scrolls dropped, in a soft fall, into her open palm.

Six's eyes lifted, briefly, in something between amusement and curiosity.

*"Well. So now you're a magician as well? Hmm."*

He stood.

*"So. How do I sign this contract of yours so we can get this out of the way?"*

*"I am not a magician,"* Trinity replied. *"The contracts were simply brought to me."*

She set one of the three scrolls on the table.

*"On how you are supposed to sign your contract — it is quite easy, since it is a coul-pactum. You simply will have to sign your name and the binding is done. Shift."*

The scroll on the table opened by itself. A small dark feather and an ink-well rose into the air a hand's breadth above it.

*"This is the contract everyone is supposed to sign. Oh — and please don't comment on how it is floating. It isn't a magician's trick."*

Six smirked.

*"I see."*

He took the feather, dipped it, signed the contract without looking at it, and sat back down.

Ezericht shook his head.

*Quite the hasty one he is. Not me, though. I must know more.*

He looked at Trinity.

*"Would you be so kind as to inform us of the details of the contract? I like to know WHAT I'm signing before I sign it. Naturally."*

Cain, from the corner, considered the conversation with the air of a man already arithmetically certain of his answer.

*"Very well, then. I will sign this contract of yours. Even though I have no use whatsoever for this 'reward' money for saving your most dearest king. Perhaps I may find something along the way that will be of value to me."*

He came to the table, took the feather from where Six had returned it, and added his name beside Six's.

He went back to his corner.

---

## X. The Reading

Trinity inclined her head to Ezericht.

She drew a slim necklace from her robe, pointed its pendant at the contract, and whispered the word *amplifico*. The text of the contract — which had been rendered in a script tight enough to be illegible from across the room — lifted off the parchment into the air above the table and enlarged itself until each line could be read by any standing within the shack.

The chronicle does not preserve the entire text, but the substance of its twelve clauses, as Trinity read them aloud while the rest of the company watched the suspended letters re-form themselves in air, was as follows:

The contract was titled, in the Latin of the Hypostasis tradition, **Absentis Consensio** — *the Mission Agreement* — and it was constructed as a *certamen-partum*, a combined contract integrating the blood and the soul instruments into a single binding. It defined three terms: the *Mission* (the agreement among signatories regarding the safe being of Dyradia's current King); the *Monastery* (represented at signature by the Gheronda and by Trinity Stolen); and the *Reward* (2,500 gold coins to each signatory at completion).

Its conditions followed in a sequence Trinity read with the patient cadence of a person who had read such documents many times before. The signatories were forbidden from disclosing the monastery's involvement or any mission-relevant information to any party outside the contract; any new information about the King discovered after signing was to be reported to the monastery, and the monastery was to reciprocate; old information held before signing on either side could be withheld; *any signatory's relevant question to any other signatory had to be answered, even if the answerer considered the question unimportant.* The reward fell due at completion. A signatory who joined after the initial signing required the concord of all prior signatories. A signatory who ceased to exist before completion had their signature struck from the contract and the conditions released for that signatory only. If the King ceased to exist before completion, the contract became null. For the completion of the mission, the King's safe being could not be put at risk by any signatory.

The amplified text held in the air.

Six and Cain had already signed.

Edward had not yet entered the room.

Zant was reading the words above the table with the patient attention of a man who had spent much of his life in close work with old texts.

Ezericht had begun to read also, with the air of a man who reserved the right to find the loophole.

---

## XI. The Two Smaller Contracts

Six set his cup down.

*"Well. Now that we have the regular contract out of the way — shall I sign the one that pertains to me and the acquisition of your blood? Or should we just forget about that one for now?"*

He let the slight smirk hang.

Trinity gave him the smile she reserved for unsubtle attempts.

*"Nice try, Mr. Six. However — I would rather you sign that contract too. Also, I might as well show Mr. Edward's contract too, I suppose."*

She lifted the necklace again and amplified the other two scrolls in their turn.

The Blood Agreement — **Crour Consensio** — was a brief instrument with a single substantive clause. It bound Six, after signing, never to alter or use Trinity Stolen's blood in any form. Its consequence was the same as the larger instrument.

The Memory Agreement — **Memoria Consensio** — was a similarly brief instrument, addressed not to Six but to *Edward Zywarf*, in the spelling the contract preserved from Edward's own self-introduction. It bound any member of the monastery from altering Edward's memory in any form — deletion, replacement, or other — at any time after signing.

The amplified texts held in the air, three sets of suspended letters, while their substance was understood and the silence in the shack settled around them.

*"However,"* Trinity said, *"I would prefer Mr. Edward to sign the other contract before this one — if he would be so kind. Only to bring some relief to my paranoid mind, that is."*

She set the feather back down.

---

## XII. A Word Outside

Edward had come, by that time, through the front door.

He had stopped just inside it. He had looked at the amplified text in the air above the table. He had looked at the signatures already placed: Six's, and Cain's, and the empty space where his own and Zant's and Ezericht's had not yet appeared.

He had thought, in the rapid quiet way of a man whose face had been trained to remain still while his mind worked, several things in close succession.

The first was that the Hypostasis monastery had been more dangerous to him than he had allowed himself to believe, and that the contract on the table was instrument enough — by its own terms — to make him their property in a way that mere money could not have done.

The second was that he could not, on the morning of the day the contract was signed, fight the monastery openly. He would not survive it.

The third was that the contract held only two of the seats — Trinity's and the Gheronda's — for the monastery side, and four of the seats — by his count — for the company side. The company side outnumbered the monastery side. The company side could, if managed correctly, do work the monastery had not specifically contracted for. The contract said nothing about *parallel* arrangements.

The fourth was that he could not propose such an arrangement to all four of the company side openly, because he did not yet know all four well enough.

The fifth was that of the two who had already signed — Six was, in Edward's judgement, too volatile to enlist into a side-arrangement, and Cain was, in Edward's judgement, far too pragmatic to refuse one.

The sixth was that of the two who had *not* yet signed — Zant was an unknown quantity and could not be approached yet, and Ezericht was — as Edward had already concluded over a number of small observations through the previous night — a man with reservations he had not yet voiced.

The seventh was that he had perhaps ninety seconds.

He turned to Ezericht.

*"A word outside, Ez."*

He did not, when he said it, look at anyone else in the room.

He turned and walked back out the door.

Ezericht set down the contract he had begun to read, exhaled in the long-suffering manner of a man who had had similar experiences with Edward before, and stood.

He followed.

The morning had brightened. The undead yardsmen had not moved from their corners.

Edward did not stop walking until they were behind the corner of the shack, where the line of sight from the door did not reach them and where the yardsmen could not, by the structure of their bindings, particularly attend.

He turned.

*"What is it?"* Ezericht said. *"Having some last-minute reservations about this?"*

Edward did not, immediately, answer.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the convergence of the company at [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s [[Sahlen]] shack on the morning after the Veneficus contract negotiation: [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s return with the newly recruited [[Zant]]; [[Six]]'s arrival from his blood-rite; [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s warning shot in response to Trinity's insult; the revelation of Trinity's non-human aspect — the **blue-white glowing eyes, the cat-slit pupils, the pale markings along cheekbone and brow, and the small luminous horns at the forehead** — in the moment her temper failed her; the formal reading of the three Veneficus instruments (the *Absentis Consensio* mission contract, the *Crour Consensio* blood agreement, and the *Memoria Consensio* memory agreement); and the signing of the mission contract by [[Six]] and [[Cain]], unread.

The chapter closes on the moment Edward drew Ezericht aside behind the shack to propose what the chronicle understands to have been the first formal step toward a parallel arrangement existing outside the monastery's binding.

[[Snap]]'s morning hunt and the brief offer of a deer's blood to Six during the doorway exchange is preserved as the first canonical attestation of his hunting practice.

*Period source: the founding fragments preserved in The RP that Started it All, posts 116–140.*
