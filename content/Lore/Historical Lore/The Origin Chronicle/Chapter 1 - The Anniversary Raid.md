# Chapter 1 — The Anniversary Raid

> *In which the founding company first finds itself bound together by steel, in a city whose South Gate would not survive what was coming.*

---

## I. The Anniversary

It was the second raid that month, and the worst of the season was not yet upon them.

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] entered the tavern as he had on this same evening every year — by the side door, to his usual table, gesturing to the barkeep for the order the barkeep already knew. Three pints to begin with. More, depending on the weight of the evening.

The weight tonight was considerable. It was the anniversary of the death of the family who had taken him in — the family whose killers he had been hunting since, and whose killers he had not yet found. He drew his dagger from his belt and began to play with it across the back of his hand: not threat, not flourish, only the slow tactile rhythm of a man who could not bear to keep his hands still. He had not yet touched his first pint when the screaming began outside.

He did not, at first, rise. A small sigh — *Another raid? That's twice this month* — and only then did he stand.

He sheathed the dagger, drew one of the two swords he carried, and walked into the street.

---

## II. The Citizen

The northern quarter was already on fire when Ezericht stepped through the tavern door. He moved without urgency, as a man does when an emergency has become routine and the body has learned the shape of it. He had a particular ear for what was coming down a street before it arrived; the gift had saved him before.

He moved through an alley toward the noise.

At its mouth he caught the glimpse of a citizen — a slight, unarmed figure pressed back against a wall by a soldier in the colors of whoever was attacking the city this season. Ezericht did not slow. He came up behind the soldier and drove the blade in beneath the ribs. The soldier folded.

*"Go on, citizen,"* he said. *"Run home."*

The figure nodded, ducked under his arm, and was gone into the smoke.

Ezericht walked on.

---

## III. The Stranger in the Alley

He had not gone twenty paces when a sword crossed his chest from the dark.

A man stepped from a doorway he had not noticed and the blade came across at throat height — a stopping motion, not a cutting one. Ezericht stopped because he had to.

*"That way isn't the real problem,"* said the stranger. *"Head to the South Gate. I'll need that sharp blade of yours."*

He did not wait for an answer. He withdrew the blade, turned, and disappeared into the alleyway running south, moving with the practiced efficiency of a man who had measured every roof and every fire escape in [[Sahlen]] in advance.

Ezericht stood a moment in the smoke.

*Who is this guy? Should I even be trusting him?*

But the man had not been wrong about the city. The screaming from the north had thinned; the silence beneath it was the wrong kind of silence. The real attack would come from the south.

He sheathed the blade, gave chase, and ran.

He did not see the third figure on the roof above him — though the third figure saw him.

---

## IV. The One on the Rooftops

[[Six]] had been watching the city for some time before the raid began. What he had been waiting for was not yet clear to him, and would not be clear to him for some weeks, but he was patient and he had time.

When he saw the two strangers — the swordsman and the unknown man who had stopped him — break south through the alleys, Six made the decision to follow. He moved across the rooftops at first, descending into the alleys only when he could no longer maintain the angle, and made no sound that an ordinary ear would catch.

But Ezericht's ear was not ordinary. Halfway to the South Gate the footsteps behind him registered, and he stopped, turned, and drew his blade against the stranger from the rooftops.

*"Who are you!? And why are you following?"* he demanded, and when the answer did not come quickly enough, louder: *"I said WHO ARE YOU?!"*

Six considered him for a long moment, said only the one word — *"Six"* — and added, *"Now put your sword away."* He turned, walked back up the alley, and as he passed a body lying still in a doorway he raised it as a thrall and walked on, the thrall following at his heel like a hound.

He chose a different route to the gate.

---

## V. The South Gate

Edward — for that was the name of the stranger who had crossed Ezericht's path, though Ezericht did not yet know it — reached the South Gate two minutes ahead. He climbed to the wall-top, sheathed his broadsword, and strung his bow in one practiced motion. Below him on the road outside the city, a squad had begun its final sprint toward the gate: the leader heavy-armored, an axe across his back, shouting orders from the rear; the men around him faster, lighter, fanning into a charge.

Edward took the outliers first. By the time Ezericht reached the gate and Six arrived from his alternate alley, the leader's squad was close enough that they could be smelled.

*"Took ya long enough!"* Edward called down. *"Go after the squad leader — aim for the back! Other person — help me take care of the rest!"*

Ezericht drew his second blade.

What followed was loud and brief. Ezericht parried, dodged, drove one blade into a soldier and the second into the next; he saw a man come up behind Six and charged across the road to bury his blade through the man's chest before Six even turned. When the next wave came through the gate Ezericht sheathed one sword and took the other in both hands, and there came the moment — there always came the moment, with Ezericht — when arrogance outran capability and he found himself outnumbered.

Six saw it.

His thrall ran in, killed a man, and Six raised that man too. Then another, and another. The undead began to do the work of holding the line that Ezericht's two blades could no longer hold alone. Soldiers fell to thralls who had been their squadmates a heartbeat earlier; the squad began to disorder.

Edward, on the wall, saw the leader turn toward the disorder and saw his moment. He left the bow on the wall and dropped down into the road. He screamed at the squad leader to draw the attention, and when the leader swung the axe — slow with the weight of his armor — Edward dodged once, heard the axe thud into the ground and refuse to come out, and put his blade crosswise into the leader's back at the gap below the breastplate.

The squad broke.

Edward stood up, breathing hard, and looked at the two strangers picking their way back through the bodies.

*"Who the hell are you?"* he shouted at Six.

---

## VI. The Tavern

It was Ezericht who answered first, panting and cleaning his blades with a rag.

*"That's Six. I found him following us through the alley. Guess he's on our side."*

He cleaned both blades, sheathed them, and looked at Edward properly for the first time. *"Nice to meet you by the way. I'm Ezericht. But you, Six — how did you acquire those powers? And where did you come from? I've never seen you around here."*

*"Don't side me with you just yet,"* Six replied. *"I haven't chosen sides, and I won't until I know what the enemy is capable of. And as to my powers and where I'm from — that doesn't concern you. At least not yet."*

Edward closed the distance, sword still in hand.

*"Very well. But if you don't want questions, don't ask any. Ezericht — don't act surprised, you're hardly inconspicuous — watch your own bacon next time. The necro,"* he added, with a sneer Ezericht would come to recognize and Six would come to ignore, *"might not be there next time. Oh, and if your spawn get in my way, I will not distinguish between them and the people they once were."* He paused, looked at the gate, looked at the bodies, looked at the city. *"Let's get out of here before people show up and ask questions. The barkeep owes me a round."*

He turned and walked.

The other two, somewhat to their own surprise, followed.

---

## VII. Six Pints on the Table

The three of them entered the tavern by the same side door Ezericht had left through scarcely an hour before. Edward signalled the barkeep for a round and led them to the corner table, taking the seat with its back to the wall. The barman delivered six pints. Edward took one, leaned back, and drained it without breath before setting it aside and reaching for the next.

*"Alright,"* he said. *"I would like some answers — from both of you. I'm not going to pry, but the more information I get, the better."*

Ezericht drank from his pint and shrugged.

*"Ok, fine. I'll let you in on who I am. My name is Ezericht, as you already know. I live my life doing whatever I can to survive. My past is my own. I'll tell you if I ever feel like I should."*

He raised his glass to Edward. *"So, tell me about yourselves."*

Six set down his cup, lifted it again, and emptied its contents onto the floor of the tavern. He drew the cork from the base of the cross he wore and ran a slow stream of crimson liquid into the empty cup, recorked it, and took a sip. He drew his dagger and began cleaning his rather long fingernails.

*"I'm Six,"* he said, *"as Ezericht already told you back at the gate. I'm also a necromancer, as you already know. But besides that — I have nothing to tell you."* He glanced up. *"Now, you tell us something about yourself."*

Edward watched Six pour the ale onto the floor with the patient irritation of a man cataloguing future arguments. *"If you don't want to help, that's fine, but I make it a point to try to get to know my charges. Your protection is worth quite a shiny coin to someone. I don't know exactly who, and I don't rightly care."*

Ezericht set down his glass.

*"I've been keeping track of you for the last month,"* Edward continued, looking at him directly. *"Figured now was as good a time as any to show up."*

He turned his attention back to Six. *"As for you, Six — you could have asked for a glass instead of wasting the ale. And try not to make such a damned scene when you swap out your liquids. Discretion will make everyone's lives easier."*

He drank again. *"That said, I would like to know what kind of enchantments I am dealing with in battle with the two of you, so I don't accidentally trigger something."*

Ezericht finished his pint, looked into the empty bottom of the glass, and sighed.

*"Well — there really isn't anything all that special about me. I've learned to train my ear to be particularly sharp. It's always best to hear what's coming down the way. I'm an experienced herbist, in the making of honorable and not-so-honorable concoctions. Being around nature so much, I've learned to become in tune with it. Not necessarily able to speak with it — but I can feel something from it."*

He raised his hand for another pint.

*"Other than that, I'm just Ezericht."* He turned to Six and lifted an eyebrow. *"And as for you? What other things do you have up your sleeve?"*

Six considered the question, took a sip, and answered it with a quiet and unaffected precision that the other two would, in time, come to know as one of the only honest registers of his voice.

*"Anything else up my sleeve? Hmm. I suppose it's safe to tell you — I don't die. I suppose that's another trick I use to my advantage. But as to **why** I don't die — that I cannot say."*

He set the cup down and looked at Edward.

*"So. As I've asked before. You know about us. Who are **you**?"*

He took another sip.

*"Oh — and as to your questions about enchantments. I have none that you would be able to interfere with. So fret not about that."*

---

## VIII. The Door Opens Again

Edward was about to answer.

The tavern door opened, and a man none of them recognised — a man named [[Zant]] — walked to the barkeep, ordered two pints, took them to a table within earshot of the corner, and sat down.

He did not introduce himself.

He listened.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter reconstructs events later remembered as the founding meeting of [[Ezericht La'Monte]], [[Edward Z Zwarf]], and [[Six]] — held in the Mehrendt city of [[Sahlen]] on the anniversary of Ezericht's adopted family's death, during a raid on the South Gate. The arrival of [[Zant]] at the chapter's close marks the moment the company began to gather in earnest.
