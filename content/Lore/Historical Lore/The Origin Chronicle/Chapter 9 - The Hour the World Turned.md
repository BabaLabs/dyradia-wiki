# Chapter 9 — The Hour the World Turned

> *In which a monastery agent overhears a rumour in a tavern, a fire-brother burns down an inn for the fun of it, a water-brother arrives at a gate where weapons rise to meet him, an earth-brother feels a pulse against his wrist, and a cart returning from the Western Barracks is stopped by a wave of soil.*

---

## I. The Tavern at Dunmisk

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] had not slept since the dispersal.

She had been carried to the centre of the western part of Dyradia by the small paper instrument the order issued its field agents — a piece of parchment that, blown on with the correct concentration, transported its bearer to the place named on its face. The face she had blown on had named **Dunmisk**: a small town in the west of moderate poverty and immoderate criminality, where the monastery kept, at her last accounting, five undercover agents who held local watch.

She did not, on arrival, contact them.

She walked the town with her hood up and her face hidden, in the long unhurried pace of a woman accustomed to picking what she needed out of the noise of an unfamiliar room. After thirty minutes of walking she selected the tavern at the town's northern margin, took the corner farthest from the door, and listened.

It was the third conversation she listened in on that surfaced the thing she had been looking for.

Two half-drunk men sat at a near table. One leaned across to the other and dropped his voice to the register men used when they were proud of something they intended to be careful about.

*"…maybe not. Hey — have you heard the other rumour?"*

The second man scratched at his nose.

*"Rumour? About what?"*

*"About the —"* the first man paused, glanced about, and whispered *— the Dark Harbingers."*

The second man's face changed.

*"Shh. Do you want to end like a cut log?"*

*"I know what you mean. But it isn't so bad to get yourself killed."*

*"Then out with it."*

*"It's said that their leader knows about the King's whereabouts —"*

*"That's old news —"*

*"Shh. Let me finish. Also — that they are here on the west, and heading to a place near here."*

The second man considered this.

*"You're an idiot. Of course those are lies."*

The first leaned back. *"I was just saying what I heard, you old drunk. I'll —"*

Trinity stopped listening.

She did not, the chronicle notes, recognize the title *Dark Harbingers* as one she had been briefed on directly. She did, however, recognize it as the colloquial name for a cult the order had been observing for some years from a careful distance — a cult that had, by the order's last assessment, kept its operations sufficiently small not to require the order's direct attention.

If the cult's leader knew the King's whereabouts — and if the cult was, in fact, in the west — then the cult's distance from the order's attention had just ended.

She stood.

She left the tavern.

She did not, in the moment, know that the King — at exactly that hour, in the rune-sealed deep of the Western Barracks — was already dead.

She would, in the next several minutes, find out.

## II. The Burning Inn

[[Natas]] woke up in a bed in a hotel.

He did not remember which hotel.

He had fallen asleep, in his last clear recollection, on a bench in a park in the southern town he had been wandering since his father's recall, and he had — in some interval he was not, as he sat up, particularly interested in reconstructing — been put to bed by a stranger.

*Damnit. How the hell did I end up in this town?* he thought. *That's the last time I fall asleep on a bench in a park ever again.*

He stood. He shrugged out the stiffness. He went down the stairs and crossed the lobby. He had nearly reached the door when the clerk at the desk hurried out from behind it after him.

*"Sir! Sir, wait!"*

Natas turned.

*"You forgot your bill."*

Natas considered the clerk for a moment.

*"Oh. Did I now? Well — let's just keep that between us, shall we."*

He turned to go.

The clerk came out from behind the desk.

*"Sir — I'm afraid I can't let you leave without paying the bill. And if you continue to refuse, I'll have to inform the police."*

Natas considered him further.

*Wow,* he thought, *this guy is really determined.*

He shrugged.

*"So,"* he said. *"You want me to pay my bill for staying at a hotel, you say?"*

*"That's right."*

*"Hmm. I see."*

Natas spread his fingers at his side without drawing the gesture's attention.

*"Well — my question to you is: what hotel?"*

The hotel behind the clerk burst into flames.

The fire was Natas's element. It was not, however, an ordinary fire. He had summoned, at the gesture his hand had made, a small fire-golem of his own composition, and the golem had taken up residence in the rafters of the hotel's upper floor and was, by the time the clerk fully registered the change in temperature, already burning the entire structure down from the inside out. The clerk turned to look. The clerk's face did the particular complicated thing that a face does when it has just lost the building behind it.

Natas walked away.

He had taken perhaps a dozen steps down the street when he became aware that the running figures around him — people heading toward the fire — were beginning, by sound, to scream.

He turned his head, just slightly.

The screams resolved into the sound of human beings inside a burning building.

*Hmm. Maybe I shouldn't have left that fire-golem behind.*

He kept walking.

*Nah. I'm sure the smart ones will be fine.*

A new sequence of screams reached him.

*Of course. I've been wrong before.*

He shrugged.

He saw, ahead of him, the figure of a hooded woman stepping out of a tavern. She paused on the threshold to look back at the smoke behind him. She was not, by her bearing, an ordinary townswoman.

She might, he thought, know something.

He crossed to her at a half-run and put his hand on her shoulder before she had finished turning.

*"Excuse me — but do you know anything about my brother Six?"*

## III. The Monastery Gate

The eastern gate of the [[Hypostasis Monastery]] was a gate that, by the order's general practice, did not open without warning.

A monk named **Medinas** had the gate watch that afternoon. He had been at his post for the better part of two hours when he heard the knock — a slow steady knocking of the kind a man who did not know the gate's customs would have given. He approached the door and opened the small narrow view-window.

What he saw outside it was not a man.

The shape on the other side of the gate had the structure of a man and the features of a man, but the chronicle records — and Medinas's later report would corroborate — that there was an unmistakable wrongness in the eye and in the carriage and in the faint salt-cool of the air around the figure that no ordinary mortal could have brought to the gate of a desert-edge monastery on a still afternoon.

Medinas closed the view-window.

He turned and stopped Calibar, a guard who had been walking past in the corridor.

*"There is someone outside,"* Medinas said. *"Not human. Alert the rest, and tell them to follow procedure. Hurry."*

Calibar ran.

Medinas returned to the gate. Within perhaps ninety seconds the walls and towers of the gate-yard were lined with bowmen and the corridor behind him with weapon-bearing monks. He looked around once, drew the bolt back, and opened the door of the gate.

The figure in the doorway was, the chronicle understands, [[Marxus]].

*"What would be your business with the monastery, creature?"*

Marxus did not answer immediately.

He looked at the bowmen on the wall. He looked at the monks in the corridor. He took the measure of the gathered force with the unhurried interior arithmetic of a water-elemental who had performed the same arithmetic at many gates of many institutions over many years.

*"Hmm,"* he said, eventually. *"A little paranoid, are we?"*

He smiled, a bit.

*"You don't have enough firepower here to defeat me — and if you were to try, I'd kill all of you."*

He let that settle.

*"But back to the business at hand. I came to ask about a mission I heard was going on involving my brother. I was hoping to get some information and maybe sign on to help out with it, if that's okay with you monks."*

He looked back at Medinas.

*"Tell me about this mission. Or let me sign up, and then tell me about it. It's up to you entirely."*

He walked past the monk into the corridor.

The bowmen on the wall did not, on Medinas's small gestured signal, fire.

The chronicle records, of the moment, the precise nature of the monastery's discipline: that the bowmen lowered their weapons not because the threat had ended but because their captain had judged the cost of the strike to exceed the cost of letting the creature walk past. Marxus reached the inner corridor unhindered.

He was shown, by a frightened novice, to a room where his brother had — by the monastery's records — never been; given a bed he had no intention of using; and told, in language that did its best to sound deferential, to please remain in his room until the senior of the order could speak with him in the morning.

The chronicle records, of the rest of his evening at the monastery, only this:

That the next morning Marxus would be informed that the mission he had come about had ended — by the death of the King and the burning of the contract — and that he would respond by drowning the novice who had been assigned to his comfort in a sphere of conjured water in the corridor outside his door.

This, however, the chronicle defers to its next chapter.

## IV. The Pulse

Far south of the Western Barracks, deep in the southern forest where the road from his father's swamp-castle crossed an unnamed river, [[Six]] was walking.

He was walking, the chronicle records, without particular destination. He had not yet returned to the search his father had told him to abandon and had not yet committed himself to the search his father had given him in its place. He had been thinking — about the strange-blood creature whose arm he had taken in the Sahlen alley, about the dagger Trinity had been carrying that morning that he had not asked her to identify, about the King he had been hired to find and the question whether the creature with the four-foot blade had been connected to him.

He felt a pulse against his wrist.

The pulse was the chain-bracelet [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] had given him at the contract signing.

It pulsed once. It pulsed again.

The chronicle records, of the moment, the specific texture of the pulse. It was not the distress signal Trinity had warned them not to use. It was a different and slower pulse — the pulse the chain made when its binding-purpose had been resolved, by the completion or the breaking of the contract that had originally tied it together.

Six stopped walking.

He stared at the bracelet for a long moment.

He understood, in that moment, several things in quick succession.

The first was that the mission the contract had bound them to had ended.

The second was that — given the cadence and the warmth of the pulse — it had ended not by completion but by the breaking of the central condition: the King's safe being.

The third was that, somewhere in Dyradia in the last hour, the King had been killed.

He raised a hand and reached, by the telepathic link he shared with his brothers, for the brother nearest to the place he had directed by his father's earlier briefing.

The link found [[Zin]].

*"Zin,"* Six said into the link. *"Get back to that place where that stranger was. Keep them from leaving that place."*

Zin's voice — slow, patient, slightly delayed — came back across the link.

*"But why, brother? Aren't they on your side?"*

*"Don't ask questions you idiot. Just do as I tell you."*

A pause.

*"Alright, brother. Am I allowed to use force to prevent them from leaving?"*

*"Of course. Just don't kill them. Only restrain them."*

*"Alrighty, then. I shall head off to prevent them from leaving."*

*"Get there as soon as possible. Use every manner of your powers to get there."*

*"Okay. Then I shall be off to restrain. Now. Talk to you when you get here."*

Six closed the link.

He stood by the river for a long moment.

Then he began walking again. Not at his ordinary pace.

## V. The Farm

[[Zin]] had been walking the road [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] had directed him to for several hours when he came in sight of the small, secluded farm at its end.

The farm was an ordinary farm.

It had a small farmhouse, a vegetable patch, an old slatted barn, a stone-walled well, and a stooped white-haired old man tending the carrots in the patch as Zin approached. The chronicle, with the particular reticence of an observer documenting a thing it does not wish to document, observes that none of these features matched the description of *a farm holding a fugitive* that Edward had given him.

Zin had taken the directions in good faith.

He knocked at the door of the farmhouse, because the old man at the carrots had not yet noticed him, and after a moment the old man came around the corner of the house with the dirt of his work still on his palms.

*"Well, hello there, sonny. How can I help you?"*

Zin considered him.

*"You don't look like a criminal."*

The old man laughed — a long, hearty laugh, the kind of laugh that came from a man who had spent many years in his vegetable patch and had not, in those years, had occasion to be addressed in the language of accusation.

*"Ho, ho. A criminal, you say? I'm no criminal. I just sit out here on this farm and tend my vegetables."*

Zin's brow furrowed.

*"But the stranger said you were a criminal. And that you were interfering with my brother's mission. And that I was supposed to kill you."*

The old man stopped laughing.

*"Now, ho —"*

He did not finish his sentence.

Zin had moved his right hand at his hip with the small habitual gesture by which he hardened the flesh of his fist into a fist of stone, and the stone-fist had crossed the small distance between them and met the old man's sternum at the speed of a man who had been doing the same work for, by his patient brother's calendar, much longer than any mortal carrot-tender had been alive. The old man's chest gave way. The old man went down.

Zin stood over the body for a moment.

He did not, the chronicle observes, derive satisfaction from the act. He had been told the farm was a fugitive's farm; he had been told the fugitive interfered with his brother's mission; he had not, in his temperament, been built to second-guess the senior brother whose mission the orders had been given to support.

He raised his other hand.

He summoned a mass of earth out of the ground around the farm and let it flow over the entire property — over the farmhouse, over the carrot-patch, over the well, over the body. The farm became, by the rule of his element, a small low hill of soil with no clear features.

He turned to walk away.

The pulse came across the link from Six.

He listened to the orders. He frowned. He set off, at a pace his temperament was not designed for, back the way he had come.

## VI. The Wave at the Barracks

The chronicle records the form Zin's return took: a flowing wave of earth at the level of a small horse's shoulder, with Zin riding the wave's crest with the unhurried balance of a man who had learned to surf his own element before his temperament had been complete.

He arrived in the country surrounding the Western Barracks shortly after [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s cart had pulled away from it.

He came down off the wave. He let it settle behind him. He looked at the soldiers riding rear-guard at the back of the cart and decided, by his own brother's instruction, that the soldiers were not the primary targets and therefore could be removed.

He stepped in front of them.

*"So,"* he said. *"Do you guys work with that stranger that sent me off to the farm?"*

The lead grunt of the three at the rear-guard looked at his fellows.

*"Hey, guys. You think he's talking about Z?"*

The second grunt rolled his eyes.

*"Who else would he be talking about, you moron? Of course that's who he's talking about."*

The third sighed.

*"You're retarded."*

Zin clarified, patiently.

*"So you guys do work for him?"*

*"Yeah. Why?"*

*"Then you die."*

The earth opened beneath them.

It closed over them.

The chronicle records — without particular comment — that for trained soldiers, the three at the rear-guard had not, in the moment that mattered, behaved like trained soldiers.

Zin walked on.

Ahead, the cart was moving at the strained speed of a vehicle that had been overloaded for fast riding. Zin raised his hands. A new wave of earth rose ahead of the horses — sudden, dense, the height of a small wall — and the horses, on meeting it, stumbled, fell backward, and panicked. The cart slewed sideways. The men around it tried to spread out and discovered that the ground beneath their feet was opening to receive them in low restraint-mounds that wrapped their legs to mid-thigh and held them in place.

A handful of the soldiers ran. They escaped.

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] did not escape, because the restraint-mound that closed around him had been one of the precise ones and had been formed by a Zin who had been told to take Edward specifically.

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] did not escape, for the same reason.

Zin walked among the captured.

*"My brother told me to capture you without causing too much harm,"* he said to Edward and Ezericht, the way a man might apologize for an inconvenience. *"So — are you guys comfortable?"*

A grunt in a nearby restraint piped up.

*"No, actually, I'm not all that comfortable —"*

Zin glanced at him.

*"I don't recall asking you."*

The grunt vanished into the earth. The mound that had held him closed neatly over the place he had been and did not, for the chronicle's purposes, return him.

Zin looked back at Edward and Ezericht.

*"I'll bring him back. If he doesn't die."*

## VII. The Fun Ride

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] did the rapid arithmetic of a man whose mood was the loudest single thing inside his head and whose face had been trained, from very young, not to communicate any of it.

*Damn it all. How the hell do you kill something like that?*

He let the thought finish.

*At least he's stupid. Maybe I can take this to my advantage. He hasn't noticed that neither Ezericht nor I are completely encased. Thank you, amulet.*

He raised his hands within the restraint, found a small ledge of rock at chest level just below the top of the mound, and gripped it.

*Now to think of a trick to let us get away from this guy.*

He looked at Zin with an expression he had perfected over a long career of dealing with people he wished to underestimate themselves.

*"HEY!"* he called. *"That's a nifty little trick you did there. Can you just raise the earth — or do you lower it too?"*

Zin turned. He looked at Edward with the slow patient interest of a man who had not, in a long time, been asked a question whose answer he was proud of.

*"I can control earth completely,"* he said. *"And if I need to, I can make it as well. I'd show you — but if I did something stupid and you got away, Six would kill me. And then bring me back. And then kill me again. Probably for several days."*

He looked at the cloud above the road.

He stayed there for a long moment.

*"Well — in that case,"* Edward said, with the same carefully calibrated brightness, *"I really want to see some fun stuff. Could you take this enclosure I'm in and put it like twenty feet up in the air and then take it back down really quickly? It'd be like a fun ride."*

Zin came back to himself.

*"Hmmm. I suppose just this one time it wouldn't hurt. But I'm still not sure of this. If you were to get away — well, I already explained that, but yeah. Hmm. But that does seem like it'd be fun."*

He returned, briefly, to the cloud.

*"Alrighty. I suppose I could do it just this once."*

He raised his hand.

The earth around Edward shot upward in a column. It carried Edward — gripping the ledge of rock he had braced himself against — twenty feet into the air. It paused, briefly, at the height of its summit. It dropped.

Edward let go.

He fell free of the falling column at perhaps fifteen feet above the ground. He tucked his shoulder, hit the grass with the practiced roll of a man who had practiced exactly this drop a number of times before, and came up on his feet with the broadsword in his hand. He crossed the distance to one of the injured horses at the cart's flank in three strides. He vaulted onto its back. He turned its head.

He rode west, toward the base.

He did not look back.

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] — still trapped — watched him go with the surprised relief of a man who had expected to be left behind and was, in some small part of himself, glad to have been.

*Looks like it's seen better days,* he thought, when his gaze settled on a second injured horse still standing in the chaos of the panicked rear-guard, *but it'll get me out of here.*

He shifted his weight against his own restraint. His own restraint, the chronicle reminds, had been formed by the same Zin who had formed Edward's; Ezericht's was, in the same particular way, not quite the full enclosure Zin had formed around the lower-priority grunts.

He pushed.

He freed his right arm.

He freed his shoulder.

He pulled himself out of the restraint with the small uncomplicated motion of a man slipping out of a coat that had not quite fit him to begin with, and crossed to the second injured horse at a run.

He mounted.

He rode east.

*I hope Z gets away safely. I have to find reinforcements to help him out. I must ride to the city fast.*

He did not, in the moment, intend to ride toward [[Six]].

He would, in the next hour, find him anyway.

## VIII. The Stranger Who Was Not the Stranger

Zin watched Edward ride west. He watched Ezericht ride east. He stood for a long moment in the slow afternoon light of the country outside the Western Barracks. He considered the two riders and the directions they had taken and the order in which Six had asked him to restrain them.

His judgement, the chronicle records, was characteristic.

He turned west.

Six's voice came across the link — sharper now, less patient.

*"Go after the one in the cart, Zin. The other one will not be a problem."*

Zin began to run.

He could not, by the speed his element gave him, keep pace with the horse Edward had taken. He could, the chronicle records, keep pace with the road. He set out after the western rider with the slow patient determination of an earth-brother who had been told, by the senior of his line, to do a thing he would not, by his own temperament, have chosen — and would, by the rule of his obedience, see done.

The chronicle leaves him on the road.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter braids four parallel threads occurring across Dyradia in the hour surrounding the regicide of King [[Alaric La'Monte]]:

1. **[[Trinity Stolen]] at [[Dunmisk]]** — overhearing, in the town's tavern, two drunkards' rumour that the **Dark Harbingers** cult had moved to the west and that its leader knew the King's whereabouts.
2. **[[Natas]] at an unnamed southern town** — burning the hotel he had spent the night in (and a number of the people inside it) by means of a fire-golem of his own composition, and approaching the hooded woman emerging from the town's tavern to ask after his brother [[Six]].
3. **[[Marxus]] at the [[Hypostasis Monastery]]** — arriving at the east gate, observed by the monk **Medinas** to be non-human, addressed by the monastery's standard procedure for such cases, and admitted past the bowmen on the wall by the captain's assessment that the cost of the strike exceeded the cost of letting him walk past.
4. **[[Six]] in the southern forest** — feeling the contract-completion pulse of [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s chain-bracelet, understanding that the King had been killed, and ordering his earth-brother [[Zin]] to detain the company at the Western Barracks.

The chapter further records [[Zin]]'s incidental killing of an old farmer at the southeastern farm to which [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] had misdirected him; Zin's subsequent arrival at the Western Barracks; the wave of earth that brought the cart down; [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s *fun-ride* gambit by which he tricked Zin into ejecting him from the restraint-mound at speed; and [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s separate escape eastward.

The *Dark Harbingers* — established here as a cult Trinity's order had been observing at a distance, and whose leader was rumoured to hold knowledge of the King — are introduced into the chronicle for the first time and reserved for later development.

*Period source: the founding fragments preserved in The RP that Started it All, posts 173–178, 184–192. (The brief vignette by a transient observer arriving in dog form has been omitted from the chronicle as immaterial to the unfolding narrative.)*
