# Chapter 11 — The Vanished Friend

> *In which an heir grabs a stone he was never meant to touch, a commander grabs a pickaxe and remembers a fire he once fought, a necromancer learns that the contract he signed was undone by a blade older than the contract itself, and a man recognizes, in the relic of another man's vanished childhood friend, the work of his own long-experimenting father.*

---

## I. The Audacity

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] rode east along the road until he was perhaps two miles from the Western Barracks. He stopped twice to listen — once for the sound of pursuit, once for the sound of a horse that was not his — and rode on.

He had been told that morning that he was a son of a King.

He had been told, that evening, that the man who had taught him to hold a sword had been a commander in the Mehrendt military all along. He had been told that the protective bracelets he and the commander wore between them were the only things keeping them out of the reach of the order he had nearly contracted to. He had been told, with the same evening's light still on his face, that the creature now alone at the base behind him was a thing he was probably not equipped to fight.

He had not, until perhaps three minutes before, been able to think clearly about any of it.

What had cleared his head was a small flicker of motion on a low ridge to the south of the road.

He saw, on the ridge, a figure walking — alone, unhurried, not from the direction of the base. The figure had Six's stride.

Ezericht turned the horse south.

He rode up the ridge at speed. He did not slow as he came within hailing distance. Twenty paces from the figure he jumped from the horse, hit the grass with the less practiced version of the same shoulder-roll Edward had performed an hour earlier outside the cart-yard, and came up on his feet running. Twelve paces from the figure he had begun to yell.

*"SIX! I need you to tell your brother to stop! You don't understand what is happening!"*

The figure turned.

It was [[Six]].

He had the expression of a man who had not been expecting anyone to come at him in the manner of the figure approaching, and the chronicle records that — in the small uncalibrated heartbeat between his turning and his decision — Six's body had begun the rotation a body begins when its owner has decided to defend itself.

Ezericht dashed the last paces.

He grabbed at Six's neck.

He closed his hand around the small dark stone that hung from a cord at the inside of Six's collar, and tore the cord from its loop in a single uncalibrated motion before Six's defensive rotation had completed.

He stepped back with the stone in his fist.

Six's hand went to his chest. The hand stopped at the place the stone had hung. It pressed against the empty space with the abrupt panicked reassurance of a man checking for an organ he had until that moment not had cause to think about.

The stone was, the chronicle reminds, [[Six]]'s **soul stone**.

Ezericht held it out at arm's length.

He spoke very fast.

*"I need your full cooperation in this for a few minutes — without you doing unnecessary things — "*

He saw Six's face begin to do a thing he did not have a name for.

*" — what? Do I really look that dumb? I know what this thing is. And if you want to get it back in a single piece, which I'm sure you do, you will listen for just a minute."*

He took two further steps back. The stone stayed at arm's length.

*God,* he thought, *I hope this goes how it did in my mind.*

---

## II. The Poison

It did not.

Ezericht had not known — and the chronicle suspects, in retrospect, that he could not have known — that the small dark stone hanging at the inside of Six's collar had been defended by its bearer against any contact that was not Six's own.

Six's defense, the chronicle records, was a slow oily film of liquid contact-paralytic that lived on the surface of the stone and seeped, on contact, through any skin that closed against it. The defense did not kill. It paralyzed. It paralyzed, by Six's later description, in a manner that did not deaden the body's capacity to feel: the bearer of the touch went still while continuing, with cruel completeness, to experience.

Ezericht's hand had been closed around the stone for perhaps eight seconds.

It was, by that measure, longer than the defense required.

He felt the paralysis begin in his palm. He felt it move up his forearm. He felt it close his elbow into its raised position. He felt his other arm — which had still been gesturing — slow and stop. He felt his hips lock. He felt his right leg refuse to make the small forward step it had been about to make.

He stayed where he stood.

He could not, by that point, move.

He could, however, see, hear, and speak.

His face, with what little control it had retained, did the small thing a face does when its owner has realized he is in a situation he had not, in advance, computed.

---

## III. The Plea

He spoke anyway.

He spoke in the clear hurried register of a man who had perhaps thirty seconds before the next thing went wrong and who had not, in his earlier rehearsal of this moment, allowed for an audience that was no longer obliged to listen.

*"I don't know how hard this will be to believe — but I'm an heir to the throne."*

Six did not move.

*"The King was kept in a place where only those of royal blood could enter — so I could kill him. The monastery was planning all along to slowly take political control over the kingdom. And the only way to get the current King out of the contract was to end his life."*

He met Six's eyes.

*"You see — we were being set up. All along. They wanted us to find the King so that they could have him in their possession — have the tides in their favour."*

He took the next breath, the chronicle records, very carefully.

*"I know you don't take sides. And I'm not asking you to. I'm not telling you to fight them — but don't fight me. If you would, though — I could really use you at my side."*

He held the stone out toward Six.

He could not, by that point, have closed his hand against the stone if he had been asked to. The stone rested against his locked palm. The chronicle understands the gesture, though it could not be willed, to have been the most that was available to him.

---

## IV. The Link

[[Six]] considered him.

The chronicle records that Six debated — for a length of time that would not, on a sensitive instrument, have registered as significant but was, for Six, unusually long — whether to kill Ezericht for what he had just done.

No one, in Six's recollection, had ever had the audacity to touch the stone before.

He did not, however, kill Ezericht.

He crossed the distance between them at his usual unhurried pace. He reached up and lifted the stone gently from Ezericht's locked palm. The film of paralytic on its surface did not, by some agreement Six had with it, touch his own skin.

He restored the stone to the cord. He restored the cord around his neck.

He looked at Ezericht.

*"Stop my brother? [[Zin]]?"*

He pursed his lips, briefly.

*"He's harmless. Well — compared to the others, he's harmless."*

He turned the stone in the fingers of one hand.

*"And if you knew my weakness — you know his. We're all the same. Although some of us defend the gem differently. He covers his with stone, if I'm not mistaken. And I cover mine in a poison which seeps through the skin of those who come in contact with it. It doesn't kill — but I tell you, paralysis is hell when you can still feel everything."*

He raised the other hand to his temple.

*"But — since you were either brave or stupid enough to pull what you just pulled — I'll stop him for you."*

He closed his eyes. He reached, by the telepathic link he shared with his brothers, for [[Zin]].

The chronicle records the link's content. Six said, into Zin's mind:

*"Stop attacking. Now."*

In the western country a moment earlier, Zin had been crushing several soldiers under a slow advancing wedge of stone. He stopped. He turned his head in a direction the chronicle understands to have been Six's direction, though he could not see across the country between them.

*"Yes, brother. I understand."*

Zin began, with the patient unhurried thoroughness of an earth-brother who had been told to undo what he had just done, to return the stone he had moved into the walls he had broken. The walls began to reform.

Six opened his eyes.

He looked at Ezericht.

*"You should be able to move again in a couple of minutes. I'm going to go check on Mr. Edward now. You enjoy yourself."*

He turned and walked back down the ridge.

*"And think really hard on what you learned today."*

He did not look back.

---

## V. The Pickaxe

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] had spent the same several minutes, the chronicle records, executing a plan that — as it would turn out — was no longer necessary.

He had run from the officer's library into the hallway that split off to the deep tunnels with the strong intention of doing the next clever thing his training had prepared him to do, and he had found, in the corridor's clutter of dropped tools, the answer he had been looking for.

*ROCKS,* the idea struck him with the clarity of a lightning bolt. *Why didn't I think of this earlier?*

He scooped a pickaxe from the floor.

He turned. He ran out of the tunnels at the same speed he had run into them. The whole place was a chaotic mess: some soldiers running for their lives in directions the geometry of the building did not support; others trying to drag fallen comrades clear of the wall the wave had broken; a number of the wounded simply lying where the impact had left them. He strode into the centre of the yard and stopped twenty paces from where [[Zin]] was, by that point, unhurriedly replacing the stone he had moved.

Edward fell into stance with the pickaxe in his right hand.

*"State your business, creature, or perish!"*

The challenge — the chronicle preserves it because it was preserved — echoed off what remained of the walls.

Zin looked up at him.

The chronicle preserves, equally, the small unimpressed tilt of Zin's head as he did so.

*"I'm doing what my brother told me to do,"* Zin said. *"And right now I'm fixing the walls I broke."*

He gestured, mildly. Some of the earth at Edward's feet flowed in a low orderly stream out from beneath the pickaxe-bearing man and joined a section of broken wall behind him.

*"My brother will be here soon to speak with you on the matter further."*

He turned to address the next piece of broken wall.

*"I honestly don't know what's going on. So — waiting for him would probably be the best idea for you."*

Edward stood, pickaxe at the ready, in the centre of the courtyard, with the slow embarrassed deflation of a man whose carefully timed dramatic entrance had been received not with the engagement he had prepared for but with the patient distracted civility of a man trying to finish a chore.

He let the pickaxe drop, just slightly.

He waited.

---

## VI. The Stand-Down

[[Six]] arrived through the courtyard's eastern gate perhaps a quarter-hour later.

He had taken his time. The walk south to the base had given him the chance to think, which he had used; the conversation with Ezericht on the ridge had given him the cause to do so, which he had not had earlier in the day. He came up behind [[Zin]] in the courtyard and laid his hand briefly on his brother's shoulder.

*"Good work for distracting them for me. Go find something to entertain yourself while I speak to Z here."*

Zin nodded the small acknowledging nod of an obedient temperament. He walked off a short distance, sat down on a low piece of broken wall, and resumed staring at the clouds.

Six crossed the yard to Edward.

He stopped at the same twenty paces Edward had been holding.

*"Now,"* he said. *"Let's discuss what's going on here, shall we?"*

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] lowered the pickaxe the rest of the way. He set it against his hip.

He breathed.

*"Well — it is very simple, really."*

He gestured for Six to walk with him toward the main building. Six walked.

---

## VII. The Truth of the Dagger

The chronicle records [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s explanation to [[Six]] in compressed form, because Edward gave it in the corridors of the base and at the writing-desk of the officer's library and in the doorway of the command room and in pieces of varying length whose precise order the chronicle has not preserved.

The substance was this.

The Gheronda — and through him the monastery he kept — had been moving, for some unrecorded number of years, to install a theocratic state in [[Mehrendt]]. The instrument of the installation had been a slow contract bound on King [[Alaric La'Monte]], by which the King would gradually transfer his legislative authority into the monastery's hands across a horizon of ten years. The people close to the King had noticed the change in his behaviour. They had inquired. They had eventually located the only loophole in the contract.

Edward made, with the heel of his hand, the small unmistakable motion of a blade across his own neck.

*"The only problem,"* he continued, *"being that the Gheronda had ordered spells to be placed on the King to prevent him from harm. But — could not prevent him from harming himself."*

He let that hang.

*"This was taken care of in the contract. And after the 'untimely demise' — "*

He shuddered. The chronicle preserves the shudder.

*" — of what the order believed were all the legitimate heirs, the contract was, by the order's reckoning, secure. The hidden son was the only remaining instrument. He was nowhere to be found."*

He stopped at a long polished table in the command room and gestured for Six to sit.

Six sat.

*"When the power shift was about to take an irreparable point,"* Edward continued, *"we finally devised a solution. I was to find the dagger — which should enable me to find the son. During that time we had to kidnap the King ourselves, to prevent him from doing irreparable damage."*

He sat across from Six.

*"This dagger,"* he said, *"was a relic from darker times. Times when men were bound in contracts for as long as their souls resided in their bodies, and necromancy was commonplace among the ones doing the contracting."*

He laid his hands flat on the table.

*"Some of these poor men were bound until their usefulness was ended or their master's was. A few powerful people of the age — too few, in the end, but a few — had decided to be able to free these men from their bindings. As this was the army that was unkillable, unstoppable, and rumbling toward them at an uneasy pace."*

He took a slow breath.

*"The dagger is meant to completely release the soul from the body and never allow it to return."*

He looked at Six.

*"What this means for you — "* he reached into his coat and drew out the contract Six had signed at [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s shack — *" — is that your contract is now completely — "*

He ripped the parchment in half.

*" — null and void."*

He set the two halves of the parchment on the table.

*"So. You know the story from there. And now you have a choice. You can help us in the ensuing war between Church and state — or you can ally yourself with the very same people who wish to exterminate you and your kind."*

He waited.

*"This is going to be a very large war. Neutrality almost certainly means death. Even for you."*

He stood up and extended his hand across the table.

*"So. What do you say?"*

---

## VIII. The Map

Six did not, immediately, take Edward's hand.

He yawned, performatively, and stretched, performatively. The chronicle records — without explanation — that the chronicle's understanding of Six is that the performance of human comforts was, for Six, a thing he sometimes did to entertain himself.

*"Well,"* he said. *"I'll have to discuss this with my brothers before I can come to a decision. But — I see them siding with you for some reason."*

He tilted his head, slightly.

*Trinity's magic.* The chronicle understands the tilt to have been Six registering, at the back of his perception, the active spellwork [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] was performing at the southern end of the underground.

*"So — my contract with them is void now? That's pretty nice. But isn't it supposed to burn or something?"*

Edward did not answer immediately. He had crossed to the wall of scrolls behind the command desk.

*"I don't know what those are supposed to do when they have expired. That's not my area of expertise."*

He pulled a large rolled map down from the wall.

*"Ah ha!"*

He unrolled the map across the long polished table.

The map was a working strategy map of the kingdom of Dyradia. It bore — at every significant intersection of road and river — small inked notations identifying the **monastery's outposts**, their assessed garrison strength, and the order's local political reach. Some cities had been marked in red. Some had been marked in green. Some had been marked in three concentric circles whose meaning Six did not, at first, know.

*"This is our primary strategy map,"* Edward explained. *"Outlining enemy strongholds and forces, along with the cities we need to look for that would no longer be loyal to the monarchy — either by general consensus or the ruling class, or by fear because of proximity to the monastery's outposts."*

He laid his finger on one of the three-circle markings.

*"This map, as well as the notes in these books and scrolls, are kept in each major outpost still loyal to the kingdom. They contain all the information we have on the order's magic, on its structure, on who we need to watch for in the upcoming struggle — both on and off the battlefield. Spy profiles. Generals. Commanders. Even the bigger merchants they get their supplies from."*

He met Six's eyes.

*"I assure you — I would not make war without testing the water first. Is this prepared enough for you?"*

---

## IX. The Brothers Gather

Six stared at the map for a long moment.

The chronicle records that what Six was doing in those moments was not simply reading the map but *transmitting* it — by the telepathic link he shared with his brothers — to all four of them simultaneously.

When he had memorized the lines and the notations to his own standard, he looked up.

*"That may be enough. But I'm sure they have secrets that neither you, nor your spies, may have access to. They have been around for quite some time, after all."*

While he had been transmitting, two of his brothers had been moving toward the base.

[[Nazu]] had, the chronicle records, dropped out of the sky into the courtyard outside, in the silent unannounced manner he preferred for such arrivals: a cloud above the base had begun, without obvious cause, to split — first into two, then into more — and Zin, sitting on his broken wall, had jumped at the first split and then calmed at the second when he had realized only one person could split clouds in that exact way. Nazu had descended through the splittings, asked Zin what was going on, ignored Zin's answer, declared his intention to sleep for a bit, and instructed Zin not to touch him on the grounds that the rotating winds around his sleeping form were sharper than any ordinary blade.

That, at least, was the chronicle's understanding of the conversation that had taken place in the courtyard.

Both brothers walked into the command room behind Six perhaps a minute later.

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] looked up.

He took the measure of the man in green who had not, by any door he could see, entered the room — and of the man in brown who walked at his shoulder — without comment.

Six did not stand.

He nodded to the brothers.

*"The one on my left, in the green, is [[Nazu]]. You've already met [[Zin]] here, so I won't bother introducing him."*

He propped his feet up on the table.

*"So,"* he said, *"how exactly do you plan to wage war on the church? Something tells me they probably have some archaic weapon of mass destruction or something up their sleeves. Who knows — maybe even the dragons of old. Or even more interesting, maybe they'll pull a Behemoth out of somewhere. That'd be insane."*

Edward did not, in his face, register the joke.

He folded his arms over the map.

*"We have prepared for many things."*

---

## X. The Blood-Red Sky

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s paralysis faded, by the chronicle's reckoning, perhaps fifteen minutes after [[Six]] had walked off the ridge.

He spent the first two minutes after movement returned testing his fingers against his palm. He spent the next two minutes drawing, from a small inner pocket of his coat, a piece of special parchment he had not, in the chronicle's earlier record, been documented to be carrying.

He pressed the parchment against the small black mark Six's poison had left on his palm — a residue of the contact-defense which had remained on his skin after the paralysis cleared — and watched the parchment absorb a thin sample of the residue into its weave.

He folded the parchment back into his pocket.

*I should have known he had some kind of defense for his gem,* he thought. *Good thing I got a small amount off of it in time. I can further examine what he used exactly. I would rather not be paralyzed again.*

He recovered the horse. He turned it back toward the Western Barracks.

He rode at the steady pace of a man who needed to arrive but did not need to arrive hurried.

The sun had begun to set by the time the base came into sight. The chronicle records, of the sky over the western country that evening, a colour that the chronicler has not been able to find a parallel for in any sky the chronicler has otherwise documented. The colour was not the ordinary deep orange of a late evening, nor the soft pink of a clear summer's, nor the violet of a stormbreak. The colour was **red** — a deep arterial red the chronicle is comfortable calling *blood-coloured* because no more comfortable word presents itself.

Ezericht looked up at the sky as he rode the last quarter-mile.

He knew, looking at it, that the conflict had only just begun.

He arrived at the base. He passed the broken wall section that was, by the time of his arrival, half-restored by [[Zin]]'s patient handiwork. He walked into the building. He followed the sound of voices to the command room.

He saw Six and Edward at the table.

*At least Six decided to not kill us,* he thought. *Perhaps he will side with us.*

He stepped into the room.

---

## XI. The Relic

He interrupted the conversation.

*"Fill me in with the rest later. For now — what is our plan of action? I suggest we plan something on the covert side."*

He addressed Edward.

*"I know for a fact that the monastery has a strong military side as well. I'm sure you remember the Holy Crusades back some twenty years ago. What I bet you don't know is that half of the men that were sent in were monks from the monastery themselves. The regular men hired for that was just a cover-up — I'm sure — to lead no suspicion to the hidden campaign the church had."*

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] inclined his head, considering. The information was new to him, and the chronicle records that Edward filed it.

Ezericht turned to [[Six]].

He took a long pause.

He sighed.

*"I really would like to apologize about just up and snatching your gem. I know of what great importance they are to you. I just needed a sure-fire way to get your attention."*

Six did not change expression.

*"I bet you're wondering how I knew about it, though. It's simple, really."*

Ezericht drew, from a pocket of his coat, an object the chronicle has not previously had cause to record: a small, semi-transparent, dark-grey stone with — at its precise centre — a small five-pointed star of a deep dark red. The star caught what little light there was in the command room. The grey of the stone around it did not.

*"I've met someone of your type before. He lived in the town when I was growing up. We were pretty good friends — even though I never was told his name. He always said 'In the end, you'll find it out anyways.'"*

He turned the stone slowly in his fingers.

*"One day he just up and vanished. And the only relic I have of him is this."*

He held it out toward Six.

*"I wasn't going to say anything until I felt I could trust you. But something is telling me you are supposed to see this. Does it mean anything to you?"*

---

## XII. The Vanished Friend

Six did not move, at first.

The chronicle records, of his face in the moment, the smallest small registering of recognition — the registering of a man who had seen, in a stranger's hand, a thing made by his own father's earlier and lesser experiments — and the careful flattening of expression by which he absorbed the registering before it could be read.

He glanced, sideways, at his brothers.

The chronicle records, of the brothers' faces in the same moment, the same kind of shift. [[Nazu]], who had stopped pretending to sleep for the conversation, looked as if he might kill Ezericht where he stood. [[Zin]], who had walked in with him, grew rather nervous.

Six reached out and took the stone from Ezericht's hand.

He turned it slowly.

*"You say your friend left this to you?"*

He held it up to the light.

*"How odd. Why would one such as himself leave something like this behind?"*

He looked at the dark red star at the centre. The chronicle does not record what he saw in the colour that no one else in the room could see.

*"And he let it empty out as well. Very odd indeed."*

He spoke the next words at the level of a man speaking to no one in particular.

*"I guess he wanted his mortality back."*

He folded his fingers over the stone.

He looked at Ezericht.

*"Well — that explains a bit."*

He stepped close enough to Ezericht for his words to carry without being heard by the brothers behind him.

*"But if you ever touch my stone again — I'm going to kill you. Do we have an understanding?"*

Ezericht did not flinch.

He did not, the chronicle observes, lower his eyes.

*"I fully understand the consequences of touching the stone. But would you have paid more attention had I just tried talking to you?"*

He held Six's gaze.

*"Exactly. I know how you are."*

He let the moment carry itself.

*"But tell me — you know who this person is? You acted as if you knew of him before."*

He paused.

*"And what do you mean — wanted his mortality back? I didn't realize you guys were born mortal."*

The chronicle records, of the final question, the deliberate evenness with which Ezericht laid it on the table.

*"Obviously — there is a side of you I'm not aware of."*

Six did not, immediately, answer.

He began, slowly, to walk in a small circle within the command room.

The chronicle records, of the moment, only that the silence that followed Ezericht's question was longer than any silence the chronicle had recorded in his company before — and that, by the time the silence ended, the sun outside the command-room window had fully set and the blood-red sky had given way to the first dark of an evening that was, by all accounts the chronicle has assembled, the longest in the lives of the company gathered in the room.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the resolution of the battle at the Western Barracks by means other than continued combat: [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s audacious removal of [[Six]]'s soul-stone — by which he secured Six's attention at the cost of full-body paralysis from the stone's defensive contact-poison — and [[Six]]'s consequent stand-down order to his brother [[Zin]], by their shared telepathic link.

The chapter further records [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s explanation to [[Six]] of the full background of the monastery's contract on King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]], the military's last-resort plan, and — first canonically — the **true origin of [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s dagger** as a relic of an older era's necromancy contracts. The chronicle places this *older era* in the late **Convergence** period (the centuries preceding [[Bohrova]]'s reforms), when mortal magic had begun systematizing for the first time since the Veiling and the contract-tradition of binding souls to bodies through necromantic instrument was widespread enough to require [[The Academic Awakening|the Bohrovan reforms]] to suppress. The dagger was made to *release the soul from the body and prevent its return* — the only known instrument capable of cleanly dissolving a body-bound necromantic pact. The chronicle further understands that **the tradition that produced [[Six's Father]]'s elemental experiments belongs to the same late-Convergence necromantic lineage** — the dagger and the soul-stones are *related instruments of a single old practice*, parallel inheritances from a world before Bohrova made magic legible. Edward rips up [[Six]]'s now-void contract.

The chapter introduces the canonical detail of **the monastery's military arm** — Ezericht's revelation that half the men of the Holy Crusades of twenty years prior had been monastery monks under regular-soldier cover — and the **Western Barracks strategy map**, by which Edward demonstrates the military's prepared positions against the order's outposts across Dyradia.

The chapter's closing reveals — first canonically — that the necromantic creation-technique by which [[Six's Father]] produced [[Six]] and his elemental brothers was **not the technique's first iteration**. Earlier, less successful experiments had also produced beings of Six's kind. One such earlier being had befriended a young [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] in his foster-family's town, had given Ezericht the same kind of small relic-stone Six and his brothers wore at their necks, and had — by Six's reading of the stone's grey-and-red residue — **chosen mortality back**, draining the stone's binding by his own act and dying as an ordinary mortal does. The friend's name was never given. He had told Ezericht: *"In the end, you'll find it out anyways."*
