# Chapter 6 — The Amulet and the Dagger

> *In which a man who has been pretending not to be a commander stops pretending, and the unwilling heir learns who his father was.*

---

## I. Behind the Shack

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] did not stop walking until he was around the corner of [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s house, where the line of sight from the front door no longer reached him and the standing undead in the yard could not, by the structure of their bindings, particularly attend.

He turned.

He took hold of Ezericht by the shoulder, spun him a quarter-turn further away from the shack's wall, and bent his head down close to the other man's ear. When he spoke, it was in the low even voice of a man who had taught himself not to be overheard.

*"You should be."*

Ezericht had asked, only a moment earlier, whether Edward was having last-minute reservations about the contract. The reply had not been the one he expected.

*"I need you to grab hold of this amulet on my neck. And run when I do. You have to trust me. I know where that dagger came from."*

Ezericht's hand stopped midway to the dagger at his hip.

*What the —*

He did not finish the thought aloud. The thought was very long and very hot inside his head. He had carried the dagger since the day his foster family had pulled him from whatever circumstance had left him in their hands; he had been told, by everyone who had ever asked, that no one had ever seen its like before; he had been told, by his foster father whose word had been good in everything else, that the etchings down its blade did not match any tradition any scholar of the family had ever consulted. He had carried it for the entire span of his adult life and he had never met a man who claimed to know its provenance.

He had met one now.

*I don't have time. He knows something about me that I don't. I have to act fast.*

He closed his hand around the turquoise amulet on Edward's neck.

*"Go for it. You get us out of this safely and tell me what you know. I'll do any and everything I can."*

Edward nodded once.

*"Good."*

He took off.

---

## II. The Run

Edward ran the alleyways of [[Sahlen]] at the speed of a man who had measured them for exactly this contingency. He held the amulet's cord around his neck steady with one hand and dragged Ezericht behind him by the chain of it, the way a man might drag a younger brother through a market he had been told to never get separated from. The morning light fell uneven through the gaps between buildings. The yardsmen at the shack's perimeter did not move; their bindings did not extend past the yard.

Inside the shack, the chronicle records, [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] received the news of their departure with one slow lift of her eyebrow, considered it for a small measured pause, and decided not to chase.

*Better now,* she thought, *than before the contract is signed.*

She took the feather from the table and signed the mission contract and the blood contract in her own name, in the patient hand of a woman who had signed many such things. The chronicle preserves the moment, because the moment matters: Edward and Ezericht were already running when Trinity's signature joined the others.

She would, in time, find them.

She did not, at this moment, intend to.

---

## III. The Armory Gate

Edward did not slow his pace until they had reached the door of the town armory on the eastern square of Sahlen, where the morning watch had begun and where the iron-shoed guard at the gate moved to interpose himself between them and the entrance.

*"What is your business here?"*

The voice was brisk. The guard's hand was on the haft of his halberd. He did not, in his glance at Edward, find recognition.

Edward did not let go of the amulet's cord.

*"Take me to your superior,"* he said.

*"On what grounds?"*

*"On the grounds of Commander Zywarf."*

The guard's eyes opened.

They opened the way the eyes of a low-ranking man open when a name he has only heard at officer's call is given to him, in person, by a stranger he had been three seconds away from striking with his weapon. He stepped sideways and did not look at either of them again.

Edward walked past him. He dragged Ezericht with him by the cord.

He did not slow until they were through the inner courtyard and at the door of the office at the back of the building.

He opened the door.

The uniformed man behind the desk stood up.

*"What the hell are you thinking bringing him in, Edward? I should revoke your contract right this INSTANT!"*

Edward did not break stride. He brought Ezericht inside with him and closed the door behind them both.

*"The monastery tried to employ him,"* he said. *"They almost got a sample of his blood. This was my only alternative, Flitney."*

The General sat back down very slowly. The chair creaked under him. He looked at Ezericht once — a long, considering look — and looked back at Edward.

*"I see. What have you told him?"*

*"Nothing yet. But he knows that we know."*

*"Okay."*

The General gestured to the amulet at Edward's neck. *"What the hell is he doing holding onto that?"*

*"He needs a bouncer, Flit. Hanging onto mine won't do too much good in the coming… problematic situations. Do you have a spare?"*

*"I always keep a spare. You know that."*

The General fished through the back of a locked drawer in his desk and produced a turquoise amulet identical, by every visible mark, to the one around Edward's neck. He held it out to Ezericht across the desk.

Ezericht took it.

The chronicle records, with the careful retrospective neutrality it reserves for such things, that the amulet served a second function neither the General nor [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] disclosed to Ezericht in the office that morning. The Mehrendt military's stated purpose for the amulet — *protection against monastery contract magic*, the *bouncer* Edward had named — was true. It was also incomplete. The amulet had been issued at Edward's first commission for the same reason it was now being placed around Ezericht's neck, and the General had not, on either occasion, told the bearer the second reason. The reason will become relevant in time. The chronicle records that it had, by that morning, been working for years.

*"Go ahead and have a seat,"* the General said. *"We might as well tell you what is going on, lad."*

---

## IV. The Dagger and the Scar

Ezericht sat down.

He had, while running, taken the precaution of leaving his sword behind at the shack. He had not, however, left the dagger. He laid it across his knee and let the daylight from the office window catch the etchings along its blade — the fine spiral marks running its length, the small unfamiliar characters at the cross-guard, the symbol he had never been able to identify near the pommel.

He spoke quietly, looking at the dagger rather than at either of the men in the room.

*"There isn't much I can really tell you about me. My history is a blur. I have no recollection of where I was before. I was found by my foster family when I was very young. All that was found with me was this dagger."*

He turned the blade so the General could see it.

*"I also think that the scar going down my face may have something to do with my lineage. Not sure what it symbolizes either. I was hoping you could answer that — since you said you know things about me."*

He looked, finally, at [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]].

*"And while you guys are at it — could you care to explain exactly who you are? This is all a bit confusing."*

Edward shrugged once, moved his hair from behind his ears to in front of them — the same gesture, Ezericht now realized, the bowman had made at the South Gate when he had wanted to hear sounds further away — and leaned against the office wall. The chronicle observes, with the same careful retrospective neutrality, that the unusual range of Edward's hearing had not, in the office that morning, struck Edward himself as remarkable. It would do so, in time. It would do so later than the chronicle wishes he had noticed.

*"The Church knows the location of the King,"* he said. *"And they are hiring a party to go retrieve him. As for this situation — have at it, Flit. Just don't yell. I'm going to try to keep an ear on the perimeter."*

He went still against the wall.

General Flitney leaned backward in his chair until the wood complained and looked at Ezericht for a long unhurried moment.

*"I'm gonna keep this as simple as I can at this point."*

---

## V. The Contract Before the Contract

The General's account, as Ezericht heard it that morning, ran as follows.

The Church had, some eight months previously, brought King [[Alaric La'Monte]] to a private audience and presented him with a contract. The contract had been worded in the long, careful, agreeable language the Church used for its public agreements and the substantive private language it used for its private ones, and it had bound the King to a gradual, measured program of legislative reform over the following ten years — reforms that would, in their cumulative effect at the program's completion, transfer complete control of Mehrendt into the Church's hands.

The King had signed it.

The General did not, in the telling, dwell on why the King had signed it. The chronicle understands, from the General's manner, that the why was not flattering.

The military leadership had learned of the contract because intelligence missions had been running, day and night, against the monastery on the western borders for a longer period than the Church likely realized. The intelligence had come back. It had been understood.

The military had taken the King into custody — quietly, away from the court, into an underground facility well known to the General and not known to anyone the Church had access to — until a way to break the contract could be found without placing the Church in immediate control of the kingdom.

The Church, in response, had propagandized a civil war.

The civil war's apparent purpose, on its public face, had been the resolution of various local grievances along the southern border. Its actual purpose, beneath the public face, had been to draw the King out of hiding — because the King would not, the Church judged, allow his subjects to die in a war his own appearance could end.

The King, hidden where he was, had not appeared.

The war had continued.

The General's face, throughout this section of the briefing, did not move.

*"In light of the recent actions,"* he said, *"we must revise our plans. The Church must not be allowed to return him to the throne while the contract exists."*

He set his hands flat on the desk.

*"The only loophole we have found is a sad option. Killing the King himself. This is our last option — and the only way to preserve the way of Dyradia."*

He looked at Ezericht.

*"The reason we need you for this is that the King is very well protected. Multiple hexes, enchantments, and barriers are in place to keep him alive. These spells are ingrained into him and his blood and have been used to preserve the royal bloodline for generations."*

He breathed out.

*"They are, however, cancelled out by people with the same protections."*

---

## VI. The Heritage

The General said the next part more gently than he had said any other part.

*"You are an unofficial son of the King."*

The room did not, in any external way, change.

In the chronicle's record of Ezericht's face in the next several seconds, what changed was the muscle behind the jaw and the line at the corner of the eye. He did not, at first, speak.

Flitney went on.

*"He wouldn't listen to us about the security issues. He persisted, in his words, in being 'a lady's man.' Which, of course, was how he was captured by the Church to begin with. One of the lady's men he was — was your mother. Who was killed during childbirth."*

He laid his palm flat on the desk between them, the way a man might lay it flat between himself and a wound he intended to clean.

*"We set up a family for you. And we have kept tabs on you ever since."*

Ezericht looked at him.

*"Now, Ezericht. We need you to do your country a favor. You must go to his holding place — and dispose of him. You are the only one who could make it to him before the Church does. The rest of the family is too far away to make it in time."*

He sat back. He addressed Edward without turning his head.

*"Your mission is still to take any measures to protect him. ANY measures. Do you understand what you are to do?"*

Edward nodded once from the wall.

*"Yes, sir. I'll want reinforcements sent ASAP to the base, as well as autonomy to carry out my mission."*

*"You'll have it."*

Flitney returned his attention to Ezericht.

*"How are you doing? Do you think you can do this?"*

---

## VII. The Stutter

Ezericht did not, immediately, answer.

He sat forward. He set the dagger down on his knee. He held the turquoise amulet in his other hand without remembering how it had gotten there.

*"I'm — the son of the KING? That can't be."*

His voice cracked on the verb.

*"Why didn't I know?!"*

The General sighed. The sigh was very tired and very old and did not belong, by its sound, to the morning of a man who was about to send a child of the Crown to murder a parent he had never met.

*"Oh, but you are. You are a direct descendant to the throne of Dyradia. We couldn't tell you, or anyone, about it. Political problems could have risen. This kingdom is monogamous, and having a womanizer as a King wouldn't have brought cheerful attention to us at all."*

Ezericht looked back down at the dagger.

*"It's all starting to make sense now."*

He turned the blade slowly in his lap.

*"The dagger, I assume, is a way of proving my lineage?"*

Flitney nodded.

*"That, and that scar. It's no ordinary cut, my friend. Both of those are undeniable proof — but only those who know of the mark and the dagger would be able to tell. Otherwise it could be considered a battle mark and a decorative blade. Nothing more."*

Ezericht considered the dagger for a long moment.

*"So what I am to do is to go to where his Majesty is — and KILL him?"*

He looked up.

*"And then the plan that the Church has will fail?"*

Flitney nodded once.

*"And what is to happen to me, once he is dead?"*

He asked the question very evenly. The chronicle preserves the cadence: not as a man asking what his reward will be, but as a man asking what shape his life will take on the far side of the act.

*"Will I step up as King?"*

---

## VIII. Third From the Throne

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] answered.

He had been silent throughout the General's briefing. The chronicle records that he answered Ezericht's question because he wished, in some part of himself that did not show on his face, to spare the General the answering.

*"You are third from the throne,"* he said.

He did not, when he said it, look at Ezericht.

*"However — the entire structure may be… influenced, by your actions here. We shall see soon enough."*

He came off the wall. He addressed the General.

*"I need as few men as possible for this to keep us moving fast. Myself, Ez, and three other men. An archer and two spearmen, if possible. You know your men. Get your first picks here with two backups, in case I find one of them to have a problem."*

The General opened his mouth to object.

*"Do not —"*

Edward reached across the desk and caught the General's finger before it had finished pointing.

*"Do you want this done or not?"*

He held the finger for one beat — long enough for the General's eye to flicker once, in something not quite anger and not quite respect, and then settle.

*"You're on thin ground. Again."*

The General let it go.

*"Then again — you haven't been wrong before. No reason to think so now."*

He gestured to the office door.

*"Open the door a second."*

Edward reached over and opened it.

*"PATRICK!"* the General shouted at the corridor outside. *"Get me the Fourth Wall Division to the stables."*

A small uniformed man in the corridor jumped visibly.

*"NOW."*

The runner scampered off.

Edward closed the door, leaned back against the wall, and looked at Ezericht.

*"Thank you, sir,"* he said to the General over his shoulder. *"We'll return shortly with the corpse of a King."*

He turned to Ezericht.

*"Come on."*

Ezericht stood up from the chair. He sheathed the dagger.

He held the turquoise amulet in his left hand and let the cord settle around his neck as he walked. The amulet was warm in a way it had not been when the General had first handed it to him. He did not, in the moment, know whether the warmth came from the General's locked drawer, the closeness of his own palm, or the bloodline he had not, until that morning, known he carried.

He followed Edward out of the office into the corridor where the runner had gone, and out through the armory's iron gates into the daylight of [[Sahlen]] — a daylight that, to him, looked the same as it had thirty minutes earlier and was, in every way that mattered, not.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the private conference between [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]], [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]], and General Flitney of the Mehrendt military in the armory office at [[Sahlen]] on the morning of the contract signing.

The chapter establishes four matters of canonical significance:

1. **[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] is in military service as Commander Zywarf** — his previously-stated bodyguard work for an unnamed paying patron was the cover identity for a military protective detail assigned to Ezericht for an unspecified period.
2. **The Church** is engaged in a covert effort to assume legislative control of [[Mehrendt]] over a ten-year horizon, by means of a contract signed by King [[Alaric La'Monte]] under circumstances the General did not fully recount.
3. **[[Ezericht La'Monte]] is the unofficial son of King Alaric La'Monte** — his mother was a private lover of the King who died in childbirth; the foster family that raised him was a military placement; the **dagger** and the **facial scar** that he carries are recognition-markers of the royal lineage, identifiable only to those who already know what to look for.
4. **The military's last resort plan** is to use Ezericht to assassinate the King and so nullify the Church's contract by removing the contracting party. Edward's role is to keep Ezericht alive long enough to complete the act.

The relationship between **the Church** as named by General Flitney and the **[[Hypostasis Monastery]]** under which [[Trinity Stolen]] operates is not resolved in this chapter. The chronicle records the General's terminology without forcing a structural relationship; later chapters may clarify.

The chronicle's later record establishes that the **turquoise amulet** issued by [[General Flitney]] — and worn by [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] from his first commission — served two purposes. The first, the *bouncer* against monastery contract magic, was the function disclosed to its bearers. The second, an aspect of Mehrendt military intelligence protocols not disclosed in the office that morning, was the masking of an Awoken signature against the Church's detection instruments. The chronicle understands, in retrospect, that the General had been quietly protecting Edward's nature from the same monastery he had been protecting [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s lineage from — and from Edward himself, who had not been told. See [[The Awoken]] and [[Edward Z Zwarf]] for the consequences when the amulet later failed.

*Period source: the founding fragments preserved in The RP that Started it All, posts 140–150. (Trinity's interim signing of the mission and blood contracts is preserved from posts 144 and 163.)*
