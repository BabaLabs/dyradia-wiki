# Chapter 12 — The Coronation of Lies

> *In which a body is carried in a mist, a contract burns itself in every chamber it had ever been stored, a Gheronda removes his hood for the first time in the lifetimes of those gathered, and a city of grieving citizens swears its loyalty to the man who arranged the grief they are grieving.*

---

## I. The Body at the Throne

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] sat for some time at the foot of King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]]'s throne after the gem-array was complete.

She had not, in the months of preparation that had led to this evening, prepared herself emotionally for the small unwanted weight of sitting alone in a sealed underground chamber with the corpse of the man whose ten-year contract she had been sent to dissolve. She had prepared mechanically. She had prepared logistically. She had not prepared for the *quiet*.

She let the quiet rest for as long as she would allow herself.

Then she stood up.

She walked back along the cleared corridor — past the place where the brown gem had parted the soil, past the place where the chains of fire had risen — and to the threshold of the chamber the order had specified for her exit.

She stopped at the threshold.

She looked back at the body slumped against the worked stone of the throne. The nine white gems she had laid at his feet brightened, faintly and in unison, as her shadow crossed back over them.

*"[[Shift]],"* she said into the dark. *"Get here soon."*

She sat down cross-legged on the floor.

She waited.

---

## II. The Mist That Carried Him

[[Shift]] arrived perhaps five minutes later.

The chronicle records the arrival — the small familiar gathering of the air's weight into a shape Trinity had come to associate with welcome — with the same quiet relief Trinity herself recorded in her own face. She smiled.

She waited until the gathering had taken its purple dragon-and-sea-serpent form, small enough to sit in her open palm.

*"Have you delivered the message to the others informing them that the contract is null — and the payment that comes with it?"*

A high single shriek-note in the affirmative.

She drew a small flat sweet cake from one of her robe's many inner pockets and gave it across. Shift devoured it without ceremony.

*"Good. Wouldn't want people to waste their time on a now pointless task — now would we."*

She turned, briefly, to look back at the body.

*"Anyway. Help me with carrying this body to the monastery. My transportation method is only for one — as you know — and I would loath to lose the body along the way. Just surround him with your presence, if you will."*

Shift, in her palm, did not move for a half-breath.

Then it dissolved.

The dissolution, the chronicle understands, was not the smooth scatter into seed-form Shift had used at [[Valham]]. The dissolution was a slow controlled expansion into a *fog* — a thick low fog that crept along the floor of the corridor at Trinity's gesture, entered the chamber by the door she had left open, climbed the stone of the throne in a slow circling motion, and settled around the body of King Alaric in the close attentive manner of a thing that had been told what to do and had decided to do it well.

The fog encased the body. The body did not move within the fog.

Trinity nodded, once, as if to herself.

She drew, from another pocket of her robe, an old piece of paper.

She whispered a phrase the chronicle has not preserved.

She closed her eyes.

The corridor around her went briefly bright. The bright went dark. When she opened her eyes again, she was no longer in the underground.

She was standing in front of the Gheronda.

---

## III. The Old Man and the Mock Bow

The Gheronda was waiting for her.

The chronicle records the moment in the careful detail it has reserved for moments it suspects may, in the years that follow, be cited often. The Gheronda stood at the centre of a small audience-chamber whose walls bore the colours of the [[Hypostasis Monastery]]'s capital outpost — pale stone, banded with cloth of two greens and a deep oxblood red — and the chamber's high narrow window overlooked the central plaza of [[Nohlle]] and, beyond it, the city's harbour. He stood in his ordinary robe, hood up, hands folded at the front. He was waiting.

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] arrived in the centre of the chamber with the small displaced *whuff* of air that her translocation paper produced at the destination end. She did not, on arrival, look at him directly.

Behind her, perhaps three breaths later, the fog of [[Shift]] arrived bearing the body of King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]].

The Gheronda's eyes moved past Trinity to the fog, and to the shape of the body within it.

*"Ah — Trinity, my child. I see you were successful."*

Trinity inclined her head.

She bowed.

The bow was performed at the depth, the angle, and the duration of the bow the order's junior agents were taught to give to the Gheronda on formal occasions, and Trinity executed it — the chronicle observes — with the small careful precision of a woman who had been taught the gesture by the same Gheronda who was now receiving it.

*"Of course,"* she said as she straightened. *"Am I ever one to fail, Gheronda."*

The Gheronda laughed.

The laugh was the brief sharp laugh of an old man who had been at his post long enough to recognize, in the deportment of his most senior field-agent, the precise calibration of mockery she had selected for him.

*"Why, Trinity — don't mock me in my old age. You are never one to show such manners of respect to myself in private. And I don't believe you have started now."*

Trinity smirked.

She raised her head and met his eyes under the hood.

*"You're right, old man. Either way — here is the body of the King."*

She gestured for Shift to bring the fog-wrapped body closer.

*"Though as you can see — they drained the blood after killing him. Not sure why. Do you have any ideas?"*

The Gheronda's hood turned, fractionally, toward the body.

He frowned.

He frowned for some time.

*"It is possible they know about the power of the blood,"* he said, eventually. *"After all — they knew about the added protection. And the bastard child."*

Trinity nodded slowly.

She did not, in the moment, let on whether she found the Gheronda's answer satisfying.

---

## IV. The Contract That Burned

She moved past the body of the King — left to right across the chamber, in the small restless motion of a woman who had been sitting too long underground — and stopped at the high narrow window.

She did not, when she stopped, look out.

*"Is everything ready to start on the second stage of the plan?"* she said over her shoulder. *"And what about the contract made with the King? Won't it be troublesome if it shows up?"*

The Gheronda's face — the chronicle reconstructs it from Trinity's later report — relaxed into the small private smile of an old conspirator whose preferred topic of conversation had been raised by his preferred audience.

*"Yes. The second stage was started already."*

He folded his hands again.

*"And I wouldn't worry about the contract. After all — it is magical. And because of that, it, and all copies that may have been made, were burned by magic as soon as the King died."*

He paused.

*"It is a shame I can't see the face of shock and anger after they can't find not even one copy of the contract — or even the contract itself — anywhere in the kingdom."*

He did not, the chronicle records, say *who* the *they* he referred to were. The chronicle suspects Trinity understood. The chronicle further suspects the Gheronda intended the ambiguity.

*"Also,"* he continued, *"after receiving your message — I made an announcement to the kingdom that the King had been found, being held by a rebel militia. The hope I built will be crushed and become vengeful anger."*

He folded his hands again.

*"And — perhaps the most useful thing the evening has given us — we now know which of his sons did it. The bastard is now known to us. Two days, perhaps three, before he is ours."*

Trinity, the chronicle records, did not — for the first time in the conversation — find an answer ready to her face. She had performed the recruitment of the company at [[Sahlen]] in the belief that the company's death or absorption at the mission's completion was the end of the matter for them. She had not understood — until the moment the Gheronda said the sentence at the window — that the company's *capture and use* was the matter the Gheronda had been planning toward.

She did not, the chronicle further records, allow her face to register the recalibration.

He turned, slowly, to look at her at the window.

*"In fact — at the moment, I am about to head to make another announcement now that we have the body."*

He extended a hand toward the chamber's door.

*"Will you follow me, please."*

---

## V. The Balcony

They walked through the monastery in silence.

Trinity drew her hood up at the door of the chamber and did not, through the corridors, push it back. The monks they passed in the corridors of the monastery bowed to the Gheronda and noted Trinity by glance. The Gheronda did not pause.

They reached the door of the balcony.

A man Trinity recognized as **Monk Sephari** stood already at the balcony's far side, a long polished horn cradled at his hip. He bowed as the Gheronda emerged. He raised the horn to his lips when the Gheronda stepped into the line of sight of the plaza below.

He blew it.

The note carried — the chronicle preserves — across the entire plaza, off the walls of the buildings ringing it, and across the wide canal at the plaza's western margin into the streets of the lower city beyond. It was the call to assembly of the high monastic order. It was a note the city of Nohlle did not, in the chronicle's understanding, hear without weight.

The citizens began to gather.

By the chronicle's reckoning, ten minutes passed. Perhaps a little more. The patio in front of the monastery filled. The streets behind the patio filled. Trinity, behind her hood at the door of the balcony, watched the gathering with the practiced patience of a woman who had been told, by the man she stood beside, to be prepared for what would happen next.

The Gheronda raised a hand.

The plaza fell silent.

---

## VI. The Address

The chronicle records the address that followed.

The chronicle records it because Trinity — who understood every cadence of it — recorded it in her own later report with the small bitter precision of a woman who had heard, in her own ear, the construction of a thing she would later be required to defend.

The Gheronda's voice carried.

*"I have an important — yet devastating — announcement to make to you all."*

He let the silence settle for a beat.

*"As I have said earlier, the King was found in the hands of that despicable rebel militia."*

The three words *despicable rebel militia* he spoke at a register between hatred and distress. The chronicle records that he had practiced the register. Many in the plaza nodded.

*"However — we were too late in the rescue. And the atrocity had already been committed."*

He paused.

*"I am most saddened to inform you all — the King has been killed."*

The plaza erupted.

The chronicle preserves the *kinds* of sound the eruption contained — angry shouts, sorrowful shouts, the long wordless cry of a woman near the front of the gathering whose voice the chronicle suspects had been a constant of public assemblies in Nohlle for many years — and the moments at which they each peaked. The Gheronda let the eruption build.

He raised a hand.

The plaza did not entirely silence. It thinned.

*"As many of you may or may not know — the monastery had taken the uttermost measures to protect the King. By enchanting his blood, so that no harm could come at him in any way."*

He let the assertion stand.

*"However — as hard as we tried — we could not make it so that his direct descendants would not be able to cause him harm."*

Many in the gathering adopted the confused look of people who had not known the limitation. Others nodded slowly, the way people who had known a thing for some time will nod when the thing is finally said in public.

*"It wasn't because we didn't trust our prince and princess. But because we knew the King had a bastard child."*

The plaza gasped.

The chronicle preserves the gasp because, in the years that followed, the gasp would be invoked by witnesses to demonstrate the precise moment at which the public's attention shifted from the monastery's failure to the bastard's act. The shift was deliberate. The shift was prepared.

*"It seems this child — in search of power and greed, and with vengeance and envy in his heart, for not being recognized as royalty — took the side of the rebels and killed his own helpless father. Who would never even think about raising his hand against his child — bastard or not."*

Some in the plaza sobbed.

Trinity, behind her hood, watched the sob spread among the women near the front of the patio and the angry murmur spread among the men behind them, and felt — the chronicle records — a small bitter amusement at the cleanness of the spreading.

The Gheronda gave the sigh of a man whose own sorrow had become too heavy for the words he was using.

*"As soon as I found out the news of the King's demise,"* he continued, *"I sent a messenger to inform the royal family to stay in the lands of [[Marencia]] for protection. But — alas — "*

He stopped. He cleared his throat. He continued.

*"The messenger — he was too late. The immediate royal family was attacked and killed by the rebels on the way back."*

The plaza, this time, did not erupt. It collapsed.

The sobs got louder. The angry murmurs became shouts. The wordless cry of the woman at the front of the gathering reached a register the chronicle does not have a word for.

The Gheronda let the collapse run.

He waited.

When the collapse had begun to settle into the long sustained anger that was, the chronicle suspects, the substance he had been building toward, he raised the hand again.

The plaza thinned.

*"A funeral for the King will be held in thirty minutes — in the plaza — for all to go."*

He breathed.

*"The King would have wanted it so."*

He sighed.

*"We must remain united in these times. These rebels are looking to take over the country — by destroying the monarchy and the monastery."*

The angry shouts rose.

He let them.

The chronicle records, of the moment at which the Gheronda raised the hand the third time, that the difficulty he encountered in regaining silence was not a difficulty he had not anticipated. The chronicle suspects he had built the difficulty in deliberately. The chronicle suspects he counted the seconds of his own quieting and noted, with satisfaction, the second at which the silence finally returned.

*"I regrettably admit,"* he said, *"that they may be successful. As strong and great as our monastery is — we do not have the force to fight a war against the militia of their kind. Not only that — but our royal forces would be in chaos. Since so many of them have been corrupted, and turned to the rebels, in seeking power."*

He stopped.

*"Dyradia won't be able to survive long. However — we may endure longer, if we had your help."*

He paused, longer than he had paused before.

He looked at the crowd under his hood.

Then — slowly, by deliberate stages — he removed the hood.

---

## VII. The Hood

The plaza gasped, again. The gasp this time was of a different kind. It had the texture of recognition reserved for sacred objects.

The chronicle preserves what it can of the Gheronda's face. It was lined, and old. Its eyes were a colour the chronicle has not seen in any other face it has documented. The bald head above the brows had the smooth high-domed shape of a head that had not, in living memory, been seen by the citizens of the city below the balcony. The chronicle records that the removal of the hood was, in the order's protocol, a thing reserved for the rarest of public addresses — and that, at its performance, the Gheronda was understood by his audience to be temporarily reminding them of his position as *a man considered nearly a god.*

The crowd, at the sight of the face, knelt.

Trinity, behind the door of the balcony, did not kneel.

She watched the kneeling crowd and thought, the chronicle records, with the calm internal commentary she reserved for moments when the order's politics performed in front of her on a stage she had helped to build:

*That manipulative old coot.*

She let the thought finish.

*Well — that's the end of that "rebelling militia." Every citizen loyal to Dyradia will be against them. At least the majority, and all the nobles, who will think themselves in danger from the "rebels."*

She breathed.

*Pity, too. They seemed like they would have been fun to have around.*

The Gheronda did not, the chronicle records, register her thought. The chronicle does not believe he had to.

He extended his bare hand toward the crowd.

*"I ask you,"* he said, *"as a man of God and Dyradia — to please help defend our country. And to stand together against these rebels — in this time of need."*

He paused.

*"So — what do you, my people, say?"*

The plaza erupted, this time, in shouts.

The shouts were of loyalty. The shouts were of promised support. The shouts were of the kind of mass voice that the chronicle has documented in only a handful of other moments in the history of Mehrendt, and has not — in the moments it has documented — found a parallel for in this one.

The shouts continued.

They continued long enough that the monks attending the Gheronda had to come up onto the balcony to help him to stand, because his knees, by the time the kneeling had concluded, would not by themselves.

He drew the hood back over his head as he passed Trinity at the door.

He stopped, briefly.

He met her eyes under his hood.

He smiled.

*"Prepare, Trinity,"* he said. *"For the war has started."*

He passed her by.

The chronicle records — without comment — that Trinity, alone at the door of the balcony, smirked once at his receding back and then turned her face to look at the kneeling crowd below.

The crowd was still shouting.

---

## VIII. End of Part One

The chronicle marks, at the close of this chapter, the end of the period it understands as **Part One** of the events surrounding the regicide of King [[Alaric La'Monte]].

The chronicle records, of the close, what it understands to have ended:

That the [[Veneficus Contracts|mission contract]] of the [[Hypostasis Monastery]] was complete and dissolved.

That King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]] was dead, and his body — by the order's procedure — secured. That he would be remembered, in the years that followed, by the title the silence between his last words and his son's strike had given him: **the Silent Crown**.

That the legitimate royal line of [[Mehrendt]] had, by the same evening's reported events, been entirely ended.

That [[Ezericht La'Monte]] — third from the throne by the standing of his birth, and now first by the simple arithmetic of his line's elimination — had been publicly denounced in the capital city of [[Nohlle]] as a kingslayer and an instrument of *rebel militia* that did not yet, by his own knowledge or [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s, exist.

That the Gheronda had, in approximately twelve minutes of public oratory from a balcony at the centre of his order's capital outpost, transformed the regicide his own contract had occasioned into the founding propaganda of the religious-political state he had been planning to install for the better part of a decade.

That [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] knew.

The chronicle records, of what was beginning:

That the company at the Western Barracks — [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]], [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]], [[Six]], and Six's elemental brothers — had not yet learned the news of the public address, and did not yet know that the war they had begun to plan from a strategy map on a long polished table had already, that evening, been formally declared against them in a plaza two hundred miles east.

That [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] carried, in her pocket, a red gem containing a spectre that had been the bloodline-protection of a dead King — and, in her conscience, a *promise* she had spoken at the foot of his throne whose substance the chronicle has not yet preserved.

That [[Six]]'s long silence at the close of the previous chapter, on the question of [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s vanished childhood friend, had ended at some hour the chronicle does not yet record, with an answer the chronicle does not yet preserve.

That the company in the swamp castle had not yet returned to their father.

That the Gheronda had not yet, despite the rare exposure of his face, given the chronicle his name. That the chronicle has, separately, reason to believe his absence post-Fracture under the older title of [[The Cardinal|Cardinal — *the Absent Authority*]] will, in the years that follow, be the same absence by a different name.

That a young Avalonian who had once been told they were not who they were — and a young Mehrendian general who had loved them quietly and been told they had died — would each, by separate paths cleared by the Gheronda's machinery and by the public Church his order presented to the world, be ground into instruments of his war. That the recognition between them, when it came, would come too late for safety and just in time for mercy. That what they would do, together, in their last act, would be the thing that closed the Fracture's longest wound — at the cost of the world remembering them honestly. The chronicle names them, here, only as [[Maeleus]] and [[Ometrieysa]]; their own files will tell the rest.

That **Dyradia**, by the chronicle's small private reckoning at the close of this chapter, was no longer the country it had been on the morning of the South Gate raid in [[Sahlen]].

---

The chronicle suspends its record here, at the close of **Part One**.

When it resumes — in a chronicle the chronicle does not yet hold — the war the Gheronda has declared will already have begun to be fought.

The chronicle will record what it can.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the closing events of Part One of the Origin Chronicle: [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s conveyance of King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]]'s body from the underground chamber of the Western Barracks to the [[Hypostasis Monastery|Hypostasis]]'s capital outpost at [[Nohlle]], by means of [[Shift]]'s fog-form transport and a translocation paper; her private audience with the Gheronda; the Gheronda's confirmation that the Church's contract had burned itself and all copies on the King's death; and the Gheronda's public address from the monastery's balcony, by which he transformed the regicide into the founding propaganda of the religious-political state he had been planning to install.

The chapter establishes, on the chronicle's first canonical record, several matters of significance:

- **[[Nohlle]]** as the capital city of Mehrendt at this period.
- **[[Marencia]]** as a polity to which the royal family had been sent for protection, where they were *attacked and killed by the rebels on the way back* — an act the chronicle, in the absence of other testimony, attributes to the Gheronda's own agents under rebel-band cover.
- **The end of the legitimate royal line of [[Mehrendt]]** — the prince and princess Alaric had left in living circumstance at the time of his death were killed in their reported return from Marencia.
- The **Church contract's magical self-burning** on its subject's death — the same self-burning property as the [[Veneficus Contracts|Veneficus instruments]], suggesting the two contractual traditions share a single deeper origin not yet specified by the chronicle.
- The **Gheronda's hood removal** — a gesture of order protocol reserved for the rarest of public addresses, by which the order's senior reminded the public of his position as *a man considered nearly a god*.
- **Monk Sephari** as the bearer of the assembly horn at Nohlle, briefly introduced.

The chronicle preserves — without confirmation — the public details given in the Gheronda's address, including the claim that *so many of [the royal forces] have been corrupted, and turned to the rebels, in seeking power.*

The chronicle preserves — with deliberate uncertainty — the Gheronda's name. The chronicle has not yet been told it. The chronicle has, however, reason to suspect that the Gheronda's western-borders identity and the [[The Cardinal|Cardinal]]'s public-Church identity belong to a single man whose two roles allowed him to survive a detonation that, in later years, would annihilate every other senior figure of the Church of Prim's leadership in a single afternoon.

The chronicle further records that the **bloodline-bind** placed upon King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]]'s holding chamber by the Church — believed by [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] in [[Chapter 6 - The Amulet and the Dagger|Chapter 6]] and [[Chapter 8 - The Fourth Chamber|Chapter 8]] to have been the Church's protective enchantment that the military had outwitted by routing [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s royal blood through it — was, in fact, the Church's *trigger*. The Church had been searching for the unknown heir for years; the bind on Alaric's chamber was the instrument designed to surface him. Edward's confidence in the office that morning was sincere, and incorrect. The Gheronda's confirmation to Trinity at the chamber window — *"the bastard is now known to us"* — marks the moment the chronicle understands the Church's true plan to have been completed by the company believing they had outwitted it.

The chronicle marks the **end of Part One** at the close of this chapter. The events that follow — *the war the Gheronda has declared* — are reserved for the chronicle that does not yet hold them.
