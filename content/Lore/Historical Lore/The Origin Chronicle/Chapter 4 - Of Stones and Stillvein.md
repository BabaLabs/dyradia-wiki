# Chapter 4 — Of Stones and Stillvein

> *In which a monk takes what he came for, an assassin returns with a warning, a herbist proves the worth of his shelves, a necromancer rebuilds himself in a circle of blood, and a woman two forests away dreams of death and decides to recruit a priest.*

---

## I. The Purple Glow

[[Zant]] had not, in any of the company at the South Gate, given his reasons for being in [[Sahlen]] that night.

He had given a half-answer to [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] at the bar — *a travelling monk with his own reasons* — and he had let the half-answer settle for both of them as enough. The chronicle records what he had not said: he had come to Sahlen for an object.

He found it, in the small hours after the contract negotiation had broken up, in the upper room of an abandoned house on the city's eastern margin.

Through the slats of the boarded window he saw two squatters — ragged men of the kind drawn to the empty places after a raid — leaning together over something they had clearly only just discovered. A stone. It rested between them on the warped floorboards of an upper room and it glowed, faintly and consistently, a deep and uneasy purple. The men were staring at it the way men stared at a thing they had already begun to believe might change their lives.

Zant raised his free hand toward the rafters of the house and chanted.

The first lightning bolt missed the roof entirely and struck the alley behind. The second went wide of the upper room. The third found its mark.

The thatch caught. The squatters jumped to their feet, grabbed the stone and bundled it into a leather pouch, and ran for the stairs. They came out the front door and met Zant standing in the street.

He held out his staff.

They fell to the ground.

Zant took the pouch from the limp hand of the nearer man, opened it, looked at the stone within, and closed it again with the patience of someone confirming that he had not, after all, made the wrong trip.

*"I'll be taking what was rightfully mine,"* he said to the unmoving men. *"And if you ever try and take it again, I assure you it will be the last thing you ever do. I spared your life. But your home is going to burn. Once it's gone, the spell on you will wear off and you will be able to move again."*

He drew his ancient book from his hip, chanted a softer thing, and the two squatters slid from paralysis into a slumber that would, in time, wear off into ordinary memory.

He started walking toward the town gate.

*"I have got what I wanted in this town,"* he said to himself. *"There is nothing more for me here."*

He walked through the gate and down the old dirt road in the cool small hour before sunrise.

He did not, then, know that someone would shortly be following him back.

---

## II. The Sword at the Throat

The first sound [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] heard the next morning was someone trying very hard not to make any.

He had — by his own description — trained his ear to be particularly sharp, and the rickety silence of his house at night was a register he knew perfectly. The smallest displacement of air registered. The faintest creak of the back step registered. The soft inhale of someone trying not to breathe loudly *especially* registered.

He was on his feet, sword drawn, and across the room before the intruder had finished crossing the threshold. The point of the sword found the throat of the figure in the doorway.

The figure raised both hands.

*"Oh,"* Ezericht said. *"It's you, Cain."*

He did not, immediately, lower the sword.

*"Why did you sneak in? You can't sneak into my house. It's always dead silent in my house at night, and even the slightest noise, I can hear. Now — what is it that you want?"*

[[Cain]] backed away into the slant of grey light from the window.

*"Firstly, I don't like sharp objects to be that close to my skin."*

He let the light touch his face.

*"Secondly. I just made a quick trip after our conference last night. And I found all our giant friends slaughtered. And some of their attackers I assumed were there also. Coming toward me. And it wasn't —"*

He stopped.

Ezericht looked at him quizzically. Cain dropped flat against the wall below the window an instant before a flame-tipped arrow whistled through it, three inches above where his head had just been, and embedded itself in the rafters.

*"Herbalist!"* Cain said, in the conversational tone of a man calling someone's attention to a detail. *"We have company."*

---

## III. Stillvein

Ezericht ducked below the window. He peeked once. He saw what he had expected to see — the slope of the road behind the house, and far enough up it that an arrow at that range was, even with magical assistance, not an ordinary shot. He could not, from the window, make out the archer.

*"You have the bow,"* he said to Cain. *"Do something about it."*

*"He's a long way out."*

Ezericht had already crossed the floor to the wide cabinet on the far wall. He pulled it open and ran his hand along the row of small dark-glass vials he kept in the upper shelf, one of which he produced and crossed the room with in three steps.

He pressed the vial into Cain's hand.

*"Here. Drink this. It will help you make that shot. It's something I made myself — it calms the muscles in your hands and arms to prevent shaking when aiming. I call it Stillvein."*

Cain examined the vial. He looked at Ezericht.

*"If this has any other effects other than what you told me — you will be in for a world of trouble."*

He pulled the cork, drank the contents in one swallow, and felt the cold chill that moved across him a heartbeat later.

His hands, when he tested them, were perfectly still.

*Coupled with my enchantments,* he thought, *this should be fun.*

He whispered the syllables for distance vision, ducked, rose, took an arrow from his coat-back, nocked it, raised, found the sniper in his magnified field — on a low hilltop, behind perhaps twenty foot-soldiers, with a single guard standing at his shoulder — exhaled once, loosed, and dropped back below the window in a single motion.

A second arrow split the air an inch above where his head had been.

Two faint screams crossed the morning air.

*"Got two of them,"* Cain said.

He stowed the bow, drew both daggers, and walked into the middle of the room.

*"Well, herbalist. I believe it is time for us to have some close-quarters fun."*

---

## IV. The Shack

Ezericht set the sword on his shoulder and grinned.

*"Close quarters. My favorite kind of combat. Let's do this."*

He nodded to Cain. A minute later, the front porch trembled under the weight of running men. The door burst inward.

Ezericht met the first one through it and put his sword through the man's throat. He pulled the blade free; the body dropped; he backed into the rear of the room. He saw Cain cross the floor at a speed that did not, on Cain, seem natural — saw a soldier come apart into ribbons under Cain's twin daggers — and thought, with the unkind affection of one fighter for another, *show-off.*

A second soldier rushed Ezericht. Ezericht parried; the impact was harder than he had expected; his sword left his hand and skittered across the floor. He drew the other in the same motion and cut the man's shoulder open, then crossed back to retrieve his fallen first blade.

He was, by then, dual-wielding again.

Cain ducked beneath a horizontal slash and came up cleaving the swordsman across the torso. The man fell. Cain clinked the daggers together once, sarcastically. He counted bodies on the floor and did the arithmetic on the rest of the wave. *Fifteen. If they just keep going through the doorway, they are just going to keep getting killed. They must realize it's just a bottleneck.*

The next three soldiers came in by three directions at once — one through the doorway, one through each of the side windows — and surrounded Cain in the middle of the room.

Cain whispered the phrase he used for activation and felt his body limber.

He crouched to torso-height, extended his arms with a dagger in each hand, and rose from the crouch spinning. The three soldiers fell apart around him in a single rotation.

He spoke the deactivation. His body slowed back to ordinary.

*I hate it when I have to fight like that,* he thought. *It's not fair. But I guess three on one isn't fair either.*

He looked up. Ezericht had finished his own engagements. The shack was very quiet.

---

## V. The Toast

Ezericht sighed heavily, leaned the sword against the wall, and crossed to the small cabinet by the back door where he kept his everyday ale.

*"What a way to start your morning. A few hours before I normally do, I must say. But still — quite fun. Come, sit down. We'll have a drink until the others come back."*

He set two cups on the table.

*"Oh — and by the way. I don't think I ever properly introduced myself. I'm Ezericht."*

He poured. He sat. He raised his cup to Cain's.

*"Cheers."*

Cain did not raise his cup.

*"Very well, Ezericht. As you already know, I am Cain. I suppose it's best that we get acquainted, since we will be working together for some time."*

He pushed the cup back across the table.

*"As for that drink you poured me — you can have that also. I'm not one for alcohol. It fogs the mind and kills the body."*

Ezericht raised his eyebrows in mild surprise, and then in mild approval, and drank both cups by himself.

## VI. The Circle of Blood

In an upper room of the Inn, at almost the same hour, [[Six]] sat down on the floor and made of himself a small private temple.

He drew the cork from the cross at his neck. He poured a long unbroken line of crimson liquid in a circle around himself on the floorboards. He set the cross beside his knee and began to mutter in a register the chronicler will not attempt to render in any tongue.

The blood circle rose.

It rose without losing its shape — circled him at the height of his torso, a hovering ring of dark red that held its own weight against gravity by some agreement Six had with it that he had not, to date, explained. He opened his eyes when the circle was stable. He watched it swirl for a moment with the satisfaction of a craftsman watching a fire come to temperature.

He unwrapped the bandages along the new arm, and along the seams where the new arm met his shoulder, and along the older scars that had accumulated along his torso over the long years of his condition. He plunged the arm into the suspended circle of blood.

The blood went into him along the scars — soaked them, blackened them, then thinned them — and his body, by some judgement of its own, accepted the new arm into a kinship with the rest.

To confirm the rite had taken, he drew a small blade and opened a clean cut along his arm above the elbow. A thin ooze of darker matter rose into the cut, sealed it, and faded.

*"And now,"* Six said, *"I'm ready to begin my day."*

He stood. He went to the window shade, opened it once, was struck by the morning sun, swore quietly, found his goggles, and closed the shade again until he was prepared.

He did not, that morning, throw anything across the room out of irritation.

He counted this, on balance, as progress.

## VII. The Dream

Two forests west of Sahlen, in a small inn-room of [[Valham]], [[Trinity Stolen]] woke from a dream about five hours after she had laid down to sleep.

She did not, ordinarily, dream. The Hypostasis training tended to discourage it. But in the small hour before dawn the dream had come anyway, and she had seen — in the close intimate detail her trained perception could not, even in sleep, refuse — death. Not the deaths at Sahlen's South Gate. Other deaths. Older. Further. Some of them familiar enough to her to wake her with a cold pressure at her chest she had not felt in years.

She breathed it down. She sat up. She crossed the small room to the basin and drew water across her face, and stood looking at her face in the small dim mirror until it was, again, her face.

She considered the company at Sahlen. She had recruited four. She had been told, in dream and not in dream, that a fifth was wanted. The face the dream had offered her was familiar — the priest she had seen at the second wave, the elvish-featured monk who had called down rain on the burning city. The chronicle records: she decided, with the immediacy of a person who has long since stopped wasting time on the gap between decision and action, to go and find him.

She dressed. She passed the front desk.

*"Lauren,"* she said. *"I will be leaving now."*

[[Lauren Fern]], at the desk, lifted a hand in acknowledgment. Trinity drew the hood over her face, opened the inn door against its small night-cooled resistance, and stepped into the cold pre-dawn air of Valham.

She ran east.

---

## VIII. The Trail in Sahlen

She reached the gates of [[Sahlen]] in something under half an hour. She slowed once she was inside the walls. The town was quiet but not entirely so; a few of the inn-rooms held early-rising laborers, and the air carried the long settled smell of a city that had not, in the last day, been entirely on fire.

She went first to the bar, then closed her eyes and reached with the part of her perception that recognized presences. She found the trace of the priest she remembered — and followed it, at the half-running walk she used when she did not want to be observed too directly, through the streets toward the eastern margin of the city.

She came to a house that had recently burned. Two ragged men lay unconscious in the rubble of its front yard. She bent over each in turn and felt for breath, found it, and did not, with her ordinary mercy, wake them. They were not the trail's source. They were a side-stop.

She followed the trail.

It led — as she had begun to suspect it would — out through the same town gate the priest had earlier walked through, and then back along the inner street toward an inn at the far end of the lane. The trail strengthened as she neared the inn. She stood beneath the wall of the inn looking up at the window from which the trail came.

The window-rim was no wider than an inch.

She took it in a single jump, landed on it without any sound she would describe as audible, opened the window, and stepped inside the room.

---

## IX. The Foot of the Bed

She crossed to the bed where the monk slept and kicked it, gently.

[[Zant]] came up out of sleep in one motion, hand already moving toward the staff against the wall.

*"Who are you?!"* he said. *"What are you doing in my room?"*

He turned his hand toward the ring of candles around the room. The candles all lit at once, throwing the room into a warm orange-red light.

*Damn,* he thought, looking at the hooded figure at the foot of the bed. *I still can't see its face.*

Trinity smiled, and pushed back the hood.

*"I am Trinity Stolen. I come from the monastery."*

She watched his face for the recognition, and found enough of it to proceed.

*"I assume you know about the involvement the monastery has with the government. So you can guess that the disappearance of the King has had a great effect on the monastery. I am here to ask you to join in a mission of rescuing the King. If you join, you shall receive five hundred gold coins beforehand, and after the mission is complete you will receive twenty-five hundred more."*

She did not pause for breath. She spoke the conditions of the contract briskly: the binding instrument, the obligation of silence outside the contract, the obligation of disclosure of new information, the protection of old information, the rule that a relevant question asked by a signatory must be answered. She named the consequence of refusal — the erasure of memory, which was, she said, *standard*.

She looked at the priest.

*"So. What is your answer?"*

Zant considered her in the candlelight.

*"Ah, yes. I know about the problems with the monastery. I will join you in your mission. I will do it freely. I am grateful to help the monastery and in no need of money. Where and when shall we start this mission?"*

Trinity inclined her head.

*"I am grateful for your kindness. However — if not for your own peace, then at least for mine — I would still like to compensate you with twenty-five hundred gold coins."*

She smiled.

*"The mission shall start as soon as the signing of the contract is made. If you would please follow me, after you get your things in order — I shall direct you to the meeting place."*

She crossed to the window, set her foot on the rim, and was gone into the air before he had answered.

She waited for him in the street below.

---

## X. The Walk

Zant gathered the few things in the room he intended to take — the ancient book, the staff, the leather pouch with what he had taken from the squatters' upper room — and came down the stairs of the inn into the early morning street.

He found Trinity waiting.

*"Well — if you insist on giving me the gold,"* he said, *"I will not argue."*

They began to walk together toward the south side of the city, where the rickety house of [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] sat at the end of its side street.

*"I wonder what kind of mission this is going to be,"* Zant said. *"And who else you have gathered."*

*"You will see once we arrive,"* Trinity said. *"Everything will be explained to you."*

They walked.

When they came in sight of Ezericht's shack, Trinity stopped.

She had felt, in the air around the shack, two familiar presences. She had felt, also, a great deal of blood that had not been there the night before. The yard around the house was scattered with the bodies of men in raiding armor, and the shack itself was very quiet.

She did not, at that moment, know that [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] was waking from his own restless sleep at the Inn, that [[Six]] had finished his blood-rite and was, by then, walking toward the same shack along the back streets, or that the rest of the company would shortly arrive at the same address from three directions and would, in the small hour after dawn, finally and formally be made what they had been recruited to be.

She walked the last hundred paces with the priest at her shoulder.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter covers the small hours before dawn and the early morning after the contract negotiation: [[Zant]]'s recovery of the unnamed purple-stone artifact from the squatters' house and his exit from [[Sahlen]] through the town gate; [[Cain]]'s morning return to [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s house to deliver the news of the giant-camp massacre, the dawn ambush of the shack by enemy soldiers, and the introduction of Ezericht's homemade muscle-stilling tincture [[Stillvein]]; [[Six]]'s solitary blood-rite at the Inn; and [[Trinity Stolen]]'s dream-driven return to Sahlen to recruit the priest who had healed the city at the second wave.

The chapter closes on the moment Trinity and Zant came in sight of Ezericht's house — the four signatories of the previous night, plus their newly recruited fifth, converging from four directions on the place where the contracts would be signed.
