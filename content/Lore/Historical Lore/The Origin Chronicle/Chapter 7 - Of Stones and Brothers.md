# Chapter 7 — Of Stones and Brothers

> *In which a contract is signed by those who remained, a sword is reclaimed from the floor of an empty room, a chain divides into four, a stone is lost to a creature with wings, and a man returns home to a castle in the swamp.*

---

## I. The Empty Chairs

When Edward and Ezericht left the shack, [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] did not chase them.

She had felt the strangeness in their departure with the part of her perception that catalogued such things, and she had decided — almost before [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s back had fully crossed the doorframe — that the cost of pursuit exceeded the cost of letting them go. *Better now,* she had thought, *than before the contract is signed.* She had taken the feather from the table and signed her own name on the mission contract and on the blood contract in the patient hand of a woman who had performed the same ceremony many times before, and the chronicle preserves the moment, because the moment matters: by the time the door of the armory office closed behind Edward and Ezericht, Trinity's signature was already on the page.

[[Cain]] watched the empty chairs with the particular disengaged interest of a man who had not, in his career, ever been surprised to find that someone he had been working with had run.

*"Very well, then,"* he said. *"I believe those two have made it increasingly clear that they have gone cold on this contract. All for the better, I say. I'm not one to work in large groups. Others tend to drag me behind. The faster this mission is over, the better it will be."*

He looked at the woman at the window.

*"So — what now, monk?"*

[[Six]] crossed the floor to Cain and laid a hand briefly on his shoulder — a gesture Cain accepted with the patient stillness of a man not in the habit of being touched and not, on this occasion, prepared to make a thing of it.

*"Don't worry,"* Six said. *"I'll try not to slow you down too much. I promise."*

He turned to Trinity.

*"But I think he's right. I don't believe they're coming back. So — is this going to change the mission any? Or not? And if it does — how much of a change will it be?"*

---

## II. The Old Sword

[[Zant]] said nothing.

The chronicle records that he had not, since the departure of the runners, lifted his eyes from the table. He read the mission contract twice through — once at the speed of a man learning what he had been brought into, and once at the speed of a man checking the work against the manner of its phrasing. He found nothing he had not been prepared to find. He took the feather from the table and signed the contract in a clean unhurried hand.

He set the feather back down.

He did not, again, speak.

Six, in the meantime, had crossed the floor of the shack and stopped before the place where [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] had — the previous evening, in the heat of his argument about the giant — driven the old sword into the floorboards and let it stand.

The sword was still standing.

Six considered it. He laid his hand on the hilt, gave a slow experimental pull to test the depth, and pulled it free in a single motion.

*"Hmm,"* he said. *"I wonder if he'll mind if I take this."*

He tested the balance against his palm.

*"Oh well. I'm taking it anyway. It might prove useful later on."*

He slid the old sword through his belt where the cross hung. He turned, again, to Trinity.

*"So,"* he said. *"What's the plan?"*

The chronicle does not record whether Ezericht's adopted father's blade was the most useful instrument Six carried out of [[Sahlen]] that morning. The chronicle records that it was, in any case, the one he carried out.

---

## III. The Compass and the Chain

Trinity drew a long breath.

She had, during the silent passage of the night's contracts, been thinking. She had been told by the Gheronda — in the second of the two presence-conveyed messages of the previous evening — that the rescue of the King had become, in the assessment of the order, a matter of breadth rather than of depth. The Gheronda wished the search dispersed. The Gheronda wished the search dispersed *now.*

Trinity had agreed.

*"Since we lost two people,"* she said, *"the plan will be simple. We will divide and search for the King — or for any information related to him — across the lands of Dyradia. Each of us shall take a section."*

She lifted her right hand. From under the sleeve of her robe she drew a fine chain that had lain along her forearm in the manner of a bracelet. She set it on the table.

She whispered a word the chronicle has not preserved.

The chain divided itself into four parallel strands, each lying separately on the wood. She picked them up by turn.

*"This is a magically bound chain,"* she said. *"It will serve as a communication route. Not only that, but if one of us is in danger, all that is needed is to send a distress signal. What that will do is — it will transport the person who is most beneficial to the situation as a replacement for the one in danger, and vice versa. As soon as the danger is cleared, the one who was sent will return to where they were."*

She paused, and added, in the tone of a woman who had used the instrument before and had learned what it cost:

*"I would recommend not using the signal, since it could be troublesome for both parties."*

She extended one of the chains to Cain.

*"Mr. Cain — the north."*

She extended one to Six.

*"Mr. Six — the south."*

She extended one to Zant.

*"Mr. Zant — the east. I, the west. The center area will go to whichever of us finishes our section first."*

She closed her own chain along her wrist.

*"What we are trying to accomplish is to gather any information that will help us locate the King — or, if possible, to reach him directly. If fighting can be avoided, please do so."*

She looked at each of them in turn.

*"You may leave and start already. Please inform me of any new information. Are there any questions?"*

Zant inclined his head.

*"Sounds like a good plan to me."*

He took the chain. He stood.

*"Well. I will be off to the east, and find out as much as I can."*

He walked out of the shack into the morning sun.

---

## IV. East

He did not, when he stepped into the daylight, head for the road.

He waited until he was clear of the line of sight from the shack — until the standing undead at the corners of the yard had ceased to register him as anything to attend to — and drew from the leather pouch at his hip the purple stone he had taken from the squatters the night before.

He held it in his open palm.

The stone caught the morning sun. The deep uneasy purple at its core brightened — slowly at first, and then, as if it had recognized what he meant by holding it — in a single hard burst of light that turned the air around his hand white. He did not, when the burst came, close his eyes. The chronicle records that he had used the stone before. He knew how to hold it.

He vanished from the side street of Sahlen.

He reappeared, in the same heartbeat, in a small village.

The eastern village had not yet woken to morning, because in the eastern village it was not yet morning. The sun he had left behind in Sahlen was here below the horizon. The moon was high. The stone in his hand, when he opened the leather pouch to return it, was glowing the bright uneasy purple of a thing that had recently been asked to do work. He returned it to the pouch. He drew the pouch's cord tight. The light dimmed.

He looked at the silent village.

*"It's late,"* he said, mostly to himself. *"I'd better get some sleep for a good start in the morning."*

He walked to the nearest inn.

It was deserted.

The front door had been left unlocked; the desk-bell was not at the desk; the candles in the wall sconces of the lobby were unlit. He called, into the empty hall, the standard greeting of a traveller who did not particularly wish to wake anyone and did not, particularly, expect anyone to answer.

His voice echoed back to him from the dark halls.

*"Guess not,"* he said. *"Oh well. I hope they don't mind me staying the night."*

He walked down the corridor.

He had not gone twenty paces when he heard — at the very edge of his hearing — a sound that did not belong to an inn.

He drew the staff from his back, drew the stone from the pouch, and chanted. The tip of the staff lit with the bright clean white light of a thing intended to expose anything in the corridor that did not, by daylight, want to be exposed.

At the far end of the hall stood a small thing.

It had wings. The wings were folded and were leathery and looked, in the white light of his staff, like the wings of a small bat with a body it did not quite fit. Its skin was a dark muddy red, almost brown. Its eyes were wide and startled.

It saw him.

It jumped — or rather, the chronicle observes, it leapt the entire length of the corridor in a single bound that no body of its size ought to have been capable of — and brushed against the front of his coat.

He yelled. He pointed the staff and fired the column of fire at it on instinct. The column of fire went wide, missed the creature by several feet, and ignited a curtain at the far end of the hall.

The creature did not run.

It laughed.

The laugh was small and high and unmistakeably pleased with itself, and it came, the chronicle records, from the place on Zant's coat that the creature had only just brushed.

Zant looked down.

The leather pouch was gone from his hip.

*"Damn it,"* he said. *"That bastard got my stone."*

The imp-thing — for the chronicle, lacking a better word, calls it that — turned and was gone in a small puff of dark smoke that smelled, faintly, of sulphur. The smoke cleared in a heartbeat. The pouch was not on the floor. The pouch was not anywhere.

Zant stood in the corridor for a long moment.

*"Not again,"* he said.

The despair in his voice was particular. The chronicle understands, from the manner of it, that he had lost the stone — or its like — before. To whom, and how many times, and at what costs, the chronicle does not yet record.

He looked down the corridor in the direction the imp had gone.

He looked back at the lit curtain.

He extinguished the curtain with a small unhurried gesture, walked to the nearest room with a closed door, opened it, lay down on the bed inside, and was — within a span the chronicle considers improbable for a man who had just lost a thing that mattered — asleep.

The stone, he had decided, would have to wait until morning.

---

## V. South

[[Six]] left the shack at his own pace.

He did not, when he left, head out through the front door. He went out through the side of the shack into the small lane between Ezericht's house and the next, where the standing undead at the corners of the yard could not, by the line of sight, see him leave. He had not, in any conversation of the previous night, told the company that he had a means of his own for crossing distance.

He had not, in any conversation of his life, told a company anything he had not been required to.

He stopped in the lane. He drew from a pocket of his coat a small object that resembled — in size, in shape, in the soft *click* of its lid — a pocket watch. The face beneath the lid was not, however, a pocket watch's face. It bore a single small button at its center and three concentric rings of unfamiliar markings around the button.

He pressed the button.

The chronicle records: Six was no longer in the lane.

---

## VI. The Castle in the Swamp

He arrived in a hall.

The hall was old. The walls had been stone and were now stone-with-moss; the roof had been timber and was now timber-with-vines; the floor had been flagstone and was now flagstone-with-the-slow-encroachment-of-the-mud-of-a-swamp. The smell was the smell of a place where standing water had not, in living memory, been still. Outside the high narrow windows of the hall, the chronicle understands, the swamp extended in every direction for an unmeasured distance.

The castle had been, at some point in its history, a battlefield.

It had not, since, been repaired.

*"Father!"* Six called into the long dim space of the hall. *"Father, I'm home!"*

There was a beat of silence.

Then there was a hearty laugh — the unmistakable laugh of an old man who had been waiting for the call, who had heard the front door open from somewhere in the structure he kept and had been on his way before he had been asked — and Six's father came down the long corridor toward him.

The father was small. The chronicle records this because it is, in context, significant: he was significantly shorter than Six, with the slightly bent posture of a long-lived man who had stopped at some point standing fully upright and had not begun again. His face was lined. His eyes, beneath the white of his brows, were the same faint red as Six's eyes when the morning sun caught them at a certain angle.

He clapped his son on the shoulder.

*"Well, well. If it isn't Six."*

The old man looked along the empty corridor behind him, then back at his son.

*"You wouldn't have happened to see your brothers out there, would you? They've been gone for quite some time now."*

Six shook his head.

*"No, Father. I haven't seen any of them. I've been rather busy of late."*

He laid the cross-and-old-sword down on a long low table inside the hall.

*"Right now I'm on a mission to find the King for the Church. They're paying me a good amount to find him too. But for now — I think I'd like to get some rest."*

The old man nodded with the patience of a man who had heard, in his long life, many such introductions to long stories.

*"Of course, mi' boy. Of course. But I expect you to tell me more about this mission when you're all rested up. Until then — you know where your room is. Good night."*

He patted Six's arm once and let him go.

Six walked down the corridor to his room and slept.

---

## VII. The Brothers

The father did not sleep.

The chronicle records that he sat at the long low table in the hall and laid his hand on a small unobtrusive switch of bone set into the wood — a switch he had, by the chronicle's understanding, set into the wood himself in the years before his sons had been old enough to use their own teleport-devices, when he had still, occasionally, called them home.

He pressed the switch four times.

In four distant places of Dyradia, four small bracelets each pulsed once.

---

### Natas

The first of the brothers was wandering the streets of an old deserted town somewhere in the south. He felt the bracelet pulse against his wrist and stopped where he stood.

*"So,"* he said, to no one in particular. *"Father wants me home, does he? I figured he would have given up by now."*

He considered. The pulse came again, sharper.

*Although — the pulse is getting stronger. Maybe I should see what he wants. Maybe Six came home finally.*

He drew from his coat a pocket-watch identical to Six's, pressed the button at its center, and was gone.

Natas's clothing was red. The chronicle records this because the colour mattered: each of the brothers wore the color of his element, and the elements were known by their colors as much as by their names. Natas's element was fire. He carried a sword.

His temperament, the chronicle records, was *wild and crazily erratic*.

### Marxus

The second of the brothers was swimming in the ocean to the south of the kingdom, where the warm currents that ran along the southern continental shelf made for long unhurried swims that did not, in his case, require him to surface. He felt the bracelet pulse against his wrist.

*Hmmm. Father wants me home, I see. Maybe Six is there. Oh how wonderful that would be. We haven't had all of us together for so long.*

He climbed to the shore, drew his pocket-watch, and pressed the button.

Marxus's clothing was blue. His element was water. He carried a trident.

His temperament was *calm but easily stirred to anger*.

### Nazu

The third of the brothers was already in the castle, in the upper rooms, where the old man's switch reached him as a particularly insistent series of pulses and not — as the device had been designed — as a single signal to come home.

*"Damnit, dad,"* he called down the stairs. *"Stop pushing that god-damned button that makes these god-damned bracelets pulse. It's getting on my god-damned nerves! And I know you have control over who the pulses go to. So stop sending one to me. I'm already here!"*

Nazu's clothing was green. His element was wind. He carried a scythe.

His temperament was, in the chronicle's words, *extremely erratic but easier to follow than Natas.*

### Zin

The fourth of the brothers was in the cellar of the castle, where he had been keeping company with whatever he had been keeping company with in the cellar of a castle in a swamp, and which the chronicle does not particularly wish to specify.

He came up the stairs at the call of the pulse.

*"Yeah, dad? What do you want?"*

Zin's clothing was brown. His element was earth. He carried an axe.

His temperament was *immensely calm.*

---

### Father's Errand

The old man gathered the four of them in the hall.

He spoke quickly. He did not wake Six, who had been asleep perhaps a quarter of an hour and looked, in the room down the corridor, as if he had not slept properly in some weeks.

*"Alright, boys. I've called you all home — or out of bed — to tell you that you just missed Six. He left a couple hours ago saying how he was on a mission for the Church."*

He looked at each of them.

*"My request to you boys is — will you go and tell me who it is he's working with? Or who might be a problem for his mission? If you find anyone that might be a problem, either eliminate them, or tell Six about them. That is all. You can leave now."*

Natas grinned.

*"So — we might get to kill someone? I'm in."*

He turned and walked out.

Marxus folded his arms across his chest.

*"Well — if it's to help Six, I suppose I can do it. Any idea where some of the others in the group might be?"*

The old man considered.

*"Well — according to the information Six gave me, one went to the west, one to the north, him to the south, and one to the east. I suppose you guys can split up and take the other three directions."*

Natas, who had walked back into the hall from the corridor, called over his shoulder:

*"Umm — I'll take the west. Someone else get the town."*

He walked back out.

Marxus inclined his head.

*"Alright. I'll get the east, then."*

He walked out.

Nazu cracked his knuckles.

*"I'll check out the north, then. Guess that leaves Zin to check out the town."*

He walked out.

Zin watched them go with the patience of a man whose temperament was the slowest of the four.

*"Alright. I guess I'll check out this town, then. I'll be back once I'm done, pops. Try not to miss us too much."*

He walked out.

The old man stood alone in his hall.

He looked, for a long unhurried moment, in the direction of the corridor where his son was sleeping.

Then he sat down again at the long low table, and waited for the morning Six would, in his own time, choose to come down into.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the dispersal of the company from [[Sahlen]] after the contract signing: [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s eventual signature; [[Zant]]'s quiet signing; [[Six]]'s appropriation of the old sword [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] had planted in the floor of his own house the previous night; Trinity's division of Dyradia into four search territories by the magically-bound chain that split into four bracelets; [[Zant]]'s teleportation to an unnamed eastern village by the purple stone he had taken from the squatters; the loss of the stone to a small winged creature in the corridor of the deserted eastern inn; and [[Six]]'s return — by his father's pocket-watch contraption — to the castle in the swamp where his father lived.

The chapter introduces [[Six's Father]] (no proper name preserved in the chronicle) and Six's four brothers — [[Natas]] (fire), [[Marxus]] (water), [[Nazu]] (wind), and [[Zin]] (earth) — each carrying a teleport-device of the same make as Six's, each bound to his element by color and by soul-stone, and each dispatched by the father to assist Six's mission.

The chronicle observes — without resolving — that Six had once described his own coming-into-being to a barfly at the Sahlen Inn with the phrase *"first 'born'"* in audible quotation. The exact mechanism by which Six and his brothers came to share a father, a face, and four mutually exclusive elemental dominions is not yet specified.
