# Chapter 2 — The Monk and the Mission

> *In which the company gains a healer, learns who let the soldiers in, and is offered a contract that will take them far from Sahlen.*

---

## I. Two Strangers

The tavern door had opened twice in quick succession.

The first man through it was the one who would shortly give his name as [[Zant]] — an elvish-featured monk in travelling robes, with a quiet step and an old book at his hip. He went to the barkeep, ordered two pints, took a table within hearing of the corner, and sat down to listen.

The second man through the door was a giant.

The chronicler will not name him here, because the company present did not name him then, and what they knew of him at that moment was only what their eyes told them: a man of unusual stature, in proportions Mehrendian custom recognized as belonging to a kind that some called by their proper word and others did not call by anything kinder. He took a place at the opposite side of the room and did not speak.

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s face changed.

He stared across the tavern, his glass forgotten at his lips, and his free hand drifted toward the dagger at his belt with the slow inevitability of a man whose mind was no longer making decisions about it.

*"I haven't seen one of them here in ages,"* he said quietly to the table. *"What's he doing here? Watch yourselves. They are never up to any good."*

He took another drink, but it did not help. Whatever he saw across the tavern was not the man at the table; it was something older, and it had teeth in him. His knuckles whitened around the dagger's hilt.

*If you make one wrong move —*

His head fell forward.

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] caught him at the shoulder before he struck the table, pinched his neck in the practiced place that returned blood to a swimming head, and lowered him gently back into his chair.

*"Control yourself,"* Edward said. *"I'll go see who that is making all the fuss."*

He stood, drew his pint with him, and crossed the room toward the bar.

---

## II. The Monk

Edward did not raise his voice as he came to the bar; he did not need to. There is a particular quietness a man develops when he has been paid to be careful, and Edward had it.

*"You are not citizens of this town,"* he said to Zant. *"Explain yourselves or you will have to leave this bar. We are fresh off an attack, and outsiders are not appreciated."*

He set the empty pint down.

*"Out with it."*

Zant did not bristle. *"The name is Zant. I have no relation to the attack that happened today. I'm a traveling monk, and I have my own reason why."*

He turned toward the room and lifted his voice, just a little, so that the company at the corner table heard him also.

*"Have any of you been injured during the battle? I can heal you, if I must."*

[[Six]] sat at the corner table taking it all in. He did not look at Zant, or at the giant. He sipped from his cup, set it down, and watched the doorway behind them, because the part of him that had been a sentinel before he had been anything else had felt the air change.

He stood.

He walked to the tavern door, opened it, and looked into the street.

*"Mm,"* he said. *"Not to kill the mood or anything. But there are enemy soldiers out here."*

---

## III. The Second Wave

Ezericht came back to himself with a long sigh, gulped the last of his drink, found his sword in his hand without remembering when he had reached for it, and stood up.

*"These guys are relentless,"* he said. *"Well, Z. Six. Looks like we have more fun ahead of us."*

He kicked the tavern door open and was already in the street before the second figure of the second wave had registered him. A soldier was setting a torch to a thatch roof a hundred paces north. Ezericht crossed the distance in seconds and made it not happen.

Edward turned to the giant. *"Help or I gut you,"* he said — flat, without theatre, in the voice of a man who was busy.

He turned to Zant. *"Stay hidden and start healing whoever comes through that door. I need one of them alive."*

He drew the broadsword from his back and went outside. *"Move it, people. We have a town to save."*

The first soldier he met across the threshold lost an arm to Edward's first stroke; Edward dragged him backward through the door by the remaining one and dropped him at Zant's feet. *"Keep him."* Then he was back in the street.

---

## IV. The Storm

Zant had not, at first, answered Edward's orders. He had bristled — *"Who are you to order me around?!"* — but he had also drawn his book.

He chanted.

The bleeding soldier on the floor stilled, eyes glazing, body holding to life only by the thinnest of the monk's thread. Above the rooftops the air thickened. The first drops fell on the burning thatch in the north, and the second came harder; within a minute the storm Zant had drawn from the book was upon the entire city. Torches drowned. Flames hissed and went out.

Zant stepped out into the street and stretched his hand into the rain.

A bolt of lightning came down where he pointed it. The squad in the southern road went over like cut wheat.

Six, in the meantime, had walked into the largest concentration of enemy soldiers as a man might walk into a room he already owned. He looked up at the tavern roof. *"Get down here, you dumb things,"* he called, and the thralls he had raised at the South Gate came down from the rooftop in a single rolling wave. Many were shot from the air by enemy archers on the descent. Those who landed kept on doing what Six had told them to do.

He turned to find arrows in his back.

It was the first time the company had seen him angry. He charged the bowmen at a sprint that should not have been possible from a man already wounded; he killed two by hand, and the third by burying his old dagger through the man's face.

*"You don't shoot me when my back's turned,"* he said to the third soldier, who was no longer in a position to answer.

Ezericht charged the largest knot of remaining soldiers with both blades drawn and built a quick spin into the wedge of them, taking three at a stroke. He sheathed one blade and drove the other through a man rushing him, then sheathed that one too as he saw what was coming through the gate.

A horse. A rider. Heavy.

*"Z!"* he called. *"Take down the horse, and I will help you kill the rider!"*

---

## V. The Rider

Edward had been making his way to Ezericht when the call came. He swore under his breath — *that's a big horse* — and ran instead at the animal, straight through the rain and the mud.

He took a sharp turn before he met it.

The horse, startled, planted its forelegs and went sideways. Edward used the half-second the rider needed to recover, slid in close along the horse's flank, and opened its throat with the broadsword. The horse went down. The rider did not; the rider dismounted at speed with the practiced violence of a man for whom dismounting was an attack, and threw Edward clear across the street before Edward had finished the second motion of his blade.

Zant turned his book to Edward and chanted again; Edward's wounds knit enough to stand on.

Then the monk went back into the tavern, took up the staff he had left on the table, and pointed its tip at a knot of infantry in the next street. The end of the staff opened, and a column of fire incinerated five of them where they stood.

Ezericht had reached the rider.

He swung and the rider caught the blade in his bare hand. Ezericht pulled the second sword and made a second stroke and the rider blocked that one too. Then the rider hit him, once, and Ezericht went three paces back into the mud.

He picked himself up.

*"Z!"* he called. *"Use your arrow and aim for his neck — NOW!"*

He threw one of his swords. It went into the rider's left thigh to the hilt. The rider looked down at it with the patience of a man considering a stain on his coat, pulled it out, and kept coming.

Edward had recovered his bow from the tavern roof in the interim and had it strung again on the high ground. He took the shot Ezericht had called. The arrow went into the rider's neck and out the other side, and the rider — though he stood for a long moment as if the matter were not yet decided — finally went down.

Edward came down the wall, walked across the wet street, and clapped Ezericht on the shoulder as he passed.

*"Nice goin' there, ace,"* he said. He did not stop. He had a captive to check on.

---

## VI. The Field

The rain slowed.

Six let his thralls fall back into stillness with a gesture of one hand, walked among the dead, and found what he was looking for: the largest of the fallen, a man whose body would carry what Six wished to make of it. He removed the cross from his neck, added a measure of the man's blood to its inner reservoir, recorked it, and let the rest of the blood run out into the gutter.

He returned to the tavern.

Edward had the wounded prisoner sitting against the wall. The man's arm was gone above the elbow and Zant had kept him alive only just enough to remain alive. Edward looked up as Six entered.

*"Maybe the big guy broke through the gate,"* Six said, glancing toward the South Gate. *"Or they had a ram of some sort. We won't know until we check it out."*

Edward nodded. He stood, took the prisoner under the remaining arm, and dragged him to the table. He threw him across it to Six.

*"That's why I wanted this one alive,"* he said. *"However, I have the feeling he won't be able to say no to **you**."*

Six considered the prisoner with the expression of a craftsman receiving stock.

*"You interrogate him,"* Edward said. *"We have someone sneaking around outside."*

He took up his sword and went out.

---

## VII. The Interrogation

The chronicler does not record what [[Six]] did to the prisoner in the tavern, post by post and cut by cut, because the chronicle is not the place for that detail and the company present did not, afterward, wish it written down.

What can be written is this:

Six asked the prisoner how the soldiers had come through the gate.

Six asked the prisoner more than once.

What Six did between askings was sustained, methodical, and conducted with the patience of a man who had done the same thing in other places to other men, and who had learned which questions broke a soldier and in what order. He used a small pouch of instruments he carried for the purpose, and four of his thralls to hold the prisoner in the positions required, and a fifth who stood by in case any of the other four needed replacing.

The prisoner did not, at first, talk.

He talked, in the end.

The answer was this: a member of Sahlen's own city guard had opened the gate from the inside.

Six let the prisoner die when the prisoner had nothing further to give him. He cleaned the instruments, returned them to their pouch, and was sitting at the corner table again, drinking from the cross-fed cup, when Ezericht walked back into the tavern.

Ezericht took one look at the mangled corpse on the floor and exhaled.

*"I surely hope you got some answers out before he died,"* he said. *"Remind me to stay on your good side."*

Six told him. Ezericht heard the part about the guard with the air of a man whose suspicions had merely been confirmed.

*"So — a guard let him in. Sounds farfetched, but I suppose it's possible. I guess we should take some time to question the guards and see who was responsible."*

He returned to his corner. He returned to drinking.

---

## VIII. The Watcher in the Tower

Edward, when he had left the tavern, had not been chasing nothing.

There was a watcher.

She had been on the north tower since before the second wave arrived, and she had stayed there through it, observing the company in their defense and reaching, in the moments after the rider went down, a quiet conclusion. She lifted one of the necklaces she wore and laid it on the stone of the tower in front of her — and the necklace, which was not in fact a necklace at all, uncoiled and became something so small as to be unfindable.

*"Shift,"* she whispered to it. *"Deliver a message to the Gheronda. Tell him we have found something promising."*

The creature that had been a necklace listened.

*"Oh — and almost forgot. Tell him that by promising I mean these men are more likely to live longer than the last. But they might die before the mission is completed."*

The thing called Shift moved off into the air and was gone.

She turned back to the tavern, and saw that Edward was already coming.

She made no move to hide.

*Men are such alcohol-lusting pigs,* she thought as she watched him approach. *Well — at least these ones can fight. If you can even call it that.*

---

## IX. The Approach

Edward came toward the tower from the angle that did not show him to its lookouts. He heard the woman whispering before he saw her, which was unusual; he heard only one voice, which was unusual also.

He stopped beneath the tower and called up to the dark.

*"You breathe awfully heavily for a monk. At least come down where I can see you."*

He paused.

*"Oh, and it is quite rude to talk about people without their knowledge. But anyway — how well does this 'mission' pay, and what do you need me to do?"*

The figure on the tower jumped.

She landed cleanly in front of him in a single drop, robe and hood unmoved.

*"I apologize for my lack of manners,"* she said. *"But if I am not wrong, last time I checked, hearing other people's conversations was also considered… rude."*

*Though the fact that you managed to hear my whispered conversation from so far away is intriguing.*

*"My name is Trinity,"* she said. *"And as you heard, I have a mission which I would like you and your companions to assist me in. However, before I start speaking about the mission, I would like to be in a more secluded place where someone is less likely to hear what I am talking about. If that is possible, of course."*

Edward considered her.

*"It is also said that one wrong turn deserves another,"* he replied. *"But answering your question — yes, there is. But I have to go get someone. Please follow me."*

He turned without waiting and walked back to the tavern.

Inside, he paused at the prisoner's body, made a noise, and looked at Six.

*"Fill me in later. Make sure nothing else bad happens."*

He crossed to the corner.

He took Ezericht's pint out of his hand mid-sip, set it on the table, and lifted Ezericht out of his chair by the elbow.

*"You're needed,"* he said. *"Come on."*

He did not give Ezericht much choice.

The three of them walked back to a small, rickety house on a side street that Edward knew to be Ezericht's, and Edward held the door open for the monk.

*"This is secluded enough,"* he said. *"It's rickety enough that I can hear any unwanted visitors. Ladies first."*

---

## X. The Mission

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] dropped into his own chair, scowling at the floor.

*"Z, do you mind?! I would have come if you had just asked. So what if my house is rickety? It's all I have. And who is this woman?? Lord — someone fill me in on something!"*

The woman bowed slightly.

*"I am sorry for your lack of information, but that should be resolved in no time."*

She looked at the room. The chronicler notes that what she saw was a house Ezericht had lived in for some time, with the lived-in clutter of one who had stopped expecting better, and an old sword leaning against the far wall whose hilt was unfamiliar in style and craftsmanship to the rest of Mehrendian make.

She faced the two men.

*"My name is Trinity Stolen,"* she said, *"and I am from Hypostasis monastery of the west borders. The monastery, though religious, has long been involved in the ruling of Dyradia — secretly, nevertheless."*

She watched Edward's expression. It did not change.

*"As the gentleman here has heard, I have a proposition for you."*

She let the silence sit a moment.

*"I would like you to help me in the rescue of the King."*

She did not name the King. She did not need to. There was, in those years, only one whose rescue would be referred to in that register.

*"If you agree now, I shall go on and speak more of the mission and what you shall receive for your help."*

She looked between them.

*"So — what do you say?"*

Edward did not move. He had been waiting for this moment for the entire walk from the tower.

*"I am [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward Zwarf]],"* he said. *"This is Ezericht."*

He let a small pause sit there.

*"We'll help you,"* he said. *"For a price."*

The price he named was fifteen hundred gold each. Plus an additional five hundred each for any extra hands they might need to call on, which, given the company they now kept, would be more than two.

*"I know the monastery is not exactly 'pressed' for money,"* he added. *"And I believe these are reasonable."*

He leaned back. *"Other than that, I can start when I get a briefing. Any problems, Ez?"*

---

## XI. The Sword in the Floor

Ezericht had been doing the arithmetic. *Fifteen hundred gold? Plus more for our extra work?* It was a sum he had not, in his most generous imaginings, expected to see in a single year of his usual work, let alone a single fee.

Then he thought about the company.

*"That… giant won't be joining us, I hope?"*

Trinity considered him.

*"I don't see why not,"* she said. *"He does have the kind of brute strength that might be helpful on the mission. In fact, the more I think about it, the more I believe the idea of him being a great help. Would you have a problem if he were to join us, Mr. Ezericht?"*

Ezericht sighed — a sound from very deep, of the kind that lives in a man for a long time before it is heard — and his eye fell on the old sword leaning against the wall.

He picked it up.

He swung it once, slowly, to feel its weight in his hand. He drove the point of it into the floor of his own house, hard enough that the boards complained, and let go of the hilt.

The sword stood there, upright, the polished pommel level with his shoulder.

*"His kind aren't to be trusted, never again. I did once. But then —"*

He could not finish that sentence. He did not try.

*"They killed my family. The only family I have. ALL I have!"*

The volume of his voice surprised him.

*"I'll never forgive them. My family trusted them with anything. So — yes. I have a problem with it."*

He sat back down in his chair, breathing hard, and looked nowhere in particular.

Edward shot him a sharp glance.

*"Calm yourself."*

He turned his attention back to Trinity, with a thought he did not voice: *Got to watch these manipulative monks. Got to remind Ezericht to keep his guard up around them later.*

*"Also,"* he said aloud, *"in my experience, rescue missions need finesse more than strength. But I have an idea how we can use him. He will probably join cheaply, but we can't trust him with any details — he seems like a loose-tongued one. Now about that brief—"*

Edward stopped speaking.

He had been listening, throughout the conversation, to the sounds in the street outside the rickety house. There had been the usual sounds of a city after a fight: the slow settling, the calls of men checking on each other, the rain easing. And just now there had been a footstep that did not belong to those sounds.

He leaned slightly to the right.

*"Whoever that is thinks they are quiet,"* he whispered. *"Let them keep thinking that."*

He resumed, for Trinity's benefit, the cadence of the prior conversation.

*"Excuse me. As I was saying — I would like to hear more about this mission. A full briefing would be nice."*

He kept his eyes on Trinity's. He nodded his head, just barely, toward the door.

His hand rested on the hilt of his sword.

---

## XII. The Door

The footstep was [[Six]]'s.

He had finished his work at the tavern and had set out to find his companions, more out of habit and a quiet curiosity than any wish to interrupt them. He had spotted the rickety house at the end of a side street and had taken the long way around to it, because the long way was the way he took to things he was not certain of. As he approached the door, he removed the goggles he had worn through the second engagement.

His eyes, which had been ordinary all evening, were now faintly red.

It was, the chronicler is given to understand, a thing that happened to him after he had drawn blood at sufficient quantity. Whether this was the magic at work in him or something older, neither Six nor any companion of his ever clarified.

He reached the door and laid his hand on it.

He turned the handle.

Inside, Edward stood — silently, in the rhythm of one motion — drew the broadsword from his back, and swung at the figure entering through the door with the speed of a man who had already decided what was on the other side of it.

The blade stopped a hand's breadth from Six's neck.

Edward held it there for a moment, and then with a small disgusted breath let it down.

*"Don't do that."*

He sheathed the sword.

*"Well. Have a seat,"* he said, gesturing to the chair beside Ezericht's. *"We were discussing a… business opportunity with the monk. I think you would be quite interested."*

He stepped back, leaned against the wall, and kept one ear open to the street.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter reconstructs events following the South Gate raid: the arrival of the monk [[Zant]] and the unnamed companion remembered only as "the giant"; the second wave of attack on [[Sahlen]]; the storm and lightning called down by Zant's chants; the interrogation of a captured soldier from which [[Six]] obtained the confession that one of Sahlen's own city guards had opened the gate from the inside; and the introduction of [[Trinity Stolen]] of the [[Hypostasis Monastery]], whose proposition — the rescue of the King — would draw the company out of Sahlen and into the wider continent.

The chapter closes on the moment Edward Z Zwarf stopped a sword's-breadth short of beheading Six in the doorway of Ezericht's house, having mistaken his companion's quiet approach for an assassin's.

*Period source: the founding fragments preserved in The RP that Started it All, posts 22–55.*
