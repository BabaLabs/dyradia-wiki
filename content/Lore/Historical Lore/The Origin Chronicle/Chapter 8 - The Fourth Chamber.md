# Chapter 8 — The Fourth Chamber

> *In which a runner is sent, a hunter is conscripted, a dagger is brought to a door it was made to open, a King who already knew his son's face speaks the last words of his line, and a thing that no longer recognized its body comes out of a throne to follow them.*

---

## I. The Runner

When [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] released his grip on General Flitney's outstretched finger, the General did not, for several seconds, move.

He looked at Edward.

He looked at [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]].

He looked back at Edward.

*"You're on thin ground,"* he said. *"Again."*

He breathed.

*"Then again — you haven't been wrong before. No reason to think so now. Open the door a second."*

Edward reached over and opened the door of the armory office. From the corridor outside, a small uniformed runner stood at the unsuspecting attention of a man who had been told to wait outside an officer's door and had not, recently, been told anything further.

The General did not raise his voice. He simply made it carry.

*"PATRICK. Get me the Fourth Wall Division to the stables."*

Patrick froze.

*"NOW."*

The runner scampered.

Edward closed the door. He leaned against the wall.

*"Thank you, sir,"* he said over his shoulder. *"We'll return shortly with the corpse of a King."*

He turned to Ezericht.

*"Come on."*

---

## II. The Knock

[[Snap]] had spent the small hours of the morning, by the chronicle's reconstruction, in his own house — having returned there after the doorway-and-deer episode at [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s shack — and was, by the hour Patrick's run took him, asleep at his table with a dagger driven into the wood beside his hand.

The knock came.

Snap drove the dagger deeper into the table.

*"Can't I get some damn peace around here?"*

He stood. He crossed to the door.

*"Who is it?"*

The voice from the other side was breathless.

*"It is Patrick. You are required at the stables immediately."*

Snap exhaled.

*"I will be there."*

He pulled the dagger from the table. He took down the two hatchets from his belt-rack, the bow from its hook, and three quivers full of arrows from beside the door. He went, by the long-practiced method of a man who did not believe in front doors when side windows would do, out through the window.

He ran for the stables.

---

## III. The Stables

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] was already there when [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] caught up to him.

Ezericht walked among the rows of stalls until he found a horse he liked — a brown mare with a clean white stripe running down its head — and turned to Edward to confirm.

*"Mind if I take this one? It looks as though it could do some good."*

*"Yeah, that's fine,"* Edward said without looking. *"Head over there and make sure your equipment is good."*

The supplies were loaded. The cart that the stablemen had readied stood at the yard's centre, already overladen, its handler's seat empty. Edward stood at the cart's side, drumming his fingers along the rail. The Fourth Wall Division had not, by any measure of the hour or of his patience, arrived.

*Where the hell is that runner?*

He turned to one of the stable guards.

The guard shrugged.

Edward grit his teeth.

*They better be damned good if I have to wait like this.*

[[Snap]] arrived through the stable gate at a half-run, the bow strapped across his back, the hatchets at his belt, the quivers in one hand and the dragged-late breath of a man who had not been told the nature of his errand.

He looked around. He saw the cart. He saw Edward at the cart's side. He saw the heavily armored figures of two soldiers at the door of the General's office.

He went to one of the soldiers.

*"Who has called me here, and why?"*

The soldier nudged his chin toward Edward and Ezericht.

*"The General. You are to accompany these two on their trip."*

Snap thanked him and went to Edward.

*"What will we be doing, if you can tell me that much?"*

He paused, almost forgetting.

*"Oh — and I completely forgot. The people around here call me Snap."*

Edward grinned, briefly.

*"Well — 'Snap' yourself over to that cart and put your gear in it. You'll be filled in when there are fewer ears about."*

Then, without changing tone, he pointed past Snap at the two heavily armored men at the General's office door.

*"The rest of them are late. I'm reassigning you to this mission. Get your gear and send messengers to your families. Make it quick."*

He went to the cart and looked inside. The supplies he had quietly ordered the previous night to be ready for any contingency the morning produced had, he was relieved to see, made it from the Inn to the cart intact. He hopped onto the handler's seat and took the reins.

He looked out over the men at the cart-yard.

*"We're leaving in ten minutes,"* he said.

He let it hang.

*"My time. Which means whenever I think you should have been ready. Anyone who isn't done by then will be personally run over by myself and this overladen cart. Do I make myself clear?"*

*"YES, SIR!"* answered several of the men, shakingly.

The rest of them scampered.

[[Snap]], who had not yet stowed his gear in the cart, decided that he had forgotten something at his house. He ran back through the gate. He passed Patrick still standing in the courtyard.

*"What are you doing here still? Z is pissed at you for not getting the other people."*

He ran on. He let himself into his own house. He locked the door behind him out of long-trained habit. He grabbed his dried meat from the pantry, the two largest axes from the wall — axes whose heft and grain he had chosen for the work he had not, until that morning, been called to do — and went, again, out through the window.

He arrived back at the cart at speed. He vaulted into the back, set the axes down beside him, found a decently comfortable corner amid the crates and bedrolls, and waited.

Ezericht mounted the brown mare and rode it to the stable's edge.

*"You better go ahead of me,"* he said to Edward. *"After all — I have no idea where to go."*

Edward took the reins, clicked his tongue at the horses, and the cart began to move.

---

## IV. The Ride

They rode for hours.

The chronicle does not preserve every league of the ride. The country between [[Sahlen]] and the western base — for that was the destination, though Edward had not yet, by the time they cleared the city's western gate, named it — was rolling and forested in patches, and the road they took was not the road most travellers used. The Fourth Wall Division did not, in the end, ever arrive to the stables; the two heavily armored men Edward had reassigned rode behind the cart at half-cart-length, and the dust they raised did not, the chronicle suspects, draw the attention of anyone the company did not want it to.

The sun set in line with the road ahead of them.

Edward tied off the reins of the cart, stood up, and turned to face the men riding behind.

*"Alright, men. I'll keep this brief, as always."*

He folded his arms over the cart's back.

*"We're on a mission of regicide for the good of the Kingdom."*

He let that land.

*"We will go into the Western Barracks, and underground is a heavily guarded compound which should be secured by our men already — seeing as it is ours. Now — all you have to do — "*

He pointed back at the heavily armored men on horseback.

*" — is make sure he — "*

He pointed sideways at Ezericht.

*" — does not die."*

He resumed his position.

*"It is not as easy as it sounds. I've been on it for a good while and he can't seem to keep his bacon off the fire. However — should the enemy approach — I will most definitely require assistance. If this unlikely event should happen, there are many places where a couple of competent men could hold off a force. I suggest you tie to the cart and save your strength. You may need it."*

He sat down. He took the reins again.

*Yep,* he thought, *I'm in trouble. Oh well. I may actually not get reported for this one.*

He clicked his tongue.

---

## V. The Building That Was Bigger Inside

The Western Barracks resolved, in the distance, as what looked at first to be no more than a small outbuilding standing alone on a slow rise in the country west of Sahlen.

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] rode up alongside [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]].

*"Wow. Armed soldiers? Heavily guarded underground compound? What are you expecting — some kind of war? I mean — this IS the monastery we are dealing with. What kind of force could THEY have?"*

He squinted at the distant shape.

*"How far is it to the compound?"*

Edward pointed forward.

In the line of the pointing, Ezericht saw nothing more than the small building on the rise.

*Truly,* he thought, *it has to be bigger than that. They wouldn't hold the King in a shack — would they?*

They rode on.

When they had nearly reached the building, Ezericht's eyes finally resolved the structure for what it was. The small outbuilding on the rise was not a small outbuilding. It was a structure significant enough to house a small army, and the eye-distortion that had made it look small at distance — the chronicle suspects, though does not confirm — was a part of its concealment.

Ezericht dismounted. He nudged Edward in the ribs.

*"Don't have too many visitors here, do you? I was right in front of it and could barely see it."*

Edward laughed once, dryly.

*"Hey. It works to our advantage. We want as little attention as possible."*

He turned to the men. He ordered them inside.

They descended the main stairway into the lower levels.

The chronicle records, of the descent, the unusual order of the descent. The lower levels were not the cellars of an outbuilding. They were a working military facility, equipped as an armory and provisioned as a barracks. Ezericht passed weapon-racks and powder-stores and supply-shelves that had not, to his eye, been used recently, but had also not, to his eye, been allowed to fall out of use.

*They ARE expecting a fight,* he thought. *But the monastery is supposed to be a peace-loving people. Perhaps they are anticipating something I am not.*

He caught up to Edward.

*"Ok. So what am I to do now? I naturally assume it's not so simple as walk in, kill the King, and walk away. And why are we so well armed? Do you guys suspect something big from the monastery?"*

Edward did not, at first, answer.

He led Ezericht down a second flight of stone steps, through a winding maze of corridors that did not appear, to Ezericht's eye, to be following any particular geographical logic, and to a large door set into the deepest wall of the deepest hall.

The door was inscribed with runes.

The runes ran in a tight close-set pattern from the door's threshold to its lintel. They were of a kind Ezericht had not, before that morning, ever seen — and they were of a kind that, on closer look, matched in their fine spiraling style the etchings down the blade of the dagger he carried at his hip.

He did not, at that moment, ask Edward whether the match was deliberate.

He suspected he knew.

---

## VI. The Brother at the Gate

Outside the building, the chronicle records that a different conversation was in progress.

[[Zin]], who had been wandering Dyradia at his ordinary patient pace since his father had dispatched him to look for the company his brother [[Six]] had been working with, had — by an accident the chronicle does not pretend was anything else — caught sight of two strangers riding into the western country on a cart at the head of a small armed escort. The taller of the two had carried himself like a man with somewhere to be; the other had had a horse with a clean white stripe down its head; both had been bound, by the line of their travel, for the same building Zin had been considering walking past.

Zin had walked, instead, toward them.

He had reached the building's outer yard just as Edward led his men down the stairs.

He stood in the yard. He called out.

*"HEY! Do you guys know anything about my brother Six?"*

Edward, the chronicle records, did not at that moment have the spare attention required to deal sympathetically with a stranger asking after the brother of a man he had no intention of being usefully connected to.

He had, however, the trained instinct of an officer for the misdirection of unwanted parties.

He came back up the stairs by himself, leaving Ezericht and the men below.

*"Hi. I know exactly who you are talking about. Guy about yea high — quite difficult to kill. This is the headquarters of his mission. He headed southeast to a farm holding a fugitive. If you are looking to help him, go ahead and grab that person and take them to the jail in the capital."*

He smiled the small steady smile that he used when he wished his face to be remembered as helpful.

*"Might you need anything else?"*

Zin considered the directions.

*"Alright. Thank you. I think I'll go get that fugitive, then, if it'll help Six."*

He turned. He walked off down the road.

Edward called after him.

*"Thank you for your help!"*

He waited until Zin was well clear of the building. He went back inside, down the stairs, and resumed his place at Ezericht's side at the rune-inscribed door.

The chronicle records — without comment — that the farm to which Edward had directed Zin was an ordinary farm, that the old farmer who lived there had no relation to anything Six had been hired to do, and that the misdirection was Edward's calculated solution to a problem he could not, in the hour available, solve any other way.

The chronicle records, equally without comment, the consequence: that Zin walked the road to the farm, did exactly as Six had told him through their telepathic bond once the pulse from the King's death reached him, and left the farmer dead under a mass of summoned earth before continuing on, with the unhurried geomancer's patience, in a direction Edward had not directed him in.

But this is to anticipate.

---

## VII. The Wood

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] shoved the door-guards aside.

*"Ezericht — put the dagger against the door. It'll open up."*

He did not look at Ezericht when he said it.

*"The King is in the fourth chamber on the left. You will get one strike — and then you must get out of the room and back to this door. The guards and I will try to close it before — "*

He did not finish.

*"Well. Let's not discuss that. Just run fast."*

He stepped aside.

Ezericht drew the dagger from his hip.

He looked at it.

He had carried the blade for as long as he had carried anything in his life. He had cleaned it at night and at the end of fights; he had played with it across the backs of his hands in his usual tavern; he had carried it the night his foster family had been killed and he had carried it through the years of searching that had followed; he had carried it through the South Gate and the second wave and the alley and the morning and the armory office, and he had never, until this corridor and this door, known what it was for.

He raised it.

He did not, immediately, touch the wood.

Before the blade made contact, he felt — the chronicle preserves the sensation, because the chronicle was given to understand it mattered — a thing wake inside him. The thing did not have a name and did not need one. It was the part of his blood that the General had told him about that morning and that he had not, until that moment, been able to verify with his own body. The part of his blood that was royal.

The part of his blood that could open the door.

The feeling rose in him until he had to brace himself to hold the dagger steady. He almost — for the small breath of a moment that the chronicle preserves because Ezericht himself, in later years, was understood to preserve it — almost lowered the dagger.

He did not.

He laid the point against the wood.

The runes lit.

They lit from the bottom of the door upward in a slow sustained illumination that ran along each spiral and along each inscribed character of the dagger's blade in tandem, until the door's runes and the dagger's etchings were singing the same light in the same key. The hall around the door rang briefly with a sound that no one in it could afterward describe but no one in it ever forgot.

The door, when the light had reached its lintel, became semi-transparent.

Ezericht stepped back. He pressed his palm against the wood. The palm went through. The wood, when he withdrew his hand, was wood again — but with the part of itself that had opened to him remaining open.

He looked at Edward.

Edward nodded.

Ezericht ran.

---

## VIII. The Fourth Chamber on the Left

He did not slow at the first three chambers.

He had been told four. He had been told left. He held the dagger out before him in the loose ready grip of a man who knew that if he stopped to look into any of the wrong chambers he might lose what little time he had been given.

He passed the first.

He passed the second.

He passed the third.

He stopped at the fourth.

The chamber on the left at the fourth count was wide and low-ceilinged and held a chair at its far wall that was not a chair so much as a throne — a throne of stone with a high back and a worked frame whose detailing Ezericht did not, in the press of the moment, particularly examine. On the throne sat a man.

The man was unhurried.

He was holding a glass of wine.

He looked up at Ezericht, into the doorway and across the chamber's distance, and he looked at him for a long even moment. Whatever he saw in that moment — the dagger, the scar, the bloodline behind the scar — was, to him, expected.

He spoke.

*"I see. I was wondering when you would show up — Ezericht."*

He set the glass of wine on the arm of the throne.

*"How unfortunate we had to meet up like this. But I suppose action has to be taken. Be quick, and strike swiftly. There isn't time for doubt and questions."*

The chronicle preserves the sentence that followed because it has been preserved, in the years since, by everyone who has had cause to remember it.

*"Just know — that what has happened, happened for a reason."*

---

## IX. The Strike

[[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] crossed the chamber.

He did not run. He did not slow. He crossed the distance in the steady unhurried walk of a man who had been told, by the very voice he had crossed the country to silence, that there was no time for doubt and that he should be quick.

He gripped the dagger.

He rested its tip at the King's heart.

He looked into the King's eyes.

The eyes that looked back at him were the eyes of a man who had loved more women than he should have, who had lost the only one whose loss he had carried, who had signed a contract he should not have signed, who had been hidden in an underground room he had not chosen, and who had, in the eight months of his captivity, made some peace with whatever was coming through the door of his chamber.

Ezericht breathed in.

He lunged.

The dagger went in beneath the breastbone at the angle Edward had — without ever having said so — taught him in the alley at the South Gate, the alley behind the shack, and the corridor that morning. The chronicle does not record whether the angle was inherited or learned. The chronicle records that it worked.

The King gasped.

His face did not, the chronicle observes, change in the way faces in stories often change when they have been struck. It eased. The breath he took with the dagger in him was not a fighting breath. It was the breath of a man for whom the weight had been lifted at the moment the weight was placed.

Ezericht almost pulled the dagger out.

He stopped.

*It needs to be done. Too many things are at stake here.*

He pushed.

He shoved the blade further in, pinning the King's back to the stone of the throne behind him.

The King's hand came up. His fingers closed in the sleeve of Ezericht's shirt at the elbow. The grip was — the chronicle records — neither attacking nor pleading. It was the grip of a man holding on, for one final moment, to the person in the room with him.

The grip relaxed.

The hand fell.

It came to rest on the King's lap.

Ezericht pulled the dagger back.

He nearly dropped it.

The hilt — which had been the cool metal of the morning, of the armory, of every morning since he had been able to remember himself holding it — was, abruptly, *hot.* Not warm. Hot, with the particular ungiving heat of a thing that had taken something into itself it had been waiting a long time to take.

*What the hell?*

He picked it up again before it could fall, and he sheathed it before it could burn him.

He looked, for the last time, at the man on the throne.

He turned.

He walked toward the door of the chamber.

---

## X. The Spectre

He had not, when he turned, expected anything further to happen.

The chronicle observes that — in retrospect — he should have.

A rumble began behind him. He heard it before he understood it. The throne where the King had been began to glow with a deep low light that did not come from any of the wall-sconces and did not match the light the runes on the door had given off. Something rose out of the body.

It was not, at first, a shape.

It became a shape.

It was not — the chronicle is careful — *the King*. The King was on the throne. The King was no longer in the room. What rose was a thing the King had been wearing, in the spell-mesh of his bloodline-protections, like a coat: a coat that had not understood that the body beneath it was no longer alive, and was now coming out of the body as if the body had become a door it could no longer remain inside.

It screamed.

The sound was not — the chronicle struggles for the word and does not find one — *audible* in the ordinary register. It was audible the way a thing one had been remembering for a long time was suddenly audible when it occurred. Ezericht clapped his hands to his ears.

*This is why I have to hurry.*

He ran.

He sprinted out through the door of the fourth chamber, past the third and second and first, and burst into the rune-inscribed door at the corridor's mouth as the door's transparency held — by a margin of perhaps half a breath — long enough for him to dive through it.

The guards slammed the door shut behind him.

The wood went solid.

The runes went dark.

On the other side of the door, the chronicle records, the screaming continued.

Ezericht lay on the floor of the corridor, breathing hard.

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] crouched beside him.

*"Z…"* Ezericht said. *"Z, what was that?"*

---

## XI. Out

Edward did not, immediately, answer.

He helped Ezericht to his feet.

*"That,"* he said, *"was a spectre. The tunnel and surrounding areas are to collapse in order to contain the body and limit the range of the spectre. Also — you might want to take the dagger out of the sheath before it catches on fire. I'll explain that more when we have time, but now we have to go. Quickly."*

He turned to the door-guards.

*"MEN — MOVE OUT!"*

He sprinted up the corridor with Ezericht behind him.

When they reached the ground level of the base he was already shouting orders.

*"Take some miners and get that tunnel collapsed at as many points as possible. The guys who came with me — get back to the cart we came in on, ASAP. I want a platoon to follow us back to the capital. Put the base on full alert. Double patrols."*

He went out through the yard. The cart waited. He climbed onto the handler's seat and grabbed the reins. He turned to the men.

*"This might get bad really fast. The enemy is sure to know what has happened, and we are still not sure of the extent of their powers. We must be on guard at all ti — "*

He stopped speaking.

He pointed at the ground.

Off to the southeast, in a wide low region of plain country that should have been still in the evening light, the ground was *moving*. It was moving the way the surface of a pond moves when a stone has been thrown into it: in slow concentric rings.

There was no stone in the pond.

Edward did not finish the sentence he had been beginning.

*"What the hell is that?"*

He set his teeth.

*"I don't want to find out. Drop excess weight, but keep anything we could defend ourselves with."*

He turned the cart east.

He whipped the reins. He screamed at the horses with all of his might. The horses pulled at the load behind them and the cart began to move, slowly at first and then faster, and the men with him rode close at the cart's flanks.

The chronicle records, of the ride away from the Western Barracks that evening, the moment [[Snap]] — who had slept through nearly the entire descent into the underground in the back of the cart and had woken only when the King was already dead — sat up in the back of the cart, looked at the rippling ground, looked at the running men, and said to no one in particular:

*"You know — you all should have woken me up."*

He drew the dried meat from his pack, found his most comfortable corner, and watched the ground in the southeast as the cart took them away from the place where Ezericht La'Monte had become, in every way that mattered, the man the title *the Unwilling Heir* had been waiting for.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the regicide of King [[Alaric La'Monte]] by [[Ezericht La'Monte]] in the western underground base of [[General Flitney]]'s military programme — the act of last resort by which the Mehrendt military intended to nullify the contract by which the Church had bound the King. The chapter establishes the canonical event by which Ezericht earned the title *The Unwilling Heir*: not by inheritance, but by the strike that ended the line he had not, until that morning, known he carried.

The chapter records [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s improvisational reassignment of his protection detail after the Fourth Wall Division failed to arrive on time; [[Snap]]'s conscription; [[Zin]]'s incidental approach to the base and Edward's misdirection of him to a southeastern farm; the rune-inscribed door whose etchings matched Ezericht's dagger; the King's recognition of his son the instant Ezericht entered the fourth chamber on the left; the King's last words; the strike; the spectre that rose from the body once the bloodline-protections lost their tether; the sealing of the chamber; and the rippling ground to the southeast as the company began their retreat.

The events at the farm to which Edward misdirected Zin — and the parallel activities of [[Trinity Stolen]] at [[Dunmisk]], [[Natas]] at an unnamed southern town, and [[Marxus]] at the [[Hypostasis Monastery|monastery]] — are deferred to the next chapter.

The chronicle observes one canonical detail of the dagger that is not yet developed: that the *heat* the dagger took on after the strike is, the chronicle suspects, an aspect of the same bloodline-spell that allowed the dagger to open the rune-door — the magic, having done what it was waiting to do, releasing its long-stored charge in the only way available to it.

The chronicle further records — with the careful retrospective neutrality it reserves for things its principal actors did not, at the time, know — that the bloodline-protections placed upon King [[Alaric La'Monte|Alaric]] were not, in fact, the Church's *defence*. They were the Church's *trigger*. The Church had searched for King Alaric's unrecognized bastard heir for years and had been unable to locate him; the bloodline-bind on Alaric's holding-chamber was the instrument designed to *force the heir's surfacing*. The military's last-resort plan — by which they routed [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]] into the chamber to do the only thing that would dissolve the contract — was the action the Church had been waiting for. The regicide identified the heir for them. The plan that [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] believed had outsmarted the Church had, in fact, been the Church's plan; the loophole he believed he had found had been the loophole the Church had quietly left open. The chronicle records [[General Flitney]] and Edward's confidence in the office that morning as sincere, and incorrect. The consequences of that error will unfold in the chapters that follow Part One.
