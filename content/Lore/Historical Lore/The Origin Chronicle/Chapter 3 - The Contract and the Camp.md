# Chapter 3 — The Contract and the Camp

> *In which the company is named to its purpose, a blood-and-soul contract is drafted by candlelight, and the assassin who tipped Edward off at the South Gate makes a discovery that will change the shape of the mission.*

---

## I. The Blade at the Door

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] held the broadsword at [[Six]]'s neck for a long enough moment to make the point, and then he let it down.

*"Don't do that."*

He sheathed the sword and stepped aside, gesturing to the empty chair beside [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s.

*"Well. Have a seat. We were discussing a… business opportunity with the monk. I think you would be quite interested."*

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] watched the doorway as Six entered. She had not, until that moment, seen Six without his goggles, and the faint red of his eyes — what little of it the candle showed — did not visibly surprise her.

She was already lifting her hand to resume the briefing when she felt the presence at the edge of her perception again, and she stopped.

A breath. Concentration. The presence was a sound, not a voice — a low frequency that any might hear and dismiss as wind through a poorly-sealed door — but Trinity heard the encrypted shape of it.

*So that is what the Gheronda has to say. Just as I expected. I shall do as asked.*

She opened her eyes.

*"I apologize. The whistling of the wind misled me to believe there was someone near."*

She looked at the three men.

*"As I was saying — there is an offer that may arouse your interest. However, I ask of you to wait for a while longer before I explain myself any further. I am not certain, but I believe we will be joined by more in only a while. Still, I would appreciate it if you, Mr. Edward and Mr. Ezericht, explained what I have so far to the necromancer."*

---

## II. The Watcher Above

The man on the rooftop two streets over was, as it happened, the same man who had sent Edward to the South Gate hours earlier. His name was [[Cain]]. He had been hired to a contract in Sahlen that night — not the contract that had brought the city's defenders to the gate, but a smaller, older sort of contract: a wife's grievance against an unfaithful husband, paid in coin and discretion.

He whispered the syllables he used for distance vision — *Lumshackl strurk shiltan* — and the night street resolved as if it were the noon hour. He watched the husband come into the candle-lit room of his arrangement, watched the man and his lover settle into the small slow movements of a thing they had done many times before, and waited for the pattern of those movements to become predictable.

It became predictable.

Cain loosed the arrow from his back-pocket arrow stash — he disliked quivers — and the shaft sailed through the open window and through the satin window covering and into the husband. There was a scream. One figure sagged out of view. The other ran from the room. Cain whispered *Shmick sitan* and his vision returned to its ordinary state.

He stowed the bow, walked off the rooftop into a back alley as comfortably as a man walking down a stair, and disappeared into the shadow.

---

## III. The Child in the Alley

He was crossing a side street when a child came running past him on his right and brushed against his hip.

Cain's hand was around the child's collar before he had finished thinking about it, and the small ebony dagger he kept on his left side was at the child's throat before the child had finished gasping.

The child looked up at him.

That look — that particular registration of fear in a particular kind of face — undid something Cain had spent a long time fastening down. He saw, for half a second, another child running toward him, arms outstretched, crying, getting farther rather than nearer; he saw himself in a place he had spent years not visiting; he saw his own hand doing a thing.

He came back to the alley.

He let go of the collar.

The child ran. Cain holstered the ebony dagger, picked up the right-hand dagger he had not, in the end, drawn, sheathed it as well, and walked on.

He had nothing to do and nowhere to go.

---

## IV. The Summons

He did not, therefore, refuse when [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] called his name from across the thoroughfare.

He had heard the commotion from the tavern earlier — had heard, in fact, the bell-cracking yell of Ezericht's argument carry through the rickety wall of the house — and a child running and crying from that direction had been good enough cause to investigate. The man crossing toward him now was a man Cain knew.

*"Cain!"*

Edward jogged the distance.

*"Good to see you. I have a contract you would be interested in. Pays well. I'll fill you in. Come on."*

Edward turned and walked back toward Ezericht's house with the particular forward-leaning stride of a man who presumed his company would follow him without needing to check.

Cain followed at the four-pace distance he always kept from men he half-knew.

He stopped at the rickety door and whispered *Themial Snatos* before entering. His hearing sharpened. Four sources of sound inside the house: three breaths and one whispered voice. One source outside the house, behind a wall: a humming so low he could barely hear it.

*Better be cautious. Who knows what enemies I have made over the years.*

He laid his left hand on the left holster.

He went in.

---

## V. The Briefing

[[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]] turned at Cain's arrival with a small, satisfied composure.

*"I believe everyone is here now,"* she said. *"At least most. So I shall explain myself from the beginning."*

She drew a slow breath.

*"I am Trinity Stolen, a monk from [[Hypostasis Monastery]] of the west borders. As you may or may not know, the monastery is involved in the ruling of Dyradia, and the disappearance of the King has affected their power greatly. Therefore they have sent me on a mission to recruit beings I see fit to rescue the King."*

The King she meant — though she did not name him, and the company did not yet ask — was [[Alaric La'Monte]] of Mehrendt, whose absence from the Crown's seat had begun to weigh on the political weather of the entire continent. The chronicle is satisfied to record the absence as the fact of it; the name, in this hour, was withheld.

Trinity paused. The presence at her perception had pressed harder; she suppressed it with a small effort that did not, on her face, register.

*"I consider you fit for the mission,"* she continued. *"You shall receive anything you wish that the monastery can afford — be it power or money. As soon as you choose, I will inform them of your terms."*

She let the silence carry the next part.

*"However, there are some conditions the monastery has of its own. Three, to be accurate."*

---

## VI. The Contracts

The first condition was the contract itself.

Trinity called it the **veneficuscruor-pactum** — a magical blood-contract signed in the blood of all parties, the Gheronda and Trinity included. The contract did not merely bind in honour; it bound in *being*. To violate the contract was to be erased — *signature and existence* — from the world. Those who could not die in the ordinary way would not escape the contract by that means; they would, instead, be drawn into a limbo dimension and held there.

The contract terms were two:

- Never to speak to anyone outside the contract of the monastery's involvement in the mission, or of any vital information gained about the mission.
- That any new information about the King discovered by either side would be reported to the other — though *old* information held by either side could be withheld.

The contract would burn itself when its purpose was completed: the King rescued, the rewards paid, the parties released.

Trinity paused.

*"If you don't want to agree, it is understandable. However, keep in mind that as soon as you decline, your memory of this meeting and my existence will be erased."*

She looked at them.

*"So — what do you say?"*

---

## VII. The Bargain

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] was calm.

*Stay calm,* he reminded himself, internally and constantly through the entire briefing. *These monks take advantage of everything.*

*"That raises the price considerably,"* he said. *"Twenty-five hundred each. With a bonus five hundred upfront for group total outfitting."*

He let that land.

*"Now for the terms of my own. Memories are not to be altered in any way. We only bring who we want on the mission — others are just liabilities. If I ask a question that is relevant to the mission, I have to get an answer, even if you can't tell if it is important — take my word for it."*

He pointed at Trinity.

*"And finally — this is the most important one — I want to see the contract burn."*

He lowered his hand.

*"Other than that, as far as I'm concerned, draft the accursed thing up."*

He looked around the room.

*"Who's next?"*

[[Cain]] spoke from the chair he had not, in fact, sat in.

*"This isn't going to fly with me. No one gets my blood. I have a reputation in this screwed-up world as a professional. I've never failed a contract. My word alone should suffice. Even for you and those politicians in the western lands."*

[[Six]] had been considering his pint without drinking from it. He spoke without looking up.

*"Hmm. A blood contract. I have nothing against signing it. But I'm afraid I won't be able to."*

He raised the cup of crimson liquid in mild salute.

*"I lack any blood of my own."*

He added, in a tone of practical request:

*"Unless of course you don't mind me signing with someone else's blood. I have plenty of that."*

He looked at Trinity.

*"But I have a requirement of my own. If you want me to sign this contract with my blood or whatever it is you'll have me use, you will have to give me some of *your* blood. Not much. Just enough for me to add to my collection."*

He turned to Cain.

*"So. Why is your blood so special?"*

Cain looked at him in the way one regards a bird that has spoken.

*"My blood is of my own accord and none of your concern. So long as it is in my body, it is my problem. And I intend to keep it that way."*

Ezericht, who had been watching the negotiation with the bemused patience of a man who had once seen a horse argue with a saddle, chuckled.

*"Yes, I know you, Cain. A notorious assassin throughout the land. But professions like that come with repercussions. You always finish your contract — but I'm willing to bet you would double-cross that client for a higher paying one. Work for the highest bidder, correct? Who's to say you won't do the same to us from someone with deeper pockets? If anything you are the least trustworthy of all, and most in need to sign the contract."*

He shrugged.

*"You have no special reason as to why you can't. So follow the group and sign. Let's see that contract. I'm ready to get to work."*

Cain considered this.

*"Good point. Good point, stranger. But your folly comes in that — who in the land has a bigger bank account than the monks and bureaucrats in the western lands? Constantly taxing the peasants with tariffs and fees. They can more than outbid any other client that may arise."*

He paused.

*"And if the rest of you are trustworthy, then no one else will know of my appointment to this contract. As none of us would be able to talk about it."*

Trinity had been considering both objections. She frowned at one and folded both into her solution.

*"Understood. However, I would like a few things to be cleared for me before I agree."*

She turned to Edward.

*"Mr. Edward — when you say 'Memories are not to be altered in any way,' do you mean during or after the mission? If you mean after, I am afraid I can't comply with those terms, seeing as that would make the contract permanent and it wouldn't ever burn until you die."*

Edward inclined his head.

*"My mistake. I shall clarify. The memory contract is to be separate from the mission contract. Also, I would like to keep track of the memory contract. Where you put the mission one is not my concern until it is over. Clear enough?"*

*"Clear enough."*

She turned to Cain.

*"And as for you — if you do not wish to give your blood, it is fine by me. However, your word alone won't be sufficient — no matter who or what reputation you have. The truth is I hold as much trust in you as you in me. Maybe less."*

She did not soften the line.

*"You can keep your blood. But a type of contract does have to be signed. I was thinking of a **veneficuscoul-pactum** instead. Maybe that is more agreeable to you. The same terms as in the veneficuscruor — but signed in soul rather than in blood."*

She turned, finally, to Six.

*"Both contracts shall be made so that they are one. A **certamen-partum**, to be more exact — a combined contract — so that no one has to sign twice."*

She held Six's eyes.

*"Also, necromancer — since you have no blood of your own, you would have to sign the veneficuscoul-pactum. About my blood — you shall receive it. Only if you sign another contract. A permanent one. In which you agree never to use my blood in a way that can cause me harm."*

She let that hang.

*"Is that ok?"*

Cain inclined his head once. *"Very well. I see no harm in that, as the soul has no correlation to my blood, and also since I have no need for something so corrupt. I shall sign this veneficuscoul-pactum of yours."*

Six smiled with what passed, on him, for warmth.

*"Alright. I can sign this new contract. But I must ask — do you not trust me with your blood, little monk? Why would you be afraid of one such as me? Surely you have some holy power up your sleeve which could destroy me in an instant. But oh well. If signing the second contract will make you give me some of your blood — I will sign."*

Trinity's relief was not visible to anyone who had not been watching for it.

*"I accept all conditions. Mr. Edward, you will get to keep the memory contract, as it is of no concern to the monastery."*

She rose.

*"We will meet here tomorrow. By then the contracts should have been made accordingly."*

She moved toward the door.

*"If you will excuse me, I shall take my leave. I have matters to attend to."*

---

## VIII. The Departures

Six did not entirely let her go.

*"Hmm. What matters do you have to attend to, I wonder? Or is it none of our business? If it's none of our business, that will just intrigue me more."*

He sipped from the cross-cup.

*"I won't let you leave until you tell us more about yourself and the monastery you work for."*

Cain did not stay for the answer.

*"Well, in that case, I will take this spare time to discern some facts that are bothering me. Until tomorrow — I bid you adieu."*

He did not sheathe his weapon. He whispered *ugth remials*, the candle in the room dimmed and threw a single blurred shadow on the wall, and when it returned to brightness Cain was no longer in the house.

Ezericht shook his head, dropped back into his chair, and rested his legs on the footrest.

*"I would like to personally read BOTH of these contracts once they are produced. No contract is without its loopholes, and I'm sure there will be something there that wasn't expected. It's not that I'm untrusting — I'm just realistic. But if that's all we are to do this evening, I am going to give myself some rest. Until tomorrow."*

He leaned his head back and was, within seconds, asleep.

Edward sighed.

*"Have to work on that. I advise we relocate. I need to get a room at the Inn, so that seems like as good a place as any. Meet me in the lobby of the Inn in fifteen minutes, if you would."*

He went out into the alley, past the bar, and to the Inn, where he secured a room and waited.

Trinity made for the door at last.

Six was, by that time, considering whether to detain her after all when she spoke a single word — *turbatio* — and the room went briefly out of register. By the time Six's mind had cleared, she was gone.

He did not rush to find her.

*"Well, well,"* he said aloud to no one, since Ezericht was already asleep and Edward had left. *"That was rather unpleasant. I dislike people using magic or enchantments on me. I think I'll let her know that next time I see her. Maybe I'll let her see an enchantment of my own. Who knows?"*

He stood.

*"Alright,"* he said in the direction of Edward's chair, though Edward was no longer in it. *"I'll meet you there in fifteen minutes. I think something rather interesting just came up, though. So I may be a little late. But I should be there within ten to fifteen minutes of fifteen minutes."*

He went out the door.

---

## IX. The Race to the Camp

[[Cain]] had not, in truth, gone to discern facts that were bothering him.

He had gone to the giant camp.

Cain had killed a giant prince, once, on a contract he had taken in younger years, and he had not forgotten the road to where the giants lived. The mention of *a* giant — in [[Sahlen]], among humans, at this hour, helping with the very mob the giants' own kind had attacked — had bothered him for the entire briefing.

He whispered *donai remiols* and broke into the unnatural speed his magic gave him over open ground. The plains north of Sahlen passed under him as if the road had decided to pull him. The northern mountain ranges rose into view. He climbed the last rise on foot — the magic was not meant for vertical work — and reached the vantage point he had used on the mission that the rest of the world had forgotten he had ever undertaken.

He whispered *Lumshackl strurk shiltan*.

His vision tightened.

The giant camp was dead.

Children at the firesides, juveniles at the watch posts, elderly at the storage racks. None of them had been soldiers. All of them had been killed where they stood, and the killing had been recent enough that the bodies had not yet begun to rot in the cold air of the heights.

Cain's hand fastened on the rock beside him.

The giant *soldiers* — every fighting-age giant who would otherwise have been at this camp — were south. They were in Sahlen. They had been *drawn* there by someone who knew that the only way to massacre a giant camp was to first remove the giants who could fight back.

The implication settled into him with the cold certainty of an arrow finding its target. The raid on Sahlen had not been a raid on Sahlen. The raid on Sahlen had been a distraction.

And the giants — those who survived the diversion — would, when they returned, find what Cain was looking at now. They would not believe Sahlen had not been complicit.

There would be more giants in Mehrendt by the end of the week.

Cain whispered *Shmick sitan* and his vision normalized.

*I need to get back to town and inform that monk and the others of the events that have conspired here. Perhaps they have a proposal for a course of action.*

He stood up.

He heard, behind him and below him, two faint thumps.

He did not stay to find the source.

*Time for me to leave this accursed place. I am not welcome here.*

He went south, at speed, and did not look back.

---

## X. The Inn

In the meantime, in [[Sahlen]], the Inn had filled with men taking the night's relief in the customary way.

A man in ragged brown shorts, no shirt, an unusually large axe strapped across his back, and a hatchet on either side of his belt, walked through the inn door and took a seat at the counter. The barkeep poured him a pint without being asked. The man drank, made small talk with the patrons near him, and was, in every respect that mattered, a regular.

His name was [[Snap]].

He had not been at the South Gate. He had not been at the second wave. He had spent the day walking the streets selling trinkets, and the night, as he had spent most nights, drinking his trinket money into ease.

The Inn's door opened again.

---

## XI. The Creature in the Alley

What had delayed [[Six]] on the way to the Inn — what had become the "something rather interesting" he had warned the empty chair about — was a strange-smelling blood on the night air.

He had noticed the same scent earlier, faintly, in Trinity's house — though the chronicle is uncertain whether he had recognized it for what it was at the time. The scent had a particular wrongness about it. Six had followed it.

He had found the creature in a back alley, two streets from the Inn.

It carried a sword with a blade four feet long, and it carried it as if it weighed nothing.

It saw Six.

Six rushed it first — closed the distance with the dagger drawn and made for its throat. It stepped backward in a single elegant motion and put the four-foot blade through Six's chest. It withdrew the blade, watched Six fall, and turned to walk away.

Behind it, Six began to laugh.

He stood.

*"You're going to need to do better than that to kill me."*

The creature turned and smiled.

It was a smile Six recognized. It was the smile of a thing that had been told the same thing before and had answered it.

It came for him.

Six jumped clear of the first stroke and lunged in close on the second; the creature drew a smaller blade from its back, blocked his dagger, and on the recovery took Six's arm off above the elbow.

The creature turned to walk away a second time.

Six did not give it the chance. He came in from behind, drove his remaining hand through the creature's back, and closed his fist on its heart. The creature's chest cavity gave way around his hand. It went down.

A dog, watching from the alley's edge, took Six's severed arm in its mouth and ran with it.

Six considered the dog with a small expression of resignation. He considered the creature with rather more.

*"Guess I'll just have to take this thing's arm."*

He cut the creature's right arm off with one of his larger scalpels, added a small measure of its blood to the cross's reservoir for later study, and walked the rest of the way to the Inn carrying the creature's arm at his side.

He went to the bar.

There was no Edward in sight. There was, however, an axe-bearing man in ragged shorts drinking a pint.

*"Excuse me,"* Six said. *"Could you hold this arm against my shoulder while I sew it on?"*

---

## XII. The Surgery

[[Snap]] turned, took in the situation, and turned back to the bar to finish the sentence he had been mid-way through with the barkeep.

Then he turned back to Six.

*"What exactly do you want me to do?"* he said. *"And who exactly are you, and why is your arm missing?"*

He looked around the Inn for someone else who might want to help instead. There was no one.

*"I want you to hold this arm against my shoulder while I sew it on,"* Six said. *"Who I am doesn't really matter. And my arm's missing because I got in a fight with this thing's initial body. So — will you hold it for me?"*

Snap considered the proposition with the bemused calculus of a drunk man assessing the probability he was hallucinating, and concluded it didn't matter either way.

*"Why the hell not,"* he said. *"Plus, those eyes of yours aren't all that pleasant."*

He finished his pint, grabbed the strange arm, and held it against Six's shoulder.

Six produced a curved needle and a fine cord from his pouch and began to sew.

*Why has this guy not bled to death yet?* Snap thought. *This has just got to be one of my weird drunk dreams.*

When the arm was attached he asked the obvious question.

*"What is your name, stranger? I haven't seen you in this city."*

He almost fell out of his chair, recovered against the bar.

*"They call me Snap around these parts."*

*"My name is Six,"* Six said, putting his goggles back on. *"Thank you for holding this arm for me."*

He flexed the new fingers. The claws at their tips grew, almost instantly, back to the length of his original claws, and the arm itself reshaped subtly across the next minute until it matched the rest of him.

He gestured to the bartender for a cup, took the cross from his neck, and poured a measure of crimson liquid into the cup.

The bartender's face did a complicated thing.

*"So,"* Six said to Snap. *"Why are you here drinking?"*

Snap rubbed his eyes.

*"I am here drinking because that's what I do the most. After a day of walking around trying to sell trinkets and other sorts of items to people, you need a few drinks to calm you down."*

He slammed the empty pint down and waved for another.

*"Now what are you drinking, if you don't mind me asking?"*

[[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]] chose that moment to come down the Inn stairs. He saw Six at the bar. He saw the new arm. He saw the bartender's face. He decided, on balance, not to ask.

*"Hey Six. I'll see you tomorrow."*

He went back upstairs and was, within minutes, asleep.

*"This is blood,"* Six said to Snap, lifting his cup. *"Would you like some? I have plenty."*

Snap shook his head.

*"No thanks. I will stick to my ale here."*

He finished it, slid off the stool, and made his way to the door.

*"Well, I must be heading off to my house. I need some sleep. You said your name was Six, right? Have a good night and enjoy your blood."*

He went out into the night.

Six watched him go.

*"Hmm. I think he thinks I'm crazy. Oh well. Most people do."*

He rented a room. He did not, he later said, sleep. He gave his body a break, which was not the same. He sat on the edge of the bed turning the new arm slowly in the candlelight, and thinking. He thought about the creature whose arm it had been, and the strangeness of its blood. He thought about the King the monk had been sent to find. He wondered, with the same quiet thoroughness with which he had earlier wondered about the bodies in the gutter, whether the creature and the mission were linked.

He did not, yet, know.

But he was prepared to find out.

---

## XIII. The Valham Inn

Trinity had not stopped running until she was two forests from [[Sahlen]].

The town she had run to was called [[Valham]]. It was a small place, filled with criminals and poverty, and its inn was located in its most secluded quarter — which was the reason Trinity had chosen it.

She entered. The door screeched. The attendant at the desk — a young woman named [[Lauren Fern]] — woke from a doze and brought a crossbow up at the sound.

Trinity crossed the lobby in less than a blink, twisted Lauren's wrist so that the bolt would, if loosed, go into the ceiling, and let her go.

*"You hesitated again, Miss Lauren. Next time — shoot first, then think."*

Lauren laughed nervously.

*"Trinity. I am glad you are back. It took you longer than usual today. Did—"*

Trinity placed a finger on the younger woman's lips.

*"Talking more than needed leads to trouble. I shall be going to my room now, okay?"*

Lauren nodded.

Trinity went to the room.

She closed the door behind her, sat on the bed, and called.

*"Shift."*

The air around her thickened, drew in toward her open hand, and settled there in the shape of a small ball of fog. Trinity smiled at it.

*"How about you change into something more easy to see, Shift."*

The fog reshaped. It took a purplish color and the form of a mini-dragon mingled with a sea serpent — small enough to sit comfortably in Trinity's palm.

*"That's better. Now — would you please deliver to me the message that you could hardly wait to tell me."*

The creature gave a low shriek-like sound, and the message resolved in Trinity's mind with the clarity of speech.

*"The message wasn't that important. It could have waited. You almost gave yourself away. It took a lot of my energy to suppress your presence. I can't afford to do that all the time."*

The creature shrank in size, apologetic.

Trinity smiled.

*"I guess I can let it go this time. Now — do me a favor. Inform the Gheronda of the happenings at the meeting. Tell him the new conditions and how the contracts should be written. Oh, and please don't forget to tell him to sign them and give them to you."*

The creature did not, at first, move.

Trinity laughed under her breath. She reached into one of the many hidden pockets in her robe and withdrew a small flat sweet cake — the kind ordinary people called a cookie. Shift took it from her palm and devoured it in a heartbeat.

*"That is all I have. Go deliver the message, Shift."*

The creature transformed again. This time it took the shape of a flower seed, multiplied into a hundred replicas of itself, and dispersed into the air of the room. Trinity blew them toward the window, and they were gone.

She closed her eyes.

*Now all I have to do is wait.*

She lay back.

For the first time since the South Gate, she slept.

## Sources & Notes

This chapter records the night of the contract negotiation in [[Ezericht La'Monte|Ezericht]]'s house — the formal binding of the company to its purpose — and the parallel events of that same night: [[Cain]]'s arrival, his subsequent ride to the northern giant camp and the discovery of its slaughter, [[Six]]'s alley encounter with the strange-bladed creature, the introduction of [[Snap]], and [[Trinity Stolen|Trinity]]'s flight to [[Valham]].

The King referred to throughout this chapter — and throughout the mission as a whole — was **[[Alaric La'Monte]]**, then-reigning monarch of [[Mehrendt]], whose disappearance precipitated the Hypostasis recruitment. The chronicle observes that none of the company in Sahlen that night yet knew either his name or the implications his rescue would have for one of their own.

This chapter also records the appearance of [[Cain]]'s magical incantations — a system of whispered command-phrases of unknown linguistic origin — and the introduction of the [[Veneficus Contracts]] as a binding instrument of [[Hypostasis Monastery|Hypostasis]] tradition.

*Period source: the founding fragments preserved in The RP that Started it All, posts 55–93.*
