Bohrova

The Sage of the Interstice  
(Archival Entry – Compiled Accounts)

---

Overview

Name: Bohrova  
Era: Pre-Fracture (exact dates disputed)  
Known Titles:

The Interstitial Sage
    
The First Conduit
    
The World-Bridger
    
The Heretic Architect (Church designation)
    

Status: Disappeared (Fate Unknown)

Bohrova is widely regarded as the most influential mortal scholar in recorded Dyradian history. His work reshaped travel, education, infrastructure, and the mortal understanding of magic itself. Though celebrated by most cultures, his legacy remains deeply contested, particularly by religious institutions descended from the Church of Prim.

---

Major Contributions

The Interstitial Network (Terminology varies)

Bohrova is credited with the creation of a vast system of stabilized magical passages allowing accelerated travel across Dyradia. These passages did not transport travelers to another plane, but rather through a contained liminal space existing between Dyradia and the realm associated with the Dyra.

Common scholarly terms include:

The Interstice
    
The Veiled Way
    
The Liminal Channel
    
The Between
    
The Dyric Span
    

Travel through the Interstice reduced months of overland travel to days, radically altering trade, diplomacy, and cultural exchange. Over decades, the network facilitated unprecedented growth and interconnection among civilizations.

Notably:

Travelers never directly entered the Dyra plane
    
The space was described as contained, navigable, and directional
    
Only Bohrova is believed to have fully understood its mechanics
    

Many Interstitial nodes remain functional following the Fracture, though their behavior has become unpredictable.

---

The Academic Awakening

Through his communion with the Dyra and exposure to non-mortal modes of perception, Bohrova introduced transformative advancements in:

Mathematics
    
Architecture
    
Astronomy
    
Medicine
    
Philosophy
    
Artistic theory
    

This period, often referred to as The Awakening, marked a transition from myth-driven tradition to structured inquiry. Institutions of learning expanded rapidly, and standardized notation systems emerged that remain foundational today.

---

Personal History

He fell in love with a Dyra attuned to the energy of the spirit. They were rumored to have had a Dyra/Human hybrid child, and introduced humans who were also attuned to the energies of the Dyra, to the world.

Tiered Knowledge Accounts

---

Tier 0 — Common Knowledge

(No roll required)

“Bohrova was a great wizard who helped the world prosper before the Fracture. He made travel easier, taught people new ways to build and learn, and then vanished before the war destroyed everything.”

Most people believe:

Bohrova was a benevolent genius
    
He lived long ago
    
His works were lost or damaged in the war
    
His disappearance spared him from blame
    

This version is comforting, tidy, and incomplete.

---

Tier 1 — Familiar History

(Low DC History / Educated Background)

“Bohrova was a planar scholar whose discoveries led to the creation of the Interstitial travel network and the spread of advanced sciences. His teachings accelerated civilization, but also brought mortals dangerously close to forces they did not fully understand.”

Known at this level:

The Interstice existed between planes
    
Travel was regulated and structured
    
The Awakening reshaped multiple kingdoms
    
The Church of Prim viewed Bohrova as dangerous
    

Students are often taught that Bohrova’s work was “too much, too fast.”

---

Tier 2 — Archival / Scholarly Record

(Moderate–High DC History, Libraries, Feythran or Gohlstout sources)

“Primary records indicate Bohrova did not merely study planar energies, but lived for extended periods within a stabilized liminal environment. His understanding of distance, scale, and resonance far exceeded contemporary magical theory.”

Archival texts confirm:

The Interstice was engineered, not discovered
    
Its geometry did not correspond to Dyradian space
    
Some nodes were deliberately left unfinished
    
Bohrova restricted access to certain regions intentionally
    

Additionally:

Early Awakening mathematics show non-Euclidean influence
    
Architectural designs mirror Interstitial flow patterns
    
Several disciplines emerged solely because of Bohrova’s teachings
    

Scholars argue whether the Fracture damaged the network — or merely revealed its instability.

---

Tier 3 — Obscure / Suppressed Knowledge

(High DC, Restricted Texts, Elven Oral Record, Dwarven Vaults)

“Elven records describe Bohrova as neither fully mortal nor entirely unchanged by his time within the Interstice. His prolonged exposure altered his perception of causality, distance, and continuity.”

Deep records suggest:

Bohrova could navigate the Interstice without anchors
    
He may have perceived Dyradia as a node, not a world
    
Some Interstitial paths do not reconnect to known locations
    
The Dyra plane was not his destination — merely adjacent
    

Most disturbingly:

Some believe Bohrova did not disappear
    
He may have withdrawn beyond conventional spatial relation
    
Or became bound to maintaining—or restraining—the system itself
    

Certain fringe theories posit:

The current instability is not decay
    
It is interference
    
As though something long held in check is pushing back
    

---

Post-Fracture State of the Interstitial Network

Following the Fracture:

Some nodes collapsed entirely
    
Others function at reduced capacity
    
Several reconnect to unfamiliar regions of Dyradia
    
A small number lead to places not recorded on any map
    

Rumors persist of:

Travelers returning changed
    
Journeys taking longer internally than externally
    
Paths that move deeper instead of across
    

Modern authorities heavily regulate Interstitial use.

---

Cultural Impact

Mehrendt

Credits Bohrova for its rise as a trade power
    
Publicly honors his contributions
    
Privately avoids his deeper theories
    

Feythra

Remembers Bohrova as a listener
    
Treats his name with reverence and caution
    
Preserves unedited accounts of his work
    

Gohlstout

Archives his mathematics and schematics
    
Refuses to speculate on his fate
    
Notes increased anomalies near deep vault passages
    

Avalon

Reveres Bohrova as proof that magic should not be controlled
    
Views the Fracture as political failure, not scholarly hubris
    

---

Current Scholarly Consensus

There is none.

Bohrova is simultaneously remembered as:

A savior
    
A fool
    
A victim
    
A warning
    

What is agreed upon is this:

Dyradia as it exists today would not exist without Bohrova.  
And whatever is now destabilizing the world is touching something he once held together.
