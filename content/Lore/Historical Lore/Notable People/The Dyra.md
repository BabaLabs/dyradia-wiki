# 📜 **The Dyra**

_Also known as: The Primordials, The Elemental Sovereigns, The First Resonance_

---

## **Overview**

**Classification:** Primordial Entities  
**Association:** Elemental Forces  
**Plane Affiliation:** Adjacent Extraplanar Domain (Designation Disputed)  
**Status:** Absent / Inaccessible / Dormant (Post-Fracture)

The **Dyra** are ancient beings believed to embody the fundamental elemental forces underlying existence in Dyradia. Long predating mortal civilization, they are described across cultures as both creators and sustainers of the world’s natural order.

The true nature of the Dyra remains one of the most contested subjects in Dyradian scholarship, theology, and magic theory.

---

## **General Characteristics**

Accounts of the Dyra consistently attribute the following traits:

- Elemental embodiment rather than physical form
    
- Immense influence over natural phenomena
    
- Limited direct interaction with mortals
    
- Communication through resonance, symbolism, or intermediary states
    
- Perception of reality fundamentally unlike mortal cognition
    

The Dyra are not universally considered gods. Whether they _should_ be considered divine is a matter of ongoing debate.

---

## **Known Attunements**

The Dyra embody specific elemental forces. Currently confirmed:

- **Spirit** — referenced via [[Bohrova]]'s mortal-Dyra union; one of the Dyra was attuned to the energy of the spirit.

## **Tiered Knowledge Accounts**

---

### **Tier 0 — Common Knowledge**

_(No roll required)_

> _“The Dyra were the elemental gods of the old world. Fire, water, stone, wind — that sort of thing. People used to worship them before the Church put an end to it.”_

Most people believe:

- The Dyra were ancient gods
    
- They are gone or destroyed
    
- Worship of them caused the Fracture
    
- Their time has passed
    

This version is simple, moralized, and culturally influenced.

---

### **Tier 1 — Familiar History**

_(Low DC History, Religious or Arcane Background)_

> _“The Dyra were powerful primordial beings tied to elemental forces. They shaped the early world and influenced magic before mortals developed independent spellcasting traditions.”_

At this level, it is known that:

- The Dyra predate organized religion
    
- They were not uniformly worshiped
    
- Some cultures revered them, others feared them
    
- Their influence waned long before the Fracture
    

Scholars often describe them as _precursors_, not rulers.

---

### **Tier 2 — Archival / Scholarly Record**

_(Moderate–High DC History, Feythran or Gohlstout Sources)_

> _“Primary records describe the Dyra as ontological anchors — entities whose existence stabilized elemental interaction across planes.”_

Scholarly consensus (limited as it is) suggests:

- The Dyra were not creators _ex nihilo_, but **organizers**
    
- Their presence maintained equilibrium between elemental forces
    
- Mortal magic originally functioned through proximity to Dyra resonance
    
- The Dyra plane was neither fully separate nor fully merged with Dyradia
    

Key insight:

> The Dyra did not grant magic.  
> They **made it coherent**.

This reframes them as infrastructure rather than deities.

---

### **Tier 3 — Obscure / Suppressed Knowledge**

_(High DC, Elven Oral Tradition, Restricted Dwarven Records)_

> _“Elven records describe the Dyra not as beings who existed within the world, but as patterns the world expressed.”_

Deep records imply:

- Each Dyra embodied a _mode of existence_, not merely an element
    
- Their ‘forms’ were interpretive manifestations perceived by mortals
    
- They did not experience time linearly
    
- They could not fully comprehend mortality — or individuality
    

Most unsettling:

- The Dyra may not have been capable of preventing Bohrova’s work
    
- Not due to weakness, but due to **incompatibility of perception**
    

A fringe interpretation suggests:

> The Dyra did not allow magic.  
> They were **never asked**.

---

## **Relationship to Magic**

Prior to the Fracture:

- Magic flowed directly through elemental resonance stabilized by the Dyra
    
- The Awoken drew power through attunement, not invocation
    
- Spellcasting lacked rigid classification
    

After the Fracture:

- The Dyra’s stabilizing influence became inaccessible
    
- Magic became mediated, categorized, and constrained
    
- Elemental magic became less predictable
    

Some scholars argue the Dyra are not gone — merely unreachable.

---

## **Relationship to Bohrova**

Accounts agree on several points:

- Bohrova established prolonged contact with the Dyra
    
- He adapted mortal cognition to interact with them
    
- He did not “command” them
    
- At least one Dyra formed a bond with him
    

Elven sources describe Bohrova as:

> _“A mortal who learned to listen without demanding answers.”_

Church doctrine disputes this entirely.

## **Cultural Interpretations**

### **Mehrendt**

- Publicly considers the Dyra a failed belief system
    
- Academic interest remains cautious and regulated
    

### **Feythra**

- Continues ritual veneration
    
- Rejects classification of the Dyra as gods
    
- Believes their absence is _temporary_
    

### **Gohlstout**

- Records Dyra references precisely
    
- Avoids theological interpretation
    
- Notes correlation between Dyra absence and material instability
    

### **Avalon**

- Views the Dyra as proof magic should not be centralized
    
- Treats them as historical forces, not objects of worship
    

---

## **Current Scholarly Disputes**

Unresolved questions include:

- Whether the Dyra still exist
    
- If their plane remains intact
    
- Whether the Interstice intersects their domain
    
- If reconnection is possible — or catastrophic
    

There is increasing concern that renewed magical instability corresponds with **resonance patterns historically attributed to the Dyra**.
