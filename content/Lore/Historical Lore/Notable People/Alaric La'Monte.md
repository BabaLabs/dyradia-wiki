# Alaric La'Monte

King of [[Mehrendt]] in the years immediately preceding [[The Fracture]]. His **disappearance** during this period precipitated a political destabilization that drew in the [[Hypostasis Monastery]], the giant clans of the northern ranges, and ultimately the company that would later be remembered as the founders of the Fracture-era resistance.

## The Disappearance

Alaric was taken into protective custody by the Mehrendt military approximately eight months before the events of the Sahlen chronicle, after intelligence missions against the western-borders monastery uncovered evidence that the King had — under circumstances the General's later briefing did not flatter — signed a contract with **the Church** of Mehrendt. The contract bound the King to a gradual ten-year programme of legislative reform whose cumulative effect would have transferred full legislative control of Mehrendt into the Church's hands.

The military hid Alaric in an underground facility while the leadership sought a means of breaking the contract that would not place the Church in immediate control. The Church, finding the King not at court, propagandized a civil war along the southern border in the hope of drawing him out — judging that the King would not allow his subjects to die in a war his appearance could end. Alaric, hidden, did not appear.

[[Hypostasis Monastery]] — whose covert role in Dyradian governance also depended on the continuity of the legitimate Crown — separately committed substantial resources to the search. [[Trinity Stolen]] was the field agent sent to identify and recruit a private company for the rescue.

The relationship between **the Church** as named by [[General Flitney]] and the **Hypostasis Monastery** under which Trinity operated is not resolved in the early chronicle; whether they are facets of one institution, allied entities, or rivals is left for later chapters to clarify.

See [[Chapter 6 - The Amulet and the Dagger]] for the General's disclosure of the contract and the military's last-resort plan.

## The Death

King Alaric was killed in the fourth chamber on the left of the rune-sealed deep corridor of the western underground base on the evening following [[Ezericht La'Monte]]'s recruitment to the military's last-resort plan. He recognized his son the instant Ezericht entered the chamber. He had been holding a glass of wine. He spoke to Ezericht briefly — the chronicle preserves his last recorded words: *"How unfortunate we had to meet up like this — but I suppose action has to be taken. Be quick, and strike swiftly. There isn't time for doubt and questions. Just know that what has happened, happened for a reason."* Ezericht's dagger went in beneath the breastbone. The King's hand closed for a final moment on Ezericht's sleeve, then released. He did not, by the chronicle's record, fight.

The death triggered an effect for which the military had only partially prepared: the bloodline-protections that had been ingrained into Alaric's body for generations released, on his death, into a **spectre** that emerged from the throne and could not be safely returned to anywhere it had been. The military's contingency was to collapse the deep corridor and the surrounding tunnels behind the sealed rune-door, containing the spectre to a limited radius. The collapse was ordered immediately after Ezericht's escape.

The rippling motion observed in the open country to the southeast of the Western Barracks shortly after the death is presumed, in chronicle record, to have been related — though the relationship is not yet specified.

See [[Chapter 8 - The Fourth Chamber]].

## The Recruitment

Trinity's recruitment of [[Ezericht La'Monte]], [[Edward Z Zwarf]], [[Six]], and [[Cain]] for the rescue mission occurred in [[Sahlen]] on the night of the South Gate raid. The recruits did not yet, on that night, know the King's name. The chronicle observes that none of the company — least of all Ezericht himself — yet understood how directly his own life would be altered by the answer to the question of who Alaric was, and what his rescue would cost.

See [[Chapter 3 - The Contract and the Camp]] for the contract that bound the rescue company to its purpose.

## Lineage Significance

The full implications of Alaric's lineage relationship to [[Ezericht La'Monte]] — and the path by which Ezericht would eventually come to be remembered as *The Unwilling Heir* — develop in subsequent chronicle chapters as the rescue mission unfolds.
