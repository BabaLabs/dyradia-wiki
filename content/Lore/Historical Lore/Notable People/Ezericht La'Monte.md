# Ezericht La'Monte

*The Unwilling Heir.* A figure of the late pre-Fracture and Fracture eras in Mehrendt, drawn into the events that reshaped his nation by way of a personal vendetta long unresolved.

## Origins

The household that raised Ezericht was not his by blood. They were the family who took him in, and they were the family whose killers he had been hunting since their deaths. The pursuit defined the early years of his adult life and remained unresolved at the time of his first meeting with the company that would later be known as the founders. He marked the anniversary of their deaths annually, alone, by returning to the same tavern in [[Sahlen]] and drinking — three pints to begin with, more depending on the weight of the evening.

## The Dagger

The dagger that proved Ezericht's lineage to those who knew the marks is, on the chronicle's first canonical disclosure, **a relic of an older era's necromancy contracts**. The dagger was made — in a time when souls were bound to bodies for the duration of their masters' usefulness — to *release the soul from the body and prevent its return.* It is the only known instrument capable of cleanly dissolving a body-bound necromantic pact. The Church's contract on [[Alaric La'Monte]] was, in substance if not in name, such a pact; the dagger's strike on the King dissolved it. See [[Chapter 11 - The Vanished Friend]] for [[Edward Z Zwarf|Edward]]'s disclosure of the dagger's true history.

## The Vanished Friend

The chronicle records, as Ezericht's first canonically-disclosed relationship of childhood, a friendship with **an unnamed being of [[Six]]'s kind** — a product of [[Six's Father]]'s earlier and lesser experiments — who befriended Ezericht in the town of his foster-placement, gave him as a parting relic a small dark-grey stone with a dark red star at its centre (the residue of his own emptied soul-stone), and *chose mortality back* by his own act before vanishing from Ezericht's life. The friend's name has not been preserved. His parting word to Ezericht was: *"In the end, you'll find it out anyways."*

[[Six]] recognized the stone the instant Ezericht produced it.

See [[Chapter 11 - The Vanished Friend]].

## The Strike

In the underground base west of [[Sahlen]], Ezericht performed the act for which the military programme had raised him: the assassination of his birth-father, King [[Alaric La'Monte]], in the fourth chamber on the left of the rune-sealed deep corridor. The strike nullified the Church's ten-year reform contract by removing the contracting party. The King, who knew Ezericht the instant he entered the chamber, did not resist; his recorded last words were *"What has happened, happened for a reason."* See [[Chapter 8 - The Fourth Chamber]].

The dagger took on intense heat after the strike — the chronicle suspects, the release of the long-stored bloodline-charge that had allowed it to open the rune-door.

This is the act by which Ezericht earned, in canonical reckoning, the title **The Unwilling Heir**.

## True Lineage

The chronicle records that Ezericht's foster family had been placed with him as a military programme administered by [[General Flitney]] of the Mehrendt military. Ezericht was, in fact, the unofficial son of King [[Alaric La'Monte]] — his birth-mother was a private lover of the King who died in childbirth. The Mehrendian Crown's strict monogamous tradition required that the King's illegitimate issue not be acknowledged publicly; Ezericht was therefore placed with a foster family and protected at distance by the military for the entirety of his early life.

His **dagger** — etched along the blade with fine spiral marks and unfamiliar characters at the cross-guard — and the **scar along his face** are royal lineage markers of a kind known only to those already informed of their meaning. To strangers, the dagger reads as decorative and the scar reads as battle-acquired. To those who hold the knowledge, both are undeniable proof of the bloodline.

Ezericht is positioned third from the throne by the standing of his birth, though the political structure that defines that position may, in the chronicle's wry phrasing, be *"influenced by his actions"* in the coming events.

See [[Chapter 6 - The Amulet and the Dagger]] for the disclosure of his lineage by [[General Flitney]] in the Sahlen armory.

## Manner & Capabilities

- **Dual-wielding swordsman.** Carried two blades at his hip and fought with both — singly or in concert. Carried a dagger besides, often in hand as a tactile fidget.
- **Trained ear.** He had deliberately sharpened his hearing as a defensive practice. *"It's always best to hear what's coming down the way."*
- **Herbist.** Skilled in the preparation of medicinal and non-medicinal preparations — *"honorable and not-so-honorable concoctions,"* in his own phrasing. Known concoction of his own naming: **[[Stillvein]]** — a muscle-quieting tincture that calmed the hands and arms against shake, used to enable difficult or long-range archery. He kept a row of small dark-glass vials in the wide cabinet of his Sahlen house.
- **Nature-attuned.** Not capable of speech with living things, by his account, but capable of feeling — a non-verbal resonance with the natural world that he treated as ordinary rather than mystical.
- **Arrogant in combat.** A flaw recognized by his earliest companions. His tendency to over-commit left him outnumbered more than once; in his first known engagement at the South Gate of Sahlen, it was [[Six]]'s necromancy that pulled him out of an encirclement of his own making.

## Known Engagements

- **The South Gate of [[Sahlen]].** First documented meeting with [[Edward Z Zwarf]] and [[Six]] during a raid on the anniversary of his adopted family's death. See [[Chapter 1 - The Anniversary Raid]].

## Toward the Title

The title *The Unwilling Heir* would come later, when the political weight of the La'Monte name fell upon him in circumstances he had never sought. The Ezericht of Sahlen — drinking alone on an anniversary, hunting killers who could not be found, riding into raids because the raids would not stop — was the man before the inheritance.
