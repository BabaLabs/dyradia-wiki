# 📜 **Historical Eras of Dyradia**

_Standard Scholarly Chronology_

Modern historians divide recorded Dyradian history into several broadly accepted eras. While exact dates vary by source, the following structure is widely used across institutions.

---

## The Fracture Reckoning

Most modern scholars date events using **Fracture Reckoning**:

- **BF** = *Before the Fracture* (counting backward from year 0)
- **AF** = *After the Fracture* (counting forward)
- **0 AF** = the year the Interstice lattice "broke publicly" — nodes failing, panic, and the first open escalations that could no longer be contained

**Campaign Present: 327 AF.**

See [[The Dyradian Calendar]] for the 12-month civic calendar that pairs with this year-numbering.

---

## **I. The Primordial Age**

_(Pre-History)_

*Range: pre-history → ~2100 BF. Ends with The Veiling — the Dyra withdraw from direct stabilization.*

> _“Before kingdoms, before law — when the world was still being negotiated.”_

### **Overview**

The Primordial Age encompasses the time when the Dyra maintained direct stabilizing influence over elemental and magical forces. Mortal civilizations existed in scattered forms, largely nomadic or proto-settled, and lacked formalized magic or governance.

### **Common Understanding**

- The Dyra shaped the world
    
- Mortals survived within it
    
- Magic existed, but was incoherent
    

### **Scholarly Notes**

- No reliable mortal records survive
    
- Elven oral tradition is the primary source
    
- Dwarven geological records confirm unusual stability
    

This era ends not with catastrophe, but with **withdrawal**.

---

## **II. The Age of Convergence**

_(Early Civilization)_

*Range: ~2100 BF → 540 BF (~1,560 years). Ends with the early standardization of magic theory and the first truly pan-Dyradian scholastic networks.*

> _“When the world learned to stand upright.”_

### **Overview**

This era marks the rise of permanent settlements, early kingdoms, and proto-religious structures. Mortals began forming theories about the world, often mythologizing the Dyra.

### **Key Developments**

- Early Mehrendian dynasties
    
- Feythran ritual codification
    
- Dwarven deep-hold expansion
    
- Initial magical experimentation
    

### **Cultural Memory**

- Seen as foundational, not sophisticated
    
- Often romanticized
    

---

## **III. The Bohrovan Period** / **The Academic Awakening**

_(Late Pre-Fracture)_

*Range: 540 BF → 210 BF (~330 years). Ends with [[Bohrova]]'s disappearance and the loss of a unifying figure.*

> _“The world learned how it worked — and mistook that for control.”_

### **Overview**

Defined by the life and influence of **Bohrova**, this era saw the emergence of structured magical theory, interstitial travel, and the **Academic Awakening**.

### **Defining Features**

- Creation of the Interstice
    
- Pan-Dyradian scholarly exchange
    
- Rapid technological and cultural advancement
    
- Emergence of the Awoken
    

### **Scholarly Consensus**

This was not a golden age — it was an **acceleration**.

---

## **IV. The Ascendancy of the Church**

_(Late Bohrovan → Early Fracture)_

*Range: 210 BF → 0 AF (~210 years). Ends with the Fracture becoming impossible to contain.*

> _“When fear found structure.”_

### **Overview**

As magic spread beyond institutional control, the Church of Prim rose to prominence, offering moral certainty in exchange for authority.

### **Key Developments**

- Doctrinal consolidation
    
- Regulation of magic
    
- Suppression of non-sanctioned study
    
- Increasing political influence in Mehrendt
    

### **Historical Debate**

Whether the Church arose _in response_ to chaos or _to create it_ remains contested.

---

## **V. The Fracture**

_(Era of Collapse)_

*Range: 0 AF → 41 AF (~41 years). Ends with The Severing — the Interstice/Dyra connection damaged badly enough that the world "settles," not because it's fixed, but because it can't sustain that level of strain anymore.*

> _“Everything broke — just not all at once.”_

### **Overview**

A multi-decade period of escalating conflict, culminating in:

- The fall of the Church
    
- The disappearance of the Awoken
    
- The destabilization of magic
    
- Massive loss of life
    

### **Defining Moments**

- Exposure of the illegitimate heir
    
- Blood-binding legitimacy rituals
    
- Capital district devastation
    
- Death of all high Church leadership except the Cardinal
    

### **Era End**

Marked not by victory, but by exhaustion.

---

## **VI. The Age of Stabilization**

_(Early Post-Fracture)_

*Range: 41 AF → 287 AF (~246 years). Ends with visible, undeniable systemic waning.*

> _“Survival masquerading as peace.”_

### **Overview**

In the aftermath of the Fracture, nations focused on containment rather than progress.

### **Characteristics**

- Simplification of magical practice
    
- Abandonment of Awakening methodologies
    
- Heavy regulation of Interstice access
    
- Cultural taboo around unmediated magic
    

This era shaped modern attitudes more than any other.

---

## **VII. The Waning Era**

_(Campaign Present)_

*Range: 287 AF → 327 AF (~40 years so far, continuing).*

> _“The world still works. It just works less well each year.”_

### **Overview**

The current age is defined by:

- Gradual magical diminishment
    
- Growing instability
    
- Resurfacing anomalies
    
- Quiet scholarly panic
    

Most people believe the world is _aging_.  
A few suspect it is **unraveling**.

# 📜 **Era Spotlight: The Primordial Age**

_(Detailed entry, parallel to the Stabilization spotlight above)_

_Also known as: The Age of Making, The Vein-Quiet, The First Stillness_

### **Overview**

**Type:** Mythic / Prehistoric Era
**Era:** Pre-History (Pre-Convergence)
**Primary Influence:** The Dyra (direct stabilizing presence)
**Scope:** Pan-Dyradian
**Status:** Ended (Legacy Foundational)

The **Primordial Age** refers to the vast stretch of time before reliable mortal record-keeping, when the Dyra's influence is believed to have shaped and stabilized the world directly. Mortal peoples existed — sometimes nomadic, sometimes proto-settled — but civilization, law, and formalized magic had not yet cohered into lasting institutions.

Historians treat this era cautiously: it is both the most referenced age in myth and the least provable by mortal documentation. What can be said with confidence is that the world behaved differently — more "held together," less variable — until an event scholars broadly call **the Veiling**, when the Dyra withdrew from direct influence.

### **Defining Characteristics**

The Primordial Age is commonly characterized by:

- Elemental forces behaving with unusual consistency ("stability beyond climate")
- Widespread reverence of the Dyra in proto-religious forms
- Scattered mortal cultures with limited long-range governance
- Incoherent, ambient "wild magic" events (rarely repeatable)
- Early sacred sites: stone rings, deep markers, river oaths, sky pillars
- A near-total absence of standardized calendars, measurement, or written law

This era is remembered not as a golden age of comfort, but as an age of **order without understanding** — a world that "worked" because something immense was quietly holding it in shape.

---

### **Tier 0 — Common Knowledge**

_(No roll required)_

> _"That was when the Dyra still walked close. The world listened better back then."_

Most people believe:

- The Dyra were "nearer" and the world was calmer
- Magic existed, but it was strange and unpredictable
- Monsters and storms were bigger "because the world was young"
- The era ended when the Dyra left, and mortals had to fend for themselves

This version is mostly folklore: campfire stories, shrine sayings, and "how things used to be."

---

### **Tier 1 — Familiar History**

_(Low DC History, Educated Background, Fey'thran cultural exposure)_

> _"The Primordial Age predates reliable written record. Most modern accounts are reconstructed from elven oral tradition and dwarven geological archives."_

At this level, it's known that:

- **No unified mortal chronology** survives from this era
- Fey'thran traditions preserve "songlines" that describe major natural shifts
- Gohlstout keeps long geological records that support the idea of abnormal stability
- The transition into the Age of Convergence involved a slow rise in settlement, governance, and repeatable craft

People who study history learn to treat the Primordial Age less like "events" and more like **conditions** — a baseline state before civilization accelerated.

---

### **Tier 2 — Archival / Scholarly Record**

_(Moderate–High DC History, Academic Access, Dwarven Archives)_

> _"Most scholarly models describe the Primordial Age as a period of reduced variance in elemental and planar behavior, likely due to direct stabilizing influence from the Dyra."_

Archival reconstructions suggest:

- The world's "rules" were more consistent: fewer anomalies, fewer abrupt shifts
- Early mortal "magic" was often **environmental resonance**, not trained casting
- Sites later treated as sacred were likely **natural stabilizers** (places where reality held firm)
- The earliest proto-cities appear late in this era, but fail repeatedly until the Veiling fully settles

Key insight:

> The Primordial Age wasn't "better." It was **more constrained** — a world with guardrails mortals didn't build.

This is why many scholars mark the Veiling as the true beginning of mortal autonomy.

---

### **Tier 3 — Obscure / Contested Knowledge**

_(High DC, Fey'thran oral record, forbidden dwarven strata notes, suppressed church commentary)_

> _"Some accounts imply the Dyra did not simply withdraw. They renegotiated the world's terms — reducing direct influence to prevent dependency, or to contain a destabilizing pressure mortals were not meant to perceive."_

Deep records and fringe schools propose:

- The Veiling may have been a **deliberate severing of direct contact**, not a disappearance
- Certain "Primordial Names" exist — conceptual identifiers tied to elemental law — rarely spoken aloud
- Some pre-Convergence relics show workmanship that doesn't align with known mortal craft traditions
- A few scholars believe the Veiling created the first true "thin places," where boundaries became _possible_ to cross (not necessarily common, but possible)

Most troubling to conservative institutions:

- If the Dyra withdrew to prevent dependency, modern attempts to "restore" direct contact may be philosophically — and physically — unsafe
- If the Dyra withdrew to contain pressure, then the world's current unraveling could represent that containment failing over time

---

### **Institutions and Structures of the Primordial Age**

While formal institutions are not reliably documented, most cultures claim origin-sites tied to this era:

- **Fey'thran Stone Circles:** early rites of season and boundary
- **Gohlstout Deep Markers:** carved strata-lines used to "measure the mountain"
- **Avalonian River Oath Stones:** covenant sites tied to passage and belonging
- **Mehrendian Sky Pillars:** proto-astral markers later absorbed into civic tradition

Modern scholars treat these as the first "semi-universal" landmarks: the earliest attempts to make the world **legible** to mortals.

---

### **Relationship to the Church of Prim**

The Church's surviving doctrines often romanticize the Primordial Age as "unfallen order," citing it as proof that the world is safest when power remains distant from mortal hands.

Church-aligned chroniclers typically argue:

- The world was stable until mortals reached beyond their station
- Later magical ages represent "hubris replacing harmony"
- The Veiling was a warning: mortals must not seek to pull the Dyra close again

Other traditions view that interpretation as selective, noting that the Primordial Age was harsh, wild, and often indifferent to mortal survival.

---

### **End of the Era**

The Primordial Age ends with **the Veiling** — the broadly accepted period in which the Dyra's direct stabilizing presence withdrew.

Scholars disagree on what that means, but most agree on its consequences:

- Elemental variance increased (storms, seasons, regional unpredictability)
- Mortal cultures began stabilizing themselves through law, architecture, and ritual
- Repeatable craft and governance became viable at larger scales
- "Magic" gradually shifted from ambient phenomena to disciplined practice and study

The Veiling is therefore remembered as both loss and liberation.

---

### **Modern Legacy**

Today, the Primordial Age survives as:

- Sacred sites used for oaths, rites, and memorial observances
- Folk beliefs that "the world remembers old rules" in certain places
- Dwarven practices treating geology as history
- Fey'thran traditions treating memory as living record

It is also used rhetorically:

- By conservatives as an argument for constraint
- By scholars as the beginning of "baseline conditions"
- By radicals as evidence that the world **can** be different than it is now

---

### **Common Rumors**

_(Unverified)_

- Some storms "choose" targets as if guided
- Certain standing stones feel warmer on nights when magic behaves strangely
- Old oaths spoken in the wrong place still bind too well
- There are routes that only exist during Stillnight that lead somewhere "before the world learned to lie"
