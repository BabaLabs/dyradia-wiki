# 📜 **The Interstice**

_Also known as: The Veiled Way, The Between, The Dyric Span_

---

## **Overview**

**Type:** Liminal Transit Phenomenon  
**Originator:** Bohrova  
**Status:** Partially Functional (Post-Fracture)  
**Access:** Regulated, Restricted, or Lost (Varies by Region)

The **Interstice** is a stabilized liminal space existing between the material world of Dyradia and the planar domain associated with the Dyra. It was engineered as a means of long-distance travel, allowing individuals and caravans to traverse vast distances in a fraction of the time required by conventional routes.

Contrary to popular belief, travelers do not enter the Dyra plane itself. Instead, they pass through a **contained in-between space**, where spatial relationships differ from those of the material world.

---

## **Function and Structure**

### **Transit Principles**

- Distance within the Interstice does not correspond linearly to distance in Dyradia
    
- Travel is directional and guided via anchored nodes
    
- Time passage is generally consistent, though deviations have been recorded
    
- Entry and exit points are fixed unless destabilized
    

The Interstice is accessed through **anchored thresholds**, commonly referred to as _gates_, _spans_, or _nodes_. These structures were placed strategically across Dyradia during the height of Bohrova’s influence.

---

## **Scale and Unmapped Fringes**

Dyradia is a massive landmass. There are still places beyond the borders of all the kingdoms that were never added to the Interstitial network. Travel through the Interstice could reduce months of overland travel to a few days through a channel of magic between the two worlds, but the network only ever reached as far as the established powers chose to extend it.

[[Bohrova]] was the only one to truly understand the mechanism by which it functioned.

## **Tiered Knowledge Accounts**

---

### **Tier 0 — Common Knowledge**

_(No roll required)_

> _“The Interstice is an old magic road. You step in one place and come out somewhere far away. It’s slower than it used to be, but it still works — mostly.”_

Most people believe:

- The Interstice is just a magical shortcut
    
- It was stronger “back in the old days”
    
- Occasional mishaps are expected
    
- Only fools or smugglers use unstable nodes
    

The Interstice is treated as useful, but unremarkable.

---

### **Tier 1 — Familiar History**

_(Low DC History, Merchant, Soldier, or Sage Background)_

> _“The Interstice was created by Bohrova to connect distant regions of Dyradia without crossing the Dyra plane. It functioned as a controlled space between worlds, allowing safe and predictable transit.”_

At this level, it is known that:

- The Interstice is **not** another plane
    
- It exists _between_ two realities
    
- Nodes were deliberately placed
    
- Some regions were never connected
    

Educated individuals understand that the system was designed, not discovered.

---

### **Tier 2 — Archival / Scholarly Record**

_(Moderate–High DC History, Access to Libraries, Feythran or Gohlstout Sources)_

> _“Primary records indicate the Interstice is a bounded liminal construct, stabilized through harmonic resonance with elemental energies associated with the Dyra.”_

Scholars agree on several points:

- The Interstice has **internal topology** distinct from Dyradia
    
- Its geometry allows spatial compression
    
- Anchors require constant, though minimal, stabilization
    
- Bohrova intentionally limited expansion beyond known lands
    

Additional findings:

- Some nodes were designed as **junctions**, not destinations
    
- A small number of paths were never completed
    
- Post-Fracture failures do not align with simple decay models
    

This is where concern begins.

---

### **Tier 3 — Obscure / Suppressed Knowledge**

_(High DC, Restricted Texts, Elven Oral Tradition, Dwarven Vault Records)_

> _“Elven accounts describe the Interstice not as a passage, but as a wound held open — a space that resists collapse only through constant correction.”_

Deep records suggest:

- The Interstice exerts pressure on adjacent planes
    
- Its stabilization required _active oversight_
    
- Bohrova alone could traverse it without fixed anchors
    
- Some paths descend _deeper_ rather than crossing outward
    

Disturbing implications include:

- Certain exits do not correspond to known Dyradian geography
    
- Some travelers have returned with altered perceptions of distance or continuity
    
- A minority of records imply the Interstice was never fully sealed from the Dyra plane
    

Most controversially:

- The Fracture may not have damaged the Interstice
    
- It may have **removed the final constraints**
    

---

## **Post-Fracture Behavior**

Since the Fracture:

- Several nodes collapsed entirely
    
- Others now cover reduced distances
    
- Some reconnect to unfamiliar regions
    
- A handful produce no exit at all
    

Modern scholars classify nodes as:

- **Stable** – predictable, regulated
    
- **Degraded** – functional with anomalies
    
- **Severed** – inert or destroyed
    
- **Unbound** – destination unknown
    

Use of Unbound nodes is universally discouraged.

---

## **The Persistent and the Strange**

The network still works after the Fracture, for the most part. But:

- Some passages are severed and don't work at all
- Some are far weaker and don't nearly cover the same distance they once did
- Some lead to places unknown in Dyradia once you come back

It is rumored that some passages out at the fringes of the land will take you someplace *beyond* — possibly the plane of the [[The Dyra|Dyra]] itself, though no one can verify, and no one who claims to have made the journey can be reliably trusted.

See also: [[Beyond the Interstice]] (the geographic mystery region; possibly related to these rumored "beyond" destinations).

## **Cultural Perception**

### **Mehrendt**

- Uses stable nodes for trade and diplomacy
    
- Heavily regulates access
    
- Downplays anomalies
    

### **Feythra**

- Views the Interstice as inherently dangerous
    
- Maintains ancient wards near forest-adjacent nodes
    
- Tracks disturbances carefully
    

### **Gohlstout**

- Records Interstitial fluctuations meticulously
    
- Notes resonance interference deep underground
    
- Restricts experimentation
    

### **Avalon**

- Uses degraded and unbound nodes strategically
    
- Considers the risk acceptable
    
- Believes the Interstice should not be controlled
    

---

## **Current Scholarly Disputes**

Unresolved questions include:

- Whether the Interstice is self-sustaining
    
- If it is expanding or contracting
    
- Whether it is reacting to external interference
    
- If Bohrova’s absence is the cause — or the safeguard
    

There is growing concern that the Interstice is no longer merely a transit system, but an **active participant** in the world’s instability.
